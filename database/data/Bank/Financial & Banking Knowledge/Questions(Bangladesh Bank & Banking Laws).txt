Part 1 — Questions 1–50
Historical foundations and Bangladesh Bank legislation
1. Which legal instrument established Bangladesh Bank?
A. Bank Company Act, 1991
B. Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972
C. Financial Institutions Act, 1993
D. Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881
Correct Answer: B. Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972
Explanation: The Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972 (President's Order No. 127 of 1972) established the legal foundation of Bangladesh's central bank. Article 3 provides for the establishment and incorporation of Bangladesh Bank.
2. What is the number of the Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972?
A. President's Order No. 26 of 1972
B. President's Order No. 127 of 1972
C. Act No. 14 of 1991
D. President's Order No. 27 of 1973
Correct Answer: B. President's Order No. 127 of 1972
Explanation: Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972 is President's Order No. 127 of 1972. It is the foundational instrument governing the central bank, subject to subsequent amendments and other applicable laws.
3. Which article of the Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972, provides for the establishment and incorporation of Bangladesh Bank?
A. Article 3
B. Article 10
C. Article 23
D. Article 36
Correct Answer: A. Article 3
Explanation: Article 3 provides for the establishment of Bangladesh Bank as a body corporate with perpetual succession and a common seal. Article references should be studied alongside subsequent amendments.
4. Which institution is primarily responsible for conducting monetary policy in Bangladesh?
A. Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission
B. National Board of Revenue
C. Bangladesh Bank
D. Ministry of Commerce
Correct Answer: C. Bangladesh Bank
Explanation: Bangladesh Bank is the country's central bank and conducts monetary policy as part of its responsibilities for monetary and financial stability. The BSEC regulates the securities market, while the NBR administers major tax and customs functions.
5. Which law primarily governs the licensing and regulation of banking companies in Bangladesh?
A. Bank Company Act, 1991
B. Companies Act, 1994 alone
C. Insurance Act, 2010
D. Securities and Exchange Ordinance, 1969
Correct Answer: A. Bank Company Act, 1991
Explanation: The Bank Company Act, 1991 establishes the principal statutory framework for banking companies, including licensing, management, supervision and other regulatory matters. It operates alongside the Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972 and other applicable legislation.
6. Which Act is the principal legislation governing negotiable instruments such as cheques and promissory notes?
A. Contract Act, 1872
B. Transfer of Property Act, 1882
C. Partnership Act, 1932
D. Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881
Correct Answer: D. Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881
Explanation: The Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 governs instruments such as promissory notes, bills of exchange and cheques. It provides rules concerning their creation, transfer, presentment, payment and related liabilities.
7. Which legislation historically governed non-bank financial institutions in Bangladesh before the Finance Company Act, 2023?
A. Bank Company Act, 1991
B. Financial Institutions Act, 1993
C. Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972 alone
D. Insurance Act, 2010
Correct Answer: B. Financial Institutions Act, 1993
Explanation: The Financial Institutions Act, 1993 was the principal historical statutory framework for financial institutions. The Finance Company Act, 2023 is the later legislation to study for the updated framework. Candidates should distinguish historical questions from current-law questions.
8. Which law is specifically associated with the recovery of loans by financial institutions through specialised courts in Bangladesh?
A. Limitation Act, 1908 alone
B. Evidence Act, 1872
C. Money Loan Court Act, 2003
D. Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 alone
Correct Answer: C. Money Loan Court Act, 2003
Explanation: The Money Loan Court Act, 2003 establishes the specialised legal framework for adjudicating money-loan recovery disputes within its scope. It is particularly important for banking credit recovery and litigation.
9. Which law primarily addresses the prevention of money laundering in Bangladesh?
A. Money Laundering Prevention Act, 2012
B. Bank Company Act, 1991 alone
C. Companies Act, 1994 alone
D. Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881
Correct Answer: A. Money Laundering Prevention Act, 2012
Explanation: The Money Laundering Prevention Act, 2012 provides the statutory framework for preventing and addressing money laundering. Banks must also follow applicable Bangladesh Bank directives and requirements concerning customer due diligence, transaction monitoring and reporting.
10. Which legislation is directly relevant to combating the financing of terrorism in Bangladesh?
A. Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1947 alone
B. Companies Act, 1994

D. Anti-Terrorism Act, 2009
Correct Answer: D. Anti-Terrorism Act, 2009
Explanation: The Anti-Terrorism Act, 2009 provides a legal framework for addressing terrorism-related offences, including relevant terrorist-financing offences. Banks must comply with applicable anti-terrorist-financing obligations and regulatory instructions.

Negotiable instruments and banking evidence
21. The Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 is which Act number?

C. Act No. XXVII of 1993
D. Act No. VIII of 2003
Correct Answer: A. Act No. XXVI of 1881
Explanation: The official text identifies the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 as Act No. XXVI of 1881. Memorising the title, year and Act number helps distinguish it from the later banking and financial-sector statutes.
22. Under the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, which of the following is a negotiable instrument expressly recognised by the Act?
A. Land registration deed
B. Company memorandum
C. Insurance survey report
D. Cheque
Correct Answer: D. Cheque
Explanation: A cheque is a negotiable instrument governed by the Act. Other principal categories include promissory notes and bills of exchange. A land deed or insurance report does not become a negotiable instrument merely because it records a financial interest.
23. Under the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, which instrument contains an unconditional undertaking to pay a certain sum of money?
A. Cheque
B. Promissory note
C. Bank statement
D. Letter of credit
Correct Answer: B. Promissory note
Explanation: A promissory note contains an unconditional undertaking by its maker to pay a certain sum of money to a specified person, that person's order, or the bearer, subject to the Act's requirements. A cheque is instead a form of bill of exchange drawn on a specified banker.
24. Under the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, a cheque is a particular form of which instrument?
A. Promissory note
B. Mortgage deed
C. Bill of exchange
D. Bond of indemnity
Correct Answer: C. Bill of exchange
Explanation: A cheque is a bill of exchange drawn on a specified banker and payable on demand, within the statutory definition. This distinction is important because a cheque involves a drawer, drawee bank and payee or holder.
25. Which section of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 is commonly associated with the statutory definition of a cheque?
A. Section 6
B. Section 20
C. Section 85
D. Section 138
Correct Answer: A. Section 6
Explanation: Section 6 defines a cheque within the Act's framework. Section 138, by contrast, concerns penalties associated with dishonour of certain cheques for insufficiency of funds or other specified grounds, subject to statutory conditions and amendments.
26. Which section of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 is associated with penalties for dishonour of certain cheques?
A. Section 4
B. Section 5
C. Section 13
D. Section 138
Correct Answer: D. Section 138
Explanation: Section 138 is the key provision associated with criminal penalties for qualifying cheque dishonour. Liability is not automatic for every returned cheque; the statutory conditions, procedural requirements, applicable amendments and judicial interpretation must be considered.

D. Limitation Act, 1908
Correct Answer: A. Bank Company Act, 1991

62. Which of the following best describes the purpose of prudential banking regulation?
A. To guarantee every bank a profit
B. To eliminate all commercial lending
C. To replace the bank's internal management
D. To promote the safety, soundness and resilience of banks
Correct Answer: D. To promote the safety, soundness and resilience of banks
Explanation: Prudential regulation addresses risks such as inadequate capital, excessive lending concentration and insufficient liquidity. It aims to strengthen financial stability rather than guarantee profits or eliminate ordinary banking risks.

Correct Answer: B. Capital adequacy, Asset quality, Management, Earnings, Liquidity, Sensitivity to market risk
Explanation: CAMELS is a supervisory assessment framework. It evaluates a bank's capital, asset quality, management, earnings, liquidity and sensitivity to market risk. It is a supervisory methodology, not a standalone Act of Parliament.

D. Mandatory government ownership
Correct Answer: C. Risk-based supervision
Explanation: Risk-based supervision prioritises supervisory attention according to the significance and likelihood of risks faced by individual institutions. Bangladesh Bank lists supervisory guidance and regulations among its official regulatory resources.
