Section 1: Deposits and Deposit Accounts (1–25)
1. Which type of bank deposit generally permits frequent withdrawals and is primarily designed for day-to-day transactions?
A. Fixed deposit
B. Current deposit
C. Recurring deposit
D. Long-term term deposit
Correct Answer: B. Current deposit
Explanation: Current accounts facilitate frequent business transactions, including receipts and payments.
2. A customer places money with a bank for a specified period at an agreed interest rate. Which deposit is this?
A. Savings deposit
B. Current deposit
C. Fixed deposit
D. Demand deposit
Correct Answer: C. Fixed deposit
Explanation: A fixed deposit is placed for an agreed term and earns a return according to the bank's product conditions.
3. Which deposit account is generally intended to encourage personal savings while allowing withdrawals subject to the account's terms?
A. Savings deposit
B. Current deposit
C. Cash credit account
D. Margin deposit
Correct Answer: A. Savings deposit
Explanation: Savings accounts encourage customers to save while permitting withdrawals under the applicable account conditions.
4. What is the principal distinction between a demand deposit and a time deposit?
A. Demand deposits are available only to companies.
B. Time deposits cannot earn interest.
C. Demand deposits must always be maintained in foreign currency.
D. Demand deposits are payable on demand, while time deposits are subject to an agreed maturity or withdrawal conditions.
Correct Answer: D. Demand deposits are payable on demand, while time deposits are subject to maturity or withdrawal conditions.
Explanation: Demand deposits provide access on demand, subject to applicable conditions, whereas time deposits have agreed maturity and early-withdrawal terms.
5. A bank accepts a customer's money and records an obligation to repay it. From the bank's accounting perspective, the deposit is generally a:
A. Fixed asset
B. Liability
C. Contingent asset
D. Shareholder's equity
Correct Answer: B. Liability
Explanation: A deposit is normally a liability because the bank owes the depositor the amount held in the account.
6. Which of the following is a typical characteristic of a current account?
A. It is designed exclusively for long-term savings.
B. It must always have a fixed maturity date.
C. It facilitates frequent business transactions.
D. It cannot receive electronic transfers.
Correct Answer: C. It facilitates frequent business transactions.
Explanation: Current accounts are generally suitable for businesses requiring frequent deposits, withdrawals and transfers.
7. A depositor withdraws a fixed deposit before maturity. Which consequence is most likely, depending on the bank's terms?
A. Premature encashment terms may reduce the interest payable.
B. The bank must always pay the full contracted interest.
C. The deposit automatically becomes share capital.
D. The depositor automatically loses the principal.
Correct Answer: A. Premature encashment terms may reduce the interest payable.
Explanation: Early withdrawal may result in a lower interest rate or other contractual consequences; it does not automatically forfeit the principal.
8. What does the term deposit mobilization mean?
A. Recovering overdue loans from borrowers
B. Selling government securities to customers
C. Transferring deposits between branches
D. Attracting and collecting deposits from customers
Correct Answer: D. Attracting and collecting deposits from customers
Explanation: Deposit mobilization involves collecting funds from depositors to support a bank's funding needs and operations.
9. Which of the following is a liability-side source of funds for a commercial bank?
A. Loans granted to customers
B. Customer deposits
C. Buildings owned by the bank
D. Investments in securities
Correct Answer: B. Customer deposits
Explanation: Customer deposits are liabilities because the bank is obligated to repay them; loans and investments are generally assets.
10. A bank pays interest on eligible deposits and earns interest on loans. The difference between these interest amounts, relative to relevant earning assets, is associated with its:
A. Cash reserve ratio
B. Capital adequacy ratio
C. Net interest margin
D. Loan-to-value ratio
Correct Answer: C. Net interest margin
Explanation: Net interest margin relates net interest income to average interest-earning assets and measures an important aspect of a bank's earnings performance.
11. Which account is most suitable for a business that needs to make frequent payments to suppliers and receive customer payments?
A. Current account
B. Long-term fixed deposit
C. Recurring deposit
D. Security deposit account only
Correct Answer: A. Current account
Explanation: Current accounts are designed to facilitate frequent business receipts and payments.
12. What is the primary purpose of Know Your Customer (KYC) procedures when opening a deposit account?
A. Guaranteeing the customer's investment return
B. Eliminating all banking risks
C. Ensuring that every customer receives a loan
D. Verifying customer identity and understanding relevant customer risk
Correct Answer: D. Verifying customer identity and understanding relevant customer risk
Explanation: KYC helps banks identify customers, assess risk and meet applicable anti-money-laundering and customer-due-diligence requirements.
13. Which of the following best describes a dormant or inactive account?
A. An account that has a negative balance in every case
B. An account classified according to the bank's inactivity criteria and applicable rules
C. An account that is legally owned by the bank
D. An account that cannot receive any deposit under any circumstances
Correct Answer: B. An account classified according to the bank's inactivity criteria and applicable rules
Explanation: Dormancy depends on the applicable inactivity criteria and rules; it does not mean the bank owns the account balance.
14. Why may a bank require a minimum balance in a deposit account?
A. To convert deposits into equity
B. To remove the need for customer identification
C. To comply with the account's product terms and applicable policies
D. To guarantee a loan to the depositor
Correct Answer: C. To comply with the account's product terms and applicable policies
Explanation: A minimum balance may be a condition of an account product, subject to applicable regulations and disclosed terms.
15. Which statement about a bank's customer deposit is correct?
A. It is generally a liability of the bank and a claim of the depositor.
B. It is always a fixed asset of the bank.
C. It is always an investment in the bank's shares.
D. It is never repayable.
Correct Answer: A. It is generally a liability of the bank and a claim of the depositor.
Explanation: The bank owes the depositor the account balance under the deposit agreement and applicable law.
16. A depositor instructs a bank to transfer money automatically into a designated savings account at regular intervals. This arrangement is best described as:
A. A bank guarantee
B. A bill of exchange
C. A letter of credit
D. A standing instruction or scheduled transfer
Correct Answer: D. A standing instruction or scheduled transfer
Explanation: A standing instruction authorizes recurring transactions according to specified conditions.
17. Which deposit feature is most closely associated with liquidity for the customer?
A. The highest possible maturity period
B. Ease and speed of accessing funds
C. The largest possible penalty for early withdrawal
D. The absence of withdrawal facilities
Correct Answer: B. Ease and speed of accessing funds
Explanation: Liquidity refers to how readily funds can be accessed or used for payments without substantial loss.
18. A bank offers a deposit with an agreed maturity date. Which risk may arise for the customer if market interest rates rise after the deposit is placed?
A. The deposit automatically becomes a non-performing loan.
B. The customer becomes the bank's creditor only after maturity.
C. The customer may miss out on higher rates available on new deposits.
D. The bank's deposit liability becomes shareholder equity.
Correct Answer: C. The customer may miss out on higher rates available on new deposits.
Explanation: A depositor locked into a lower rate may lose the opportunity to earn a higher rate on a new deposit.
19. Which statement best distinguishes a deposit from a loan granted by a bank?
A. A deposit is normally a liability of the bank, whereas a loan is normally an asset.
B. Both are always liabilities of the bank.
C. Both are always assets of the bank.
D. A deposit is always off-balance-sheet, whereas a loan is always off-balance-sheet.
Correct Answer: A. A deposit is normally a liability of the bank, whereas a loan is normally an asset.
Explanation: The bank owes deposits to customers, while borrowers owe outstanding loan amounts to the bank.
20. A bank pays interest on a savings account. The applicable rate and calculation method are normally determined by:
A. The depositor alone after opening the account
B. The stock exchange
C. The borrower of the bank's loans
D. The bank's disclosed product terms and applicable regulatory requirements
Correct Answer: D. The bank's disclosed product terms and applicable regulatory requirements
Explanation: Deposit interest depends on the bank's product terms and any applicable regulatory requirements.
21. Which practice is most appropriate when a person opens a deposit account on behalf of a company?
A. Accepting any signature without checking authority
B. Verifying the entity, authorized signatories and required documentation
C. Treating the company as an individual without any documentation
D. Waiving all AML-related checks
Correct Answer: B. Verifying the entity, authorized signatories and required documentation
Explanation: Corporate account opening requires appropriate entity verification, documentation and confirmation of authorized account operators.
22. What is a premature withdrawal of a term deposit?
A. Adding interest at the maturity date
B. Transferring a deposit to another bank at maturity
C. Encashing the deposit before its agreed maturity date
D. Converting a loan into a deposit
Correct Answer: C. Encashing the deposit before its agreed maturity date
Explanation: Premature withdrawal occurs when a depositor encashes a term deposit before its contractual maturity.
23. Which of the following is a principal purpose of deposit insurance or deposit protection arrangements?
A. Protecting eligible depositors up to the applicable legal limit and conditions
B. Guaranteeing that every bank investment will earn a profit
C. Insuring every borrower against default
D. Preventing all bank failures
Correct Answer: A. Protecting eligible depositors up to the applicable legal limit and conditions
Explanation: Deposit protection safeguards eligible deposits within the limits and conditions established by the applicable scheme.
24. If a bank's deposit funding grows rapidly while its lending standards weaken, which concern becomes especially relevant?
A. The bank has eliminated credit risk.
B. All deposits automatically become capital.
C. Its loans no longer require monitoring.
D. The bank may take excessive credit or liquidity risks.
Correct Answer: D. The bank may take excessive credit or liquidity risks.
Explanation: Weak underwriting can increase defaults, while unsuitable funding and lending structures can expose the bank to liquidity pressure.
25. A bank's customer deposits rise by Tk 20 million, with all other entries ignored. What is the immediate basic accounting effect of accepting the funds in cash?
A. Assets decrease and liabilities increase.
B. Assets and liabilities both increase by Tk 20 million.
C. Assets increase and liabilities decrease.
D. Equity increases by Tk 20 million without any liability.
Correct Answer: B. Assets and liabilities both increase by Tk 20 million.
Explanation: Cash, an asset, increases by Tk 20 million, and the bank's deposit liability increases by the same amount.
Section 2: Cheques and Negotiable Instruments (26–50)
26. A cheque is primarily an instrument containing:
A. An unconditional order to a banker to pay a specified sum
B. A promise by the banker to lend money
C. A contract to insure goods in transit
D. An instruction to issue company shares
Correct Answer: A. An unconditional order to a banker to pay a specified sum
Explanation: A cheque directs a specified banker to pay money according to the instrument and applicable law.
27. Under the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, a cheque is a type of:
A. Promissory note
B. Letter of credit
C. Bill of exchange drawn on a specified banker and payable on demand
D. Bank guarantee
Correct Answer: C. Bill of exchange drawn on a specified banker and payable on demand
Explanation: The Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 defines a cheque as a bill of exchange drawn on a specified banker and not expressed to be payable otherwise than on demand. Apply the relevant Bangladesh law and amendments.
28. Which instrument contains an unconditional undertaking in writing, signed by the maker, to pay a certain sum of money?
A. Cheque
B. Promissory note
C. Letter of credit
D. Payment voucher
Correct Answer: B. Promissory note
Explanation: A promissory note contains the maker's written, signed and unconditional undertaking to pay a certain sum, subject to statutory requirements.
29. In a bill of exchange, the person who makes the order to pay is the:
A. Drawee
B. Payee
C. Endorsee
D. Drawer
Correct Answer: D. Drawer
Explanation: The drawer makes the order to pay, while the drawee is the person or institution directed to pay.
30. In a cheque transaction, the bank on which the cheque is drawn is called the:
A. Drawee bank
B. Endorser
C. Payee
D. Beneficiary under a guarantee
Correct Answer: A. Drawee bank
Explanation: The drawee bank is the bank directed to make payment from the drawer's account.
31. What is the primary effect of crossing a cheque with two parallel transverse lines?
A. It automatically makes the cheque payable in cash over the counter.
B. It cancels the cheque.
C. It directs payment through a banker, subject to the crossing and applicable law.
D. It converts the cheque into a promissory note.
Correct Answer: C. It directs payment through a banker, subject to the crossing and applicable law.
Explanation: General crossing restricts the manner of payment under the applicable rules; it does not invalidate the cheque.
32. What does a cheque crossed “A/C Payee” generally indicate in banking practice?
A. It must be paid to any bearer.
B. It should be collected and credited to the account of the named payee, subject to applicable rules.
C. It can only be used to withdraw foreign currency.
D. It is automatically invalid.
Correct Answer: B. It should be collected and credited to the account of the named payee, subject to applicable rules.
Explanation: Account-payee crossing directs collection toward the named payee's account and reduces the risk of misdirected proceeds.
33. What is endorsement of a negotiable instrument?
A. Writing a signature on the instrument, usually to negotiate or transfer it as permitted by law
B. Destroying the original instrument
C. Depositing cash in an account
D. Cancelling the underlying debt in every case
Correct Answer: A. Writing a signature on the instrument, usually to negotiate or transfer it as permitted by law
Explanation: Endorsement generally involves signing an instrument for its legally permitted negotiation or transfer.
34. A cheque payable to bearer is generally negotiated by:
A. Registration with the stock exchange
B. Issuing a bank guarantee
C. Obtaining approval from the central bank in every case
D. Delivery, subject to applicable law and the instrument's circumstances
Correct Answer: D. Delivery, subject to applicable law and the instrument's circumstances
Explanation: Bearer instruments are generally negotiated by delivery, subject to applicable law.
35. Which is the most appropriate first response when a customer reports a cheque book as lost?
A. Ignore the report until the cheque book expires.
B. Immediately grant a loan against the lost cheque book.
C. Record the report, verify the customer and take appropriate stop-payment or risk-control action.
D. Treat every cheque previously issued by the customer as paid.
Correct Answer: C. Record the report, verify the customer and take appropriate stop-payment or risk-control action.
Explanation: The bank should authenticate the report, document it and apply suitable controls to reduce unauthorized payment risk.
36. What does “dishonour of a cheque” generally mean?
A. The cheque has been crossed.
B. The bank refuses payment of the cheque.
C. The cheque has been deposited successfully.
D. The cheque has been endorsed.
Correct Answer: B. The bank refuses payment of the cheque.
Explanation: Dishonour occurs when payment is refused, subject to the applicable circumstances and legal rules.
37. A cheque is presented for Tk 50,000, but the drawer's available balance is insufficient and no overdraft arrangement covers the shortfall. Which outcome is most likely?
A. The cheque must be paid regardless of balance.
B. The cheque becomes a bank draft automatically.
C. The cheque is converted into a term deposit.
D. The cheque may be returned unpaid for insufficient funds.
Correct Answer: D. The cheque may be returned unpaid for insufficient funds.
Explanation: If sufficient funds or authorized overdraft capacity are unavailable, the bank may return the cheque unpaid.
38. What is the primary function of a collecting banker?
A. To collect payment of instruments deposited by customers and credit the proceeds when realized
B. To guarantee every cheque drawn by any customer
C. To set the central bank's policy rate
D. To issue all government securities
Correct Answer: A. To collect payment of instruments deposited by customers and credit the proceeds when realized
Explanation: A collecting banker processes instruments for collection and credits the proceeds when payment is realized.
39. What is a stop-payment instruction?
A. An instruction to increase the customer's deposit interest rate
B. An instruction to terminate every banking service permanently
C. A request to the bank not to pay a specified payment instrument, subject to verification and applicable rules
D. A request to convert a cheque into cash automatically
Correct Answer: C. A request to the bank not to pay a specified payment instrument, subject to verification and applicable rules
Explanation: A stop-payment request requires verification and timely action according to the instrument's status and applicable procedures.
40. Which statement about a post-dated cheque is correct?
A. It is always payable immediately regardless of its date.
B. It bears a future date and is generally not payable before that date.
C. It is a cheque drawn only on a foreign bank.
D. It cannot be signed by the drawer.
Correct Answer: B. It bears a future date and is generally not payable before that date.
Explanation: A post-dated cheque bears a date later than the date on which it is issued.
41. What is a stale cheque?
A. A cheque with a future date
B. A cheque that has been crossed
C. A cheque that has been endorsed once
D. A cheque presented after its applicable validity period
Correct Answer: D. A cheque presented after its applicable validity period
Explanation: A stale cheque is presented after the period during which it remains valid for payment under the applicable rules.
42. Which instrument is a written order by one person directing another to pay a certain sum to a specified person or bearer, as applicable?
A. Bill of exchange
B. Fixed deposit receipt
C. Share certificate
D. Bank statement
Correct Answer: A. Bill of exchange
Explanation: A bill of exchange is a written order to pay a certain sum, subject to the legal requirements governing the instrument.
43. What is the key difference between a promissory note and a bill of exchange?
A. A promissory note is always issued by a bank.
B. A bill of exchange can never involve three parties.
C. A promissory note contains a promise to pay, while a bill of exchange contains an order to pay.
D. A bill of exchange is never negotiable.
Correct Answer: C. A promissory note contains a promise to pay, while a bill of exchange contains an order to pay.
Explanation: The maker promises payment in a promissory note; the drawer orders payment in a bill of exchange.
44. Who is the payee of a cheque?
A. The bank maintaining the drawer's account
B. The person named to receive the payment
C. The person who clears the cheque at the central bank
D. The bank's external auditor
Correct Answer: B. The person named to receive the payment
Explanation: The payee is the person designated to receive the cheque proceeds.
45. Which of the following is the safest approach to verifying a suspicious cheque?
A. Pay it immediately because it has a signature.
B. Rely solely on the customer's verbal assurance.
C. Ignore alterations if the amount is small.
D. Check authenticity, alterations, signatures and applicable verification procedures before payment.
Correct Answer: D. Check authenticity, alterations, signatures and applicable verification procedures before payment.
Explanation: Banks must verify the instrument and comply with relevant payment controls rather than rely on a signature alone.
46. What does “account payee” crossing primarily seek to reduce?
A. The risk that the cheque proceeds will be collected for someone other than the intended payee
B. The bank's need to maintain accounts
C. The need to verify signatures
D. The possibility of electronic banking
Correct Answer: A. The risk that the cheque proceeds will be collected for someone other than the intended payee
Explanation: Account-payee crossing helps direct the proceeds to the named payee's account.
47. Under ordinary negotiable-instrument terminology, the person to whom an instrument is transferred by endorsement is the:
A. Drawer
B. Drawee
C. Endorsee
D. Maker of a separate promissory note
Correct Answer: C. Endorsee
Explanation: The endorsee receives the instrument through endorsement, subject to applicable law.
48. Which factor is most important before paying a cheque drawn on a customer's account?
A. Whether the customer has borrowed from another bank
B. Validity of the instrument, mandate, available funds and relevant payment controls
C. Whether the payee owns shares in the bank
D. Whether the cheque was printed in another country
Correct Answer: B. Validity of the instrument, mandate, available funds and relevant payment controls
Explanation: The paying bank must verify the instrument, authority, available funds and applicable payment requirements.
49. A cheque is altered materially after signing, without proper authentication. What should the paying bank generally do?
A. Pay it because all alterations are harmless.
B. Convert it into a demand draft.
C. Ignore the alteration if the cheque is crossed.
D. Apply the relevant verification and legal rules and refuse payment if the alteration is unauthorized or otherwise invalid.
Correct Answer: D. Apply the relevant verification and legal rules and refuse payment if the alteration is unauthorized or otherwise invalid.
Explanation: Unauthorized material alterations can affect an instrument's validity and must be handled under the applicable legal rules.
50. Which statement best describes a banker's draft or pay order, subject to the instrument's terms and local practice?
A. It is an instrument issued by a bank directing payment in the prescribed manner.
B. It is a customer's unsecured promise to pay interest.
C. It is a share in the bank's capital.
D. It is an insurance contract for imported goods.
Correct Answer: A. It is an instrument issued by a bank directing payment in the prescribed manner.
Explanation: A banker's draft or pay order is a bank-issued payment instrument whose operation depends on its terms and applicable banking practice.
Section 3: Lending Products and Credit Operations (51–75)
51. Which lending facility is generally used to finance a business's short-term operating needs against eligible current assets or other agreed security?
A. Long-term project equity
B. Residential savings deposit
C. Working-capital finance
D. Deposit insurance
Correct Answer: C. Working-capital finance
Explanation: Working-capital finance supports short-term operating requirements, including inventory, wages and receivables.
52. An overdraft facility generally permits a customer to:
A. Withdraw beyond the available account balance up to an agreed limit
B. Avoid all repayment obligations
C. Receive interest on every unpaid cheque
D. Withdraw unlimited funds without bank approval
Correct Answer: A. Withdraw beyond the available account balance up to an agreed limit
Explanation: An overdraft allows borrowing through an account within an approved limit and agreed conditions.
53. A cash credit facility is commonly associated with:
A. Purchasing shares in the lending bank only
B. Receiving a fixed deposit from the bank
C. Paying customs duties without any borrowing
D. Drawing funds for business needs within an approved limit and agreed terms
Correct Answer: D. Drawing funds for business needs within an approved limit and agreed terms
Explanation: Cash credit provides business financing through drawings permitted within an approved credit limit.
54. Which loan is most directly associated with purchasing a house or apartment?
A. Export packing credit
B. Housing or mortgage finance
C. Bank guarantee
D. Letter of credit
Correct Answer: B. Housing or mortgage finance
Explanation: Housing finance supports eligible residential property purchases, construction or related purposes.
55. What is collateral in lending?
A. A customer's monthly salary only
B. The bank's annual profit
C. An asset or other security offered to support a credit obligation
D. A government tax receipt that always discharges the loan
Correct Answer: C. An asset or other security offered to support a credit obligation
Explanation: Collateral provides security for a loan but does not guarantee complete recovery if the borrower defaults.
56. Which of the following is one of the traditional “5 Cs” of credit?
A. Character
B. Currency printing
C. Clearing
D. Custody of banknotes
Correct Answer: A. Character
Explanation: The five Cs are Character, Capacity, Capital, Collateral and Conditions.
57. A bank evaluates whether a borrower can generate sufficient cash flow to repay a loan. Which credit factor is most directly being assessed?
A. Colour of the collateral documents
B. Number of branches owned by the borrower
C. The borrower's preferred bank logo
D. Capacity
Correct Answer: D. Capacity
Explanation: Capacity assesses the borrower's ability to repay debt from income or business cash flow.
58. What is the principal purpose of credit appraisal?
A. To guarantee that no borrower will ever default
B. To assess repayment ability, risk, purpose, security and loan suitability before approval
C. To replace all loan documentation
D. To eliminate the need for monitoring
Correct Answer: B. To assess repayment ability, risk, purpose, security and loan suitability before approval
Explanation: Credit appraisal helps a bank evaluate whether a proposed loan is suitable and whether repayment is reasonably supported.
59. Which facility allows a bank to finance a customer against invoices or receivables, subject to eligibility and agreement?
A. Savings deposit
B. Banknote issuance
C. Receivables financing
D. Deposit protection
Correct Answer: C. Receivables financing
Explanation: Receivables financing provides liquidity against eligible amounts owed to a business by its customers.
60. In a typical amortizing loan, the scheduled instalments are designed to repay:
A. Principal and interest over the agreed term
B. Interest only, with no principal repayment ever
C. The bank's share capital
D. The borrower's deposits held at unrelated banks
Correct Answer: A. Principal and interest over the agreed term
Explanation: Amortizing instalments generally repay both principal and interest over the contractual repayment period.
61. Which statement best describes a secured loan?
A. It can never become overdue.
B. It is always interest-free.
C. It requires no assessment of the borrower.
D. It is supported by collateral or security under the agreed legal arrangements.
Correct Answer: D. It is supported by collateral or security under the agreed legal arrangements.
Explanation: A secured loan has collateral or other agreed security, but the borrower can still default.
62. What is the main difference between a term loan and a revolving credit facility?
A. A term loan is always a deposit.
B. A term loan normally has a defined repayment schedule, while revolving credit allows redraws within an approved limit under its terms.
C. Revolving credit can never be repaid.
D. A term loan is always issued in foreign currency.
Correct Answer: B. A term loan normally has a defined repayment schedule, while revolving credit allows redraws within an approved limit under its terms.
Explanation: Term loans generally follow a defined repayment schedule, whereas revolving facilities permit drawings and potential redrawings within their limits.
63. A bank lends Tk 800,000 at a simple annual interest rate of 10% for one year. Ignoring fees and taxes, what is the interest?
A. Tk 8,000
B. Tk 40,000
C. Tk 80,000
D. Tk 800,000
Correct Answer: C. Tk 80,000
Explanation: Simple interest = Principal × Rate × Time = Tk 800,000 × 10% × 1 = Tk 80,000.
64. A borrower uses a loan granted for a specified business purpose for an unauthorized unrelated purpose. This is primarily a concern about:
A. Diversion or misuse of loan funds
B. Deposit mobilization
C. Cheque crossing
D. Interest capitalization in every case
Correct Answer: A. Diversion or misuse of loan funds
Explanation: Using borrowed funds for an unauthorized purpose may breach the loan agreement and increase credit risk.
65. Which is the most appropriate action when a loan account shows early signs of repayment stress?
A. Stop monitoring the borrower.
B. Automatically write off the entire loan.
C. Increase the limit without assessment.
D. Review the borrower's financial position, monitor risk and take action under policy and applicable rules.
Correct Answer: D. Review the borrower's financial position, monitor risk and take action under policy and applicable rules.
Explanation: Early-warning signals call for timely review, closer monitoring and appropriate risk-management measures.
66. What does loan-to-value (LTV) ratio measure?
A. Interest expense divided by bank deposits
B. Loan amount relative to the value of the asset securing the loan
C. Bank capital divided by operating expenses
D. Deposits divided by staff salaries
Correct Answer: B. Loan amount relative to the value of the asset securing the loan
Explanation: LTV = Loan amount ÷ Collateral value × 100%. A Tk 6 million loan against property valued at Tk 10 million has an LTV of 60%.
67. A borrower takes Tk 500,000 and repays Tk 200,000 of principal. Ignoring other transactions, what principal balance remains?
A. Tk 500,000
B. Tk 400,000
C. Tk 300,000
D. Tk 200,000
Correct Answer: C. Tk 300,000
Explanation: Outstanding principal = Tk 500,000 − Tk 200,000 = Tk 300,000.
68. Which statement about loan security is correct?
A. Security can reduce potential loss but does not eliminate the need to assess repayment capacity.
B. Collateral guarantees that the borrower will never default.
C. Collateral makes loan documentation unnecessary.
D. Security always removes liquidity risk from a bank.
Correct Answer: A. Security can reduce potential loss but does not eliminate the need to assess repayment capacity.
Explanation: Collateral may help recovery after default, but prudent lending still requires assessment of the borrower's ability to repay.
69. What is a non-performing loan (NPL), in general terms?
A. Any loan repaid before its due date
B. A loan that is always fully secured by cash
C. A loan used to purchase inventory
D. A loan classified as non-performing under the applicable regulatory classification criteria
Correct Answer: D. A loan classified as non-performing under the applicable regulatory classification criteria
Explanation: NPL classification depends on the relevant regulatory criteria, including applicable overdue and other classification conditions.
70. Why is loan provisioning important?
A. It converts all loans into cash.
B. It recognizes expected or prescribed credit losses in accordance with applicable accounting and regulatory requirements.
C. It guarantees loan recovery.
D. It eliminates the need for capital.
Correct Answer: B. It recognizes expected or prescribed credit losses in accordance with applicable accounting and regulatory requirements.
Explanation: Provisioning recognizes credit losses as required by accounting and regulatory rules; it does not itself recover the loan.
71. A bank issues a guarantee on behalf of a customer. Before the guarantee is invoked, the bank's obligation is generally:
A. A customer deposit
B. A fully disbursed term loan in every case
C. A contingent obligation, subject to its terms and applicable accounting and regulatory treatment
D. Share capital belonging to the beneficiary
Correct Answer: C. A contingent obligation, subject to its terms and applicable accounting and regulatory treatment
Explanation: A guarantee generally creates a contingent obligation before invocation, although its accounting and regulatory treatment depends on applicable requirements.
72. What is the primary purpose of loan covenants?
A. To impose agreed conditions, restrictions or obligations on the borrower
B. To remove the borrower's repayment obligation
C. To guarantee an increase in collateral value
D. To replace the loan agreement
Correct Answer: A. To impose agreed conditions, restrictions or obligations on the borrower
Explanation: Covenants establish obligations or restrictions that help the bank monitor and manage credit risk.
73. A bank lends Tk 1,000,000 at 12% simple annual interest for six months. What is the interest, ignoring fees and taxes?
A. Tk 120,000
B. Tk 12,000
C. Tk 60,000
D. Tk 600,000
Correct Answer: C. Tk 60,000
Explanation: Simple interest = Tk 1,000,000 × 12% × 0.5 year = Tk 60,000.
74. Which lending practice best reflects prudent credit risk management?
A. Approving every loan based only on collateral
B. Independently assessing the borrower, purpose, repayment source and risks
