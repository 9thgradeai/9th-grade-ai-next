Questions 1–25: Credit Appraisal and the 5 Cs
1. Which of the following is the primary source of repayment for a normally structured business loan?
A. Sale of the borrower's personal residence
B. Operating cash flow generated by the business
C. Enforcement of the personal guarantee
D. Sale of the bank's other assets
Correct Answer: B. Operating cash flow generated by the business
Explanation: A business loan should normally be repaid from cash generated by the borrower's operations or other reliable repayment sources. Collateral and guarantees are generally secondary sources of recovery rather than substitutes for sound credit appraisal.
2. Which of the following is NOT one of the traditional 5 Cs of credit?
A. Character
B. Capacity
C. Capital
D. Competition
Correct Answer: D. Competition
Explanation: The traditional 5 Cs of credit are Character, Capacity, Capital, Collateral and Conditions. Competition may influence business performance and industry conditions, but it is not one of the five Cs.
3. In credit appraisal, the borrower's willingness to repay is most directly assessed under which C?
A. Character
B. Collateral
C. Capital
D. Conditions
Correct Answer: A. Character
Explanation: Character refers to the borrower's integrity, willingness to repay, repayment history and reliability. Banks assess character through credit records, references, transaction behaviour and verification of information supplied.
4. A bank evaluates whether a borrower's projected cash flow can cover principal and interest payments. Which C is being assessed?
A. Character
B. Collateral
C. Capacity
D. Conditions
Correct Answer: C. Capacity
Explanation: Capacity measures the borrower's ability to repay a loan from available cash flow. Banks assess projected income, operating expenses, existing liabilities and scheduled debt service to determine repayment capacity.
5. A borrower contributes substantial personal funds to a proposed business project. This most directly strengthens which C?
A. Character
B. Capital
C. Conditions
D. Capacity
Correct Answer: B. Capital
Explanation: Capital represents the borrower's own financial contribution and financial strength. A meaningful equity contribution can provide a buffer against losses and demonstrate the borrower's financial commitment to the project.
6. Which of the following is the clearest example of collateral?
A. The borrower's sales forecast
B. The borrower's repayment history
C. The bank's internal audit report
D. Land mortgaged to secure a loan
Correct Answer: D. Land mortgaged to secure a loan
Explanation: Collateral is an asset provided as security for a loan. Land mortgaged to a bank may serve as a secondary recovery source if the borrower defaults, subject to valid ownership, enforceability and applicable legal procedures.
7. A bank studies inflation, industry competition, export demand and government policy before approving a loan. Which C is most relevant?
A. Character
B. Capital
C. Conditions
D. Collateral
Correct Answer: C. Conditions
Explanation: Conditions refer to the economic, industry, market and regulatory environment affecting a borrower's ability to repay. Inflation, changes in export demand and government policies can significantly affect business cash flow.
8. What is the principal objective of credit appraisal?
A. To determine the borrower's creditworthiness and repayment capacity
B. To maximise loan disbursement regardless of risk
C. To ensure every loan is secured by land
D. To eliminate every possibility of default
Correct Answer: A. To determine the borrower's creditworthiness and repayment capacity
Explanation: Credit appraisal evaluates whether a borrower can and is likely to repay a proposed loan. It considers financial strength, cash flow, credit history, business conditions, security and the bank's risk appetite. No appraisal can eliminate all default risk.
9. Which document is generally most useful for assessing a business's historical profitability and financial position?
A. Employee attendance register alone
B. Marketing brochure alone
C. Personal reference letter alone
D. Financial statements, supported by relevant records
Correct Answer: D. Financial statements, supported by relevant records
Explanation: Financial statements provide information about profitability, assets, liabilities and financial performance. Banks should examine supporting records and verify material figures rather than relying exclusively on management-provided statements.
10. A business reports high accounting profits but repeatedly experiences cash shortages. What should the lender investigate most closely?
A. The colour and design of its office
B. Cash-flow timing, receivables and working-capital needs
C. The number of advertisements it publishes
D. The owner's personal preferences
Correct Answer: B. Cash-flow timing, receivables and working-capital needs
Explanation: Accounting profit does not necessarily mean cash is available to repay debt. Delayed customer payments, excess inventory, operating expenses and capital expenditure may create cash shortages even when the business reports profits.
11. Which is the best example of independent credit verification?
A. Accepting the borrower's statements without review
B. Relying exclusively on the loan officer's opinion
C. Checking financial records, bank statements and credit information independently
D. Approving the loan because a competitor granted one
Correct Answer: C. Checking financial records, bank statements and credit information independently
Explanation: Independent verification reduces information asymmetry and helps detect inaccurate disclosures, undisclosed liabilities and repayment weaknesses. It complements the borrower's submitted documents and the bank's internal appraisal.
12. What is the main purpose of obtaining a credit report or relevant credit-information records?
A. To assess existing borrowing, repayment behaviour and potential credit exposure
B. To guarantee future profitability
C. To determine the market price of every asset
D. To replace all other credit appraisal procedures
Correct Answer: A. To assess existing borrowing, repayment behaviour and potential credit exposure
Explanation: Credit information helps a bank understand a borrower's existing obligations and repayment history. In Bangladesh, relevant Bangladesh Bank credit-information arrangements can support credit assessment, but they do not replace independent financial analysis.
13. A bank discovers that a borrower has concealed substantial existing liabilities. Which aspect of creditworthiness is most directly called into question?
A. Collateral marketability only
B. Seasonal demand only
C. Industry growth only
D. Character and the reliability of disclosed financial information
Correct Answer: D. Character and the reliability of disclosed financial information
Explanation: Concealing liabilities raises concerns about honesty and the reliability of the borrower's disclosures. It may also distort leverage calculations and lead the bank to underestimate the risk of repayment failure.
14. Which financial measure most directly indicates whether a borrower can meet scheduled debt payments from available cash flow?
A. Number of employees
B. Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)
C. Market share alone
D. Gross sales alone
Correct Answer: B. Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)
Explanation: DSCR compares cash available for debt service with scheduled principal and interest payments. A ratio above 1 indicates that the defined cash flow exceeds scheduled debt service, although the appropriate lending threshold depends on the circumstances and bank policy.
15. A project costs Tk. 10 million. The borrower contributes Tk. 3 million and seeks a bank loan for the balance. What is the proposed debt-to-equity ratio, using only these project financing amounts?
A. 3:7
B. 7:10
C. 7:3
D. 10:3
Correct Answer: C. 7:3
Explanation: The bank loan is Tk. 10 million − Tk. 3 million = Tk. 7 million. The debt-to-equity ratio is therefore Tk. 7 million divided by Tk. 3 million, or 7:3 (approximately 2.33:1).
16. Which lending practice best reflects sound credit risk management?
A. Separating credit approval from relevant operational and control duties
B. Allowing the same officer to approve, disburse and independently audit a loan without controls
C. Disbursing funds before completing mandatory documentation
D. Renewing every loan automatically
Correct Answer: A. Separating credit approval from relevant operational and control duties
Explanation: Segregation of duties reduces the risk of fraud, errors and unauthorised transactions. Appropriate separation between origination, approval, disbursement and independent review strengthens the bank's internal controls.
17. A bank considers lending to a company whose income depends heavily on one export market. What is the most important additional appraisal concern?
A. The company's logo
B. The number of pages in its annual report
C. The colour of its packaging
D. Concentration risk and vulnerability to changes in that market
Correct Answer: D. Concentration risk and vulnerability to changes in that market
Explanation: Heavy dependence on one export market exposes the borrower to concentrated demand, trade-policy and economic risks. A downturn or restriction in that market could weaken cash flow and repayment capacity.
18. What does a bank primarily assess when conducting sensitivity analysis on a proposed loan?
A. Whether the borrower can avoid all business uncertainty
B. How changes in assumptions affect cash flow and repayment capacity
C. Whether the loan officer can increase the sanctioned amount
D. Whether collateral documents can replace financial analysis
Correct Answer: B. How changes in assumptions affect cash flow and repayment capacity
Explanation: Sensitivity analysis tests the effects of changes in variables such as sales, costs, interest rates and exchange rates. It helps identify whether a borrower can continue servicing debt under less favourable conditions.
19. A bank lends against a warehouse of goods. Before approval, which issue is especially important?
A. The borrower's preferred accounting software
B. The bank's office location
C. Ownership, valuation, condition, insurance and enforceability of the security
D. The number of branches operated by competitors
Correct Answer: C. Ownership, valuation, condition, insurance and enforceability of the security
Explanation: The lender must establish that the goods exist, are properly valued and can legally serve as security. Their condition, insurance, ownership and susceptibility to diversion or deterioration can affect potential recovery.
20. What is the principal purpose of a loan covenant?
A. To impose agreed conditions that help monitor and control credit risk
B. To remove the need for borrower monitoring
C. To guarantee that collateral values will rise
D. To make every borrower exempt from repayment
Correct Answer: A. To impose agreed conditions that help monitor and control credit risk
Explanation: Loan covenants are contractual conditions designed to manage risk. They may require periodic financial reporting, restrict additional borrowing or require the borrower to maintain specified financial conditions.
21. A borrower has good collateral but weak cash flow and a poor repayment history. What is the soundest lending decision?
A. Approve automatically because collateral exists
B. Ignore the repayment history
C. Increase the loan amount to offset the risk
D. Investigate the weaknesses and approve only if the overall risk is acceptable
Correct Answer: D. Investigate the weaknesses and approve only if the overall risk is acceptable
Explanation: Strong collateral does not automatically make a loan safe. The bank should examine repayment capacity, credit history and the reasons for the weaknesses before deciding whether the facility meets its risk appetite and lending standards.
22. Which is the most appropriate use of a bank's internal credit risk rating?
A. Replacing all legal documentation
B. Supporting risk differentiation, approval decisions, monitoring and pricing
C. Guaranteeing that a borrower will never default
D. Eliminating the need for financial statements
Correct Answer: B. Supporting risk differentiation, approval decisions, monitoring and pricing
Explanation: Internal credit ratings help banks distinguish different levels of borrower risk. They can inform approval, pricing, exposure limits and monitoring intensity, but they cannot guarantee repayment.
23. A lender compares projected cash inflows with scheduled principal and interest payments. If the DSCR is 1.25, what does this indicate under the stated calculation?
A. Cash flow covers only 25% of debt service
B. Debt service is 125 times cash flow
C. Cash flow available for debt service is 1.25 times scheduled debt service
D. The borrower has no credit risk
Correct Answer: C. Cash flow available for debt service is 1.25 times scheduled debt service
Explanation: A DSCR of 1.25 means that cash available for debt service is 125% of scheduled debt service. For every Tk. 1 of scheduled debt service, the borrower generates Tk. 1.25 under the stated calculation. The remaining 25% is a cushion, not proof of zero credit risk.
24. Which situation most strongly suggests a need for enhanced credit monitoring after disbursement?
A. The borrower reports falling sales, rising overdue receivables and repeated covenant breaches
B. The borrower submits complete reports on time
C. The borrower maintains stable operating cash flow
D. The borrower repays instalments as agreed
Correct Answer: A. The borrower reports falling sales, rising overdue receivables and repeated covenant breaches
Explanation: Falling sales, delayed collections and covenant breaches are early-warning indicators of deteriorating credit quality. The bank should investigate promptly and reassess the borrower's repayment capacity.
25. A bank proposes a loan to a profitable company operating in a rapidly deteriorating industry. Which appraisal approach is best?
A. Approve solely because last year's profit was high
B. Reject every borrower in that industry automatically
C. Ignore industry conditions if the loan is secured
D. Stress-test forecasts and assess industry outlook, repayment resilience and mitigants
Correct Answer: D. Stress-test forecasts and assess industry outlook, repayment resilience and mitigants
Explanation: Historical profitability alone is insufficient when industry conditions are deteriorating. The bank should evaluate future cash flows under adverse scenarios, assess the borrower's resilience and consider appropriate risk mitigants before approving the loan.
Questions 26–45: Collateral, Security and Credit Documentation
26. What is the main difference between a primary source of repayment and collateral?
A. Both always mean the same thing
B. The primary source is expected repayment cash flow; collateral is a secondary source of recovery
C. Collateral must always be sold before the first instalment
D. The primary source is always the borrower's personal guarantee
Correct Answer: B. The primary source is expected repayment cash flow; collateral is a secondary source of recovery
Explanation: The primary repayment source is the cash flow expected to repay the loan. Collateral provides an alternative recovery source if the borrower defaults. Sound lending requires assessment of repayment capacity even when substantial security is available.
27. Under conventional secured lending terminology, a mortgage most commonly creates security over:
A. A future sales forecast
B. An unsecured promise to repay
C. Immovable property
D. A bank employee's salary
Correct Answer: C. Immovable property
Explanation: A mortgage typically creates a security interest over immovable property, such as land or buildings. The legal form, documentation and perfection requirements depend on the applicable jurisdiction and governing law.
28. Hypothecation generally refers to:
A. A charge over movable assets where possession ordinarily remains with the borrower
B. An outright sale of the bank's assets
C. A deposit paid into the central bank
D. The cancellation of a loan agreement
Correct Answer: A. A charge over movable assets where possession ordinarily remains with the borrower
Explanation: Hypothecation is a common form of security over movable assets, including inventory and receivables, in which the borrower ordinarily retains possession. The lender obtains security rights subject to the applicable agreement and law.
29. In a typical pledge arrangement, what distinguishes the security from hypothecation?
A. A pledge cannot secure a debt
B. A pledge always involves land
C. A pledge eliminates the need for a loan agreement
D. Possession of the pledged goods or security is delivered to the pledgee or its authorised custodian, as applicable
Correct Answer: D. Possession of the pledged goods or security is delivered to the pledgee or its authorised custodian, as applicable
Explanation: Delivery of possession is a defining feature of a traditional pledge. In ordinary hypothecation, the borrower generally retains possession of the charged movable assets.
30. What does the loan-to-value (LTV) ratio measure?
A. Annual profit divided by annual sales
B. Loan exposure divided by the recognised value of the relevant collateral
C. Total deposits divided by bank capital
D. Interest expense divided by operating expenses
Correct Answer: B. Loan exposure divided by the recognised value of the relevant collateral
Explanation: The LTV ratio measures the loan amount relative to the recognised value of collateral. It is calculated as loan amount divided by collateral value, multiplied by 100. A higher LTV generally means a smaller collateral-value cushion.
31. A bank grants a loan of Tk. 6 million against collateral valued at Tk. 8 million. What is the LTV ratio?
A. 75%
B. 80%
C. 125%
D. 133.33%
Correct Answer: A. 75%
Explanation: The LTV ratio is calculated as follows:
\[ LTV=\frac{6}{8}\times100=75\% \]
The loan represents 75% of the stated collateral value.
32. A collateral asset is valued at Tk. 10 million. The bank applies a 20% valuation haircut. What is the adjusted collateral value?
A. Tk. 2 million
B. Tk. 8 million
C. Tk. 10 million
D. Tk. 12 million
Correct Answer: B. Tk. 8 million
Explanation: The haircut is Tk. 10 million × 20% = Tk. 2 million. Therefore, the adjusted collateral value is Tk. 10 million − Tk. 2 million = Tk. 8 million. A haircut provides a margin against valuation uncertainty and potential losses.
33. Why might a bank apply a haircut to collateral value?
A. To increase the borrower's accounting profit
B. To avoid checking ownership
C. To guarantee a higher selling price
D. To allow for valuation uncertainty, market fluctuations and potential liquidation costs or delays
Correct Answer: D. To allow for valuation uncertainty, market fluctuations and potential liquidation costs or delays
Explanation: A collateral haircut reduces the recognised value of security to account for possible price declines, forced-sale discounts, enforcement delays and realisation costs. It does not guarantee the amount that will ultimately be recovered.
34. What is the principal purpose of verifying title documents before accepting land as collateral?
A. To determine the borrower's preferred loan tenure
B. To establish the property's ownership and identify legal defects or competing claims
C. To forecast the company's sales
D. To calculate the central bank policy rate
Correct Answer: B. To establish the property's ownership and identify legal defects or competing claims
Explanation: Title verification helps establish whether the borrower or security provider has the relevant ownership rights. It can reveal disputes, competing claims, restrictions or defects that may undermine the bank's ability to enforce its security.
35. Why is perfection of security important to a lender?
A. It removes all business risks
B. It guarantees that the borrower will repay voluntarily
C. It helps establish and preserve the lender's enforceable security rights under applicable law
D. It makes collateral valuation unnecessary
Correct Answer: C. It helps establish and preserve the lender's enforceable security rights under applicable law
Explanation: Perfection involves completing the applicable legal steps needed to establish or preserve security rights and, where relevant, their priority against competing claimants. The precise requirements vary by asset and governing law.
36. A guarantor promises to meet a borrower's obligations if the agreed conditions for calling the guarantee are satisfied. This is an example of:
A. Credit risk mitigation through a guarantee
B. A cash-flow forecast
C. A loan write-off
D. An equity contribution by the borrower
Correct Answer: A. Credit risk mitigation through a guarantee
Explanation: A guarantee can provide an additional source of repayment if the borrower defaults and the guarantee is enforceable. Its effectiveness depends on the guarantee's terms, the guarantor's financial capacity and applicable legal requirements.
37. Which statement about collateral is most accurate?
A. Every secured loan is risk-free
B. Collateral always generates enough cash to repay the loan
C. Collateral makes credit appraisal unnecessary
D. Collateral can reduce potential loss, but it does not eliminate default risk
Correct Answer: D. Collateral can reduce potential loss, but it does not eliminate default risk
Explanation: Collateral may reduce the lender's loss if a borrower defaults, but asset values can fall and enforcement can be delayed or costly. Banks must still assess the borrower's repayment capacity and overall creditworthiness.
38. Which collateral is generally the most liquid, assuming valid ownership and immediate availability?
A. An unregistered claim to disputed land
B. Cash held under a legally enforceable, properly controlled cash-collateral arrangement
C. Specialised machinery with no known resale market
D. An unfinished building with unresolved title disputes
Correct Answer: B. Cash held under a legally enforceable, properly controlled cash-collateral arrangement
Explanation: Properly controlled cash collateral is generally more readily realisable than disputed property or specialised machinery. The lender must still consider legal access, restrictions, competing claims and other relevant conditions.
39. What is the main risk of relying on an outdated property valuation?
A. The borrower's sales automatically increase
B. The bank's capital becomes risk-free
C. The collateral's current realisable value may be materially lower than recorded
D. The loan agreement becomes a deposit account
Correct Answer: C. The collateral's current realisable value may be materially lower than recorded
Explanation: Property prices and asset conditions change over time. An outdated valuation can overstate the security available to support a loan and create an unexpected shortfall during recovery.
40. Why is it important for a bank to monitor inventory financed through a working-capital facility?
A. To detect shortages, deterioration, diversion, overvaluation or duplicate financing
B. To replace all financial statements with physical counts
C. To guarantee that inventory prices rise
D. To remove the borrower's obligation to insure goods where required
Correct Answer: A. To detect shortages, deterioration, diversion, overvaluation or duplicate financing
Explanation: Inventory monitoring helps ensure that the financed goods exist, retain value and remain available as security. It can also reveal diversion of stock, obsolete inventory, inaccurate reporting or multiple financing claims.
41. Which document most directly records the terms of an approved loan, including the amount, pricing, tenor and repayment conditions?
A. A marketing leaflet
B. A newspaper advertisement
C. A supplier's catalogue
D. The executed facility or loan agreement, together with its applicable terms
Correct Answer: D. The executed facility or loan agreement, together with its applicable terms
Explanation: The executed facility or loan agreement records the agreed lending terms and obligations. Additional security documents, guarantees and supporting records may also be necessary to establish and enforce the bank's rights.
42. A bank's security documents are signed, but a legally required registration or perfection step has not been completed. What is the main concern?
A. The loan becomes automatically profitable
B. The security may not have the intended enforceability or priority
C. The borrower no longer owes the debt
D. The collateral's market value must double
Correct Answer: B. The security may not have the intended enforceability or priority
Explanation: Signing documents may not be sufficient to complete every legal requirement for establishing security. Failure to complete a required registration or perfection step can affect enforceability or priority, depending on the applicable law.
43. What does collateral concentration risk mean?
A. Every loan has identical repayment terms
B. The bank holds only cash deposits
C. A bank is excessively exposed to similar collateral types or correlated collateral values
D. Borrowers use different industries
Correct Answer: C. A bank is excessively exposed to similar collateral types or correlated collateral values
Explanation: Collateral concentration risk arises when a large portion of a bank's security depends on similar assets or markets. A common market downturn can reduce the value of many collateral assets simultaneously.
44. What is the most appropriate response if collateral value falls materially after a loan is disbursed?
A. Reassess exposure and risk, review contractual rights and consider appropriate mitigants
B. Ignore the fall if the loan was initially approved
C. Automatically write off the full loan
D. Stop monitoring the borrower
Correct Answer: A. Reassess exposure and risk, review contractual rights and consider appropriate mitigants
Explanation: A material decline in collateral value may increase the bank's potential loss. The lender should reassess coverage, borrower repayment capacity and applicable contractual rights, then take proportionate action.
45. A bank holds collateral worth Tk. 5 million after applying an appropriate valuation haircut. The outstanding loan is Tk. 4 million. Ignoring accrued costs, what is the collateral coverage ratio?
A. 80%
B. 100%
C. 120%
D. 125%
Correct Answer: D. 125%
Explanation: The collateral coverage ratio is calculated as follows:
\[ \text{Coverage ratio}=\frac{5}{4}\times100=125\% \]
The adjusted collateral value is 125% of the outstanding loan. Actual recovery may still be lower because of enforcement costs, delays or further changes in asset value.
Questions 46–65: NPLs, Classification and Provisioning
46. What is a non-performing loan (NPL)?
A. Any loan that has been fully repaid
B. A loan or credit exposure classified as non-performing under the applicable regulatory framework
C. Any loan secured by property
D. A loan that generates interest income on schedule
Correct Answer: B. A loan or credit exposure classified as non-performing under the applicable regulatory framework
Explanation: A non-performing loan is a loan classified as non-performing under the relevant regulatory criteria. The classification depends on the applicable rules, including overdue status and other prescribed conditions.
47. Which development is a strong early warning signal of possible deterioration in a performing loan?
A. Regular repayment and improving cash flow
B. Timely submission of financial statements
C. Persistent overdue payments and worsening borrower cash flow
D. A decrease in the borrower's inventory holding period, with all else stable
Correct Answer: C. Persistent overdue payments and worsening borrower cash flow
Explanation: Persistent overdue payments and deteriorating cash flow indicate that the borrower may be experiencing financial difficulty. Early investigation allows the bank to reassess credit risk and consider corrective action.
48. What is loan classification intended to achieve?
A. To identify the credit quality of exposures using applicable criteria
B. To determine bank employees' annual leave
C. To eliminate all credit risk
D. To establish the market price of shares
Correct Answer: A. To identify the credit quality of exposures using applicable criteria
Explanation: Loan classification identifies the credit quality of exposures according to regulatory and institutional criteria. It supports credit monitoring, provisioning, financial reporting and regulatory compliance.
49. What is the main purpose of loan-loss provisioning?
A. To increase gross loan disbursement
B. To guarantee recovery of every defaulted loan
C. To replace the bank's capital requirement
D. To recognise expected or prescribed credit losses through appropriate expense and allowance arrangements
Correct Answer: D. To recognise expected or prescribed credit losses through appropriate expense and allowance arrangements
Explanation: Loan-loss provisions recognise the financial impact of credit losses under the applicable framework. Adequate provisioning helps present a more realistic picture of asset quality and profitability, but it does not guarantee recovery or replace capital requirements.
50. What is the difference between a gross NPL ratio and a net NPL ratio?
A. Both always use the same numerator and denominator
B. The gross ratio uses gross NPLs relative to gross loans; the net ratio adjusts for relevant provisions and other specified items under its reporting definition
C. The gross ratio measures deposits, while the net ratio measures staff costs
D. The net ratio is always higher than the gross ratio
Correct Answer: B. The gross ratio uses gross NPLs relative to gross loans; the net ratio adjusts for relevant provisions and other specified items under its reporting definition
Explanation: The gross NPL ratio compares gross non-performing loans with gross loans. A net NPL measure accounts for eligible provisions and other prescribed adjustments under its reporting definition. The exact formula should be checked before comparing reported figures.
51. A bank has gross loans of Tk. 500 million and gross NPLs of Tk. 40 million. What is its gross NPL ratio?
A. 4%
B. 6%
C. 8%
D. 12.5%
Correct Answer: C. 8%
Explanation: The gross NPL ratio is calculated as:
\[ \frac{40}{500}\times100=8\% \]
Therefore, gross NPLs represent 8% of the bank's gross loan portfolio.
52. A bank has Tk. 20 million of NPLs and Tk. 15 million of eligible provisions. Ignoring other adjustments, what is the uncovered NPL amount?
A. Tk. 5 million
B. Tk. 15 million
C. Tk. 20 million
D. Tk. 35 million
Correct Answer: A. Tk. 5 million
Explanation: The uncovered amount is Tk. 20 million − Tk. 15 million = Tk. 5 million. This simplified calculation assumes the entire provision amount is eligible to offset the specified NPL balance.
53. Under an expected-credit-loss (ECL) approach, the main objective is to:
A. Recognise losses only after every recovery action has failed
B. Treat all loans as having identical risk
C. Ignore forward-looking economic information
D. Estimate credit losses using relevant historical, current and forward-looking information
Correct Answer: D. Estimate credit losses using relevant historical, current and forward-looking information
Explanation: An expected-credit-loss approach estimates credit losses using relevant historical experience, current conditions and reasonable forward-looking information. It does not necessarily wait until a borrower has completely defaulted before recognising expected losses.
54. Under the framework described in Bangladesh Bank's 27 November 2024 master circular, as summarised in its 2024 Financial Stability Report, which overdue-duration mapping was specified for objective classification criteria?
A. SS at 1–3 months; DF at 3–6 months; Bad/Loss at 6 months or more
B. SS at 3 to less than 6 months; DF at 6 to less than 12 months; Bad/Loss at 12 months or more
C. SS at 6–12 months; DF at 12–18 months; Bad/Loss after 24 months
D. All loans become Bad/Loss after 3 months
Correct Answer: B. SS at 3 to less than 6 months; DF at 6 to less than 12 months; Bad/Loss at 12 months or more
Explanation: The historical framework described in the question mapped the specified overdue periods to Substandard (SS), Doubtful (DF) and Bad/Loss categories. These figures should not automatically be treated as the rules currently applicable in October 2026; subsequent Bangladesh Bank circulars may change classification requirements.
55. Under the same framework summarised in the cited 2024 report, what specific provisioning rates were stated for SS, DF and Bad/Loss, respectively?
A. 5%, 10%, 20%
B. 10%, 20%, 50%
C. 20%, 50%, 100%
D. 25%, 75%, 100%
Correct Answer: C. 20%, 50%, 100%
Explanation: The historical framework summary cited for this question stated specific provision rates of 20% for SS, 50% for DF and 100% for Bad/Loss. These are framework-specific figures; current requirements, applicable provision bases and permitted adjustments must be verified against the operative Bangladesh Bank circular.
56. Under that same summary, what general provision rates were specified for STD-0, STD-1 and STD-2, and for SMA, respectively?
A. 1% and 5%
B. 0% and 1%
C. 2% and 10%
D. 5% and 20%
Correct Answer: A. 1% and 5%
Explanation: The historical summary specified a 1% general provision rate for STD-0, STD-1 and STD-2 and 5% for SMA. These rates should be checked against the latest applicable Bangladesh Bank rules before being used as current regulatory facts.
57. A loan balance of Tk. 2 million is subject to a hypothetical specific provision rate of 20%, with no eligible adjustments or deductions. What provision is required under that assumption?
A. Tk. 40,000
B. Tk. 200,000
C. Tk. 2 million
D. Tk. 400,000
Correct Answer: D. Tk. 400,000
Explanation: The provision is calculated as Tk. 2,000,000 × 20% = Tk. 400,000. The question explicitly supplies a hypothetical rate; this calculation does not establish that the rate applies to every current loan category.
58. A bank's required provision is Tk. 12 million, but its eligible provision balance is Tk. 9 million. What is the shortfall?
A. Tk. 3 million
B. Tk. 9 million
C. Tk. 12 million
D. Tk. 21 million
Correct Answer: A. Tk. 3 million
Explanation: Provision shortfall equals required provision minus eligible provision already held. Therefore, Tk. 12 million − Tk. 9 million = Tk. 3 million.
59. What is the principal danger of under-provisioning for credit losses?
A. It necessarily increases cash collections
B. It eliminates the need for capital
C. It may overstate reported profit and asset quality while understating the impact of credit losses
D. It automatically improves borrowers' repayment capacity
Correct Answer: C. It may overstate reported profit and asset quality while understating the impact of credit losses
Explanation: Under-provisioning can understate expected or prescribed losses and overstate reported profit or net asset values. This can mislead stakeholders about the bank's actual financial condition and resilience.
60. A bank reschedules a troubled loan under an approved arrangement. What is the most accurate statement?
A. Rescheduling always means the debt has been repaid
B. Rescheduling changes repayment terms but does not automatically eliminate underlying credit risk or override applicable classification rules
C. Rescheduling makes collateral legally unnecessary
D. Rescheduling automatically converts the loan into equity
Correct Answer: B. Rescheduling changes repayment terms but does not automatically eliminate underlying credit risk or override applicable classification rules
Explanation: Rescheduling changes the repayment timetable under an approved arrangement. It does not establish that the borrower is financially sound or automatically remove the exposure's classification and provisioning obligations.
61. What does loan restructuring generally aim to do?
A. Increase the borrower's debt without assessment
B. Hide every default from financial statements
C. Remove all legal obligations
D. Modify the financing terms or structure to address repayment difficulties, subject to applicable rules
Correct Answer: D. Modify the financing terms or structure to address repayment difficulties, subject to applicable rules
Explanation: Loan restructuring may change repayment schedules, tenor or other financing terms to address financial difficulties. It should be based on a realistic repayment assessment and comply with applicable regulatory requirements.

Questions 81–100: Risk Types, Ratios and Advanced Applications
81. A borrower fails to pay principal and interest as agreed. Which risk has directly materialised for the bank?
A. Reputational risk only
B. Market risk only
C. Credit risk
D. Strategic risk only
Correct Answer: C. Credit risk
Explanation: Credit risk is the possibility of financial loss because a borrower or counterparty fails to meet its contractual obligations. Missed principal or interest payments are direct manifestations of this risk.
82. A bank incurs losses because market interest rates change and affect the value or income of its positions. Which risk is most relevant?
A. Market risk
B. Credit risk only
C. Legal risk only
D. Reputational risk only
Correct Answer: A. Market risk
Explanation: Market risk arises from adverse movements in market variables, including interest rates, foreign exchange rates and security prices. Changes in interest rates can affect the value of financial instruments and a bank's income.
83. A bank cannot meet payment obligations when due without incurring unacceptable losses. Which risk is this?
A. Strategic risk
B. Model risk
C. Reputational risk
D. Liquidity risk
Correct Answer: D. Liquidity risk
Explanation: Liquidity risk is the risk that a bank cannot meet its financial obligations when they fall due without incurring unacceptable losses. It differs from solvency risk, which concerns the institution's ability to absorb losses and sustain its financial position.
84. A payment-processing system fails because of a technology outage and weak operational controls. Which risk is most directly involved?
A. Sovereign risk only
B. Operational risk
C. Equity risk only
D. Credit concentration risk only
Correct Answer: B. Operational risk
Explanation: Operational risk arises from inadequate or failed internal processes, people, systems or external events. Technology outages and weaknesses in transaction-processing controls are common examples.
85. A bank's reputation suffers after customers lose confidence because of a major service failure. Which risk is most directly described?
A. Settlement risk
B. Interest-rate risk
C. Reputational risk
D. Collateral risk
Correct Answer: C. Reputational risk
Explanation: Reputational risk is the possibility of loss resulting from negative perceptions of a bank's reliability, integrity or service quality. A serious service failure can reduce customer confidence and damage business relationships.
86. A bank grants a large proportion of its loans to one industry. A downturn in that industry could cause widespread borrower defaults. This is primarily:

Explanation: Concentration risk arises when exposures are excessively concentrated in one borrower, industry, geographical area or other correlated group. A downturn affecting that group can cause simultaneous defaults and substantial losses.

C. Inventory turnover risk
