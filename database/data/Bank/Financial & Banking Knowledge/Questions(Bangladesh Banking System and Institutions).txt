Questions 1–20: Bangladesh Bank — Establishment and Functions
1. Bangladesh Bank is the central bank of which country?
A. Bangladesh
B. Nepal
C. Sri Lanka
D. Myanmar
Correct Answer: A. Bangladesh
Explanation: Bangladesh Bank is the central bank of Bangladesh. It performs central banking functions, including monetary policy, foreign-reserve management and banking supervision.
2. Which legal instrument established Bangladesh Bank?
A. Bank Company Act, 1991
B. Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972
C. Financial Institutions Act, 1993
D. Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881
Correct Answer: B. Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972
Explanation: The Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972 (President's Order No. 127 of 1972) provides the foundational legal framework for Bangladesh Bank.
3. From which date is Bangladesh Bank deemed to have been established?
A. 26 March 1971
B. 16 December 1972
C. 16 December 1971
D. 31 October 1972
Correct Answer: C. 16 December 1971
Explanation: The Bangladesh Bank Order deems the Bank established with effect from 16 December 1971. Do not confuse this date with the Order's promulgation in 1972.
4. Which article of the Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972 specifies the Bank's main functions?
A. Article 3
B. Article 20
C. Article 44
D. Article 7A
Correct Answer: D. Article 7A
Explanation: Article 7A specifies the main functions of Bangladesh Bank. It includes monetary-policy formulation and implementation, foreign-exchange intervention, management of official reserves, and regulation and supervision of banks and financial institutions within its mandate.
5. Which of the following is a core function of Bangladesh Bank?
A. Formulating and implementing monetary policy
B. Setting national income-tax rates
C. Conducting all commercial lending directly to households
D. Auditing every private company as its primary function
Correct Answer: A. Formulating and implementing monetary policy
Explanation: Bangladesh Bank manages monetary and credit conditions to support macroeconomic stability. Taxation is principally a fiscal-policy matter, while ordinary household lending is generally conducted by commercial banks and other authorised lenders.
6. Which institution has the principal responsibility for managing Bangladesh's official foreign-exchange reserves?
A. Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission
B. Bangladesh Bank
C. National Board of Revenue
D. Dhaka Stock Exchange
Correct Answer: B. Bangladesh Bank
Explanation: Bangladesh Bank manages the country's official foreign reserves. These reserves support external payments, foreign-exchange management and broader economic stability.
7. Which institution is responsible for issuing Bangladesh's banknotes within its statutory authority?
A. Ministry of Commerce
B. Sonali Bank PLC
C. Bangladesh Bank
D. Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission
Correct Answer: C. Bangladesh Bank
Explanation: Bangladesh Bank has statutory authority concerning banknote issuance. Commercial banks distribute banknotes through their banking operations but do not independently issue national banknotes.
8. In its capacity as banker to the government, Bangladesh Bank may:
A. Set every government expenditure priority independently
B. Replace the Ministry of Finance
C. Guarantee every government contractor's debt
D. Handle government banking transactions and public-debt management as provided by law
Correct Answer: D. Handle government banking transactions and public-debt management as provided by law
Explanation: Bangladesh Bank performs specified government banking and public-debt functions under the applicable legal framework. It does not replace the government in determining overall taxation and expenditure policy.
9. Which institution primarily regulates and supervises banking companies in Bangladesh?
A. Bangladesh Bank
B. Bangladesh Public Service Commission
C. Bangladesh Competition Commission alone
D. Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics
Correct Answer: A. Bangladesh Bank
Explanation: Bangladesh Bank exercises banking supervision under the Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972, the Bank Company Act, 1991, and other applicable legislation and directives.
10. What is the principal purpose of monetary policy?
A. Determining the ownership of every commercial bank
B. Managing monetary and credit conditions in pursuit of macroeconomic stability
C. Fixing every company's selling price
D. Eliminating all commercial risks
Correct Answer: B. Managing monetary and credit conditions in pursuit of macroeconomic stability
Explanation: Monetary policy influences liquidity, credit conditions, interest rates and inflationary pressure. It cannot eliminate every economic risk or guarantee a specific outcome.
11. When Bangladesh Bank increases its policy interest rate, the intended transmission mechanism may include:
A. Automatically increasing every bank's profit
B. Eliminating inflation immediately
C. Raising borrowing costs and moderating credit demand
D. Increasing the money supply without any offsetting effect
Correct Answer: C. Raising borrowing costs and moderating credit demand
Explanation: Higher policy rates may increase market borrowing costs, discouraging some consumption and investment. The actual effect depends on the strength and timing of monetary-policy transmission.
12. Which instrument involves Bangladesh Bank buying or selling securities to influence liquidity?
A. Company registration
B. Income-tax assessment
C. Export licensing
D. Open-market operations
Correct Answer: D. Open-market operations
Explanation: Open-market operations involve securities purchases or sales to influence liquidity and monetary conditions. They are distinct from taxation and company-registration activities.
13. If Bangladesh Bank sells securities to banks in a liquidity-absorbing operation, the immediate intended effect is generally to:
A. Reduce banking-system liquidity
B. Increase banks' excess reserves
C. Eliminate credit risk
D. Automatically increase government tax revenue
Correct Answer: A. Reduce banking-system liquidity
Explanation: Buyers pay for the securities, withdrawing funds from the banking system in a conventional liquidity-absorbing transaction. The precise effect depends on the operation's structure and settlement arrangements.
14. What does a cash reserve requirement generally oblige a bank to do?
A. Hold all deposits as physical cash in its branches
B. Maintain a prescribed reserve with the central bank
C. Invest all deposits in government bonds
D. Keep every loan fully secured by property
Correct Answer: B. Maintain a prescribed reserve with the central bank
Explanation: The cash reserve requirement (CRR) concerns reserves maintained with Bangladesh Bank under applicable rules. It does not mean that all deposits must remain as physical cash in bank branches.
15. The statutory liquidity requirement generally concerns a bank's obligation to maintain:
A. A fixed number of branches
B. Only foreign-currency deposits
C. Prescribed liquid assets in accordance with applicable rules
D. Only cash held in ATMs
Correct Answer: C. Prescribed liquid assets in accordance with applicable rules
Explanation: The statutory liquidity requirement (SLR) concerns qualifying liquid assets maintained under applicable regulations. It differs from CRR, which concerns prescribed central-bank reserves.
16. Which statement about Bangladesh Bank's exchange-rate responsibilities is most accurate?
A. It has no role in the foreign-exchange market
B. It guarantees that the taka will never depreciate
C. It sets global prices of foreign currencies
D. It formulates and implements foreign-exchange intervention policies within its mandate
Correct Answer: D. It formulates and implements foreign-exchange intervention policies within its mandate
Explanation: Bangladesh Bank has responsibilities relating to foreign-exchange policy and intervention. These responsibilities do not constitute a guarantee that the taka will never depreciate.
17. Bangladesh Bank's role in the national payment system includes:
A. Promoting, regulating and ensuring secure and efficient payment systems
B. Manufacturing all payment cards
C. Approving every retail purchase
D. Operating every private e-commerce website
Correct Answer: A. Promoting, regulating and ensuring secure and efficient payment systems
Explanation: Bangladesh Bank has responsibilities for payment-system regulation and development. Commercial banks and authorised payment-service providers deliver many customer-facing services.
18. Which statement best describes Bangladesh Bank's responsibility for credit information?
A. It determines every borrower's salary
B. It collects and furnishes credit information under the applicable framework
C. It guarantees repayment of every bank loan
D. It replaces all banks' internal credit appraisal
Correct Answer: B. It collects and furnishes credit information under the applicable framework
Explanation: Credit information supports banks' lending decisions and risk assessment. It does not guarantee repayment or eliminate the need for independent credit appraisal.
19. Which of the following is NOT a core central-bank function?
A. Managing official foreign reserves
B. Implementing monetary policy
C. Operating a retail grocery business
D. Supervising banks under its statutory authority
Correct Answer: C. Operating a retail grocery business
Explanation: Monetary policy, reserve management and banking supervision are recognised central-bank functions. Operating a grocery business is unrelated to Bangladesh Bank's institutional mandate.
20. Which statement best distinguishes monetary policy from fiscal policy?
A. Both are exclusively set by private commercial banks
B. Monetary policy concerns taxation only
C. Fiscal policy concerns reserve requirements only
D. Monetary policy manages monetary and credit conditions; fiscal policy concerns government revenue and expenditure
Correct Answer: D. Monetary policy manages monetary and credit conditions; fiscal policy concerns government revenue and expenditure
Explanation: Monetary policy operates through instruments such as policy rates and liquidity management. Fiscal policy concerns taxation, public expenditure and government borrowing.
Questions 21–40: Types of Banks and Institutional Classification
21. What is the principal legal distinction between a scheduled bank and a non-scheduled bank in Bangladesh?

B. A non-scheduled bank must be privately owned
C. A scheduled bank is included in the list maintained under the Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972
D. A scheduled bank can operate only in urban areas
Correct Answer: C. A scheduled bank is included in the list maintained under the Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972
Explanation: Scheduled status is a legal classification, not a description of ownership or geographic coverage. A bank's status should be verified against Bangladesh Bank's official list.
22. Which institution is the central bank rather than an ordinary commercial bank?
A. Bangladesh Bank
B. Agrani Bank PLC
C. BRAC Bank PLC
D. Eastern Bank PLC
Correct Answer: A. Bangladesh Bank
Explanation: Bangladesh Bank performs central banking functions. Agrani Bank, BRAC Bank and Eastern Bank are commercial banking institutions serving customers and businesses.
23. Which of the following is a state-owned commercial bank?
A. Standard Chartered Bank
B. Eastern Bank PLC
C. BRAC Bank PLC
D. Sonali Bank PLC
Correct Answer: D. Sonali Bank PLC
Explanation: Sonali Bank is a state-owned commercial bank. Standard Chartered is a foreign bank, while Eastern Bank and BRAC Bank are private commercial banking institutions.
24. Which institution is primarily associated with agricultural credit as a specialised banking mandate?
A. Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission
B. Bangladesh Krishi Bank
C. Bangladesh Bank's Currency Museum
D. Bangladesh Development Research Institute
Correct Answer: B. Bangladesh Krishi Bank
Explanation: Bangladesh Krishi Bank is a specialised bank focused on agricultural finance. Its role differs from the central bank's system-wide monetary and supervisory functions.
25. Rajshahi Krishi Unnayan Bank primarily focuses on:
A. Securities-market regulation
B. International credit-rating services
C. Agricultural development in its designated northwestern service area
D. Foreign central-bank reserve management
Correct Answer: C. Agricultural development in its designated northwestern service area
Explanation: Rajshahi Krishi Unnayan Bank (RAKUB) supports agricultural and rural development in its designated region. It is not a securities regulator or a central bank.
26. Which of the following is a foreign commercial bank operating in Bangladesh?
A. Standard Chartered Bank
B. Sonali Bank PLC
C. Bangladesh Krishi Bank
D. Rajshahi Krishi Unnayan Bank
Correct Answer: A. Standard Chartered Bank
Explanation: Standard Chartered is a foreign banking institution operating in Bangladesh. Sonali Bank is state-owned, while Bangladesh Krishi Bank and RAKUB have specialised agricultural mandates.
27. A foreign commercial bank operating in Bangladesh generally differs from a locally incorporated private commercial bank because it:
A. Is exempt from every Bangladesh banking law
B. Cannot provide banking services to businesses
C. Is regulated exclusively by its home-country government
D. Operates as a branch or other authorised presence of a bank incorporated abroad
Correct Answer: D. Operates as a branch or other authorised presence of a bank incorporated abroad
Explanation: A foreign bank may operate through a branch or another authorised form. Its operations in Bangladesh remain subject to applicable local licensing and regulatory requirements.
28. Which institution is associated with financing small and medium-sized enterprises in Bangladesh?
A. Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission
B. BRAC Bank PLC
C. Bangladesh Bank's Monetary Policy Department alone
D. Bangladesh Public Service Commission
Correct Answer: B. BRAC Bank PLC
Explanation: BRAC Bank is well known for its SME banking activities. However, SME finance is not exclusive to this institution; other banks and financial institutions also provide SME financing.
29. Which of the following is a specialised bank rather than a conventional private commercial bank?
A. Prime Bank PLC
B. City Bank PLC
C. Bangladesh Krishi Bank
D. Dhaka Bank PLC
Correct Answer: C. Bangladesh Krishi Bank
Explanation: Bangladesh Krishi Bank has a specialised agricultural-financing mandate. Prime Bank, City Bank and Dhaka Bank are private commercial banking institutions.
30. A bank is described as a state-owned commercial bank primarily because of its:
A. Ownership structure and commercial banking role
B. Exclusive use of mobile banking
C. Foreign incorporation
D. Prohibition on deposit-taking
Correct Answer: A. Ownership structure and commercial banking role
Explanation: The classification reflects government ownership and the institution's commercial banking function. It does not depend on whether the bank uses digital channels or accepts deposits.
31. Which statement about Islamic banking in Bangladesh is correct?
A. Every Islamic bank is a government department
B. Islamic banks are outside the banking regulatory framework
C. Islamic banks cannot finance trade
D. Islamic banks conduct banking according to Shariah principles within the applicable regulatory framework
Correct Answer: D. Islamic banks conduct banking according to Shariah principles within the applicable regulatory framework
Explanation: Islamic banks use Shariah-compliant financing and investment structures. They remain subject to applicable banking laws and regulatory supervision.
32. A conventional private commercial bank differs from a full-fledged Islamic bank principally in its:
A. Nationality of customers
B. Operating and financing principles
C. Obligation to comply with applicable banking laws
D. Need to maintain accurate accounts
Correct Answer: B. Operating and financing principles
Explanation: Conventional banking commonly uses interest-based arrangements, while Islamic banking uses Shariah-compliant structures. Both must comply with applicable banking laws.
33. A bank that offers both conventional and Islamic banking services is commonly described as a bank with:
A. No regulatory obligations
B. Exclusive central-bank ownership
C. Dual banking operations
D. No deposit-taking authority
Correct Answer: C. Dual banking operations
Explanation: Dual banking operations refer to offering conventional and Islamic banking services within the applicable legal and regulatory framework. The term does not imply exemption from supervision.
34. Which of the following is an example of a non-bank financial institution rather than a commercial bank?
A. A licensed finance company
B. Sonali Bank PLC
C. City Bank PLC
D. Standard Chartered Bank Bangladesh
Correct Answer: A. A licensed finance company
Explanation: A finance company is a non-bank financial institution operating under its applicable regulatory framework. Commercial banks have a different legal status and set of permitted activities.
35. Which statement about finance companies in Bangladesh is generally correct?
A. They may perform every function of a scheduled bank without restriction
B. They are regulated only by stock exchanges
C. They are the central bank of Bangladesh
D. They operate under the applicable finance-company regulatory framework and face restrictions distinguishing them from banks
Correct Answer: D. They operate under the applicable finance-company regulatory framework and face restrictions distinguishing them from banks
Explanation: Finance companies are governed by the applicable finance-company legislation and Bangladesh Bank's relevant regulations. Their permitted activities differ from those of scheduled banks.
36. Which activity generally distinguishes finance companies from banks under the applicable Bangladesh regulatory framework?
A. Maintaining accounting records
B. Receiving demand deposits and issuing cheques as a bank does
C. Providing any financing whatsoever
D. Being subject to regulation
Correct Answer: B. Receiving demand deposits and issuing cheques as a bank does
Explanation: Finance companies face restrictions on certain bank-like activities, including demand-deposit-taking and cheque issuance. The precise legal scope should be checked against current legislation and Bangladesh Bank directives.
37. What is the principal purpose of a specialised bank?
A. To replace the central bank
B. To regulate the securities market
C. To serve a defined development or sectoral financing mandate
D. To set the country's tax policy
Correct Answer: C. To serve a defined development or sectoral financing mandate
Explanation: Specialised banks focus on defined sectors or development objectives, such as agriculture. They complement rather than replace the central bank and general-purpose commercial banks.
38. Which statement about ownership and regulatory oversight is most accurate?
A. Private ownership does not exempt a bank from Bangladesh Bank's applicable supervision
B. Government ownership removes all prudential requirements
C. Foreign ownership automatically removes local licensing requirements
D. Islamic ownership prevents regulatory examination
Correct Answer: A. Private ownership does not exempt a bank from Bangladesh Bank's applicable supervision
Explanation: Banks remain subject to applicable licensing, prudential, reporting and governance requirements regardless of their ownership structure.
39. Which of the following is NOT a valid classification based on a bank's ownership or institutional role?
A. State-owned commercial bank
B. Foreign commercial bank
C. Specialised bank
D. Bank classified according to the colour of its logo
Correct Answer: D. Bank classified according to the colour of its logo
Explanation: Banking classifications are based on legal status, ownership, purpose or operating model. A bank's logo colour has no legal significance for institutional classification.
40. What is the best way to verify the current number of scheduled banks in Bangladesh before an examination?
A. Rely on an undated coaching-centre infographic
B. Consult Bangladesh Bank's latest official banking statistics or bank list
C. Add up bank names remembered from previous years

Correct Answer: B. Consult Bangladesh Bank's latest official banking statistics or bank list

Questions 41–60: Laws, Regulators and Institutional Responsibilities
41. Which law is a principal statute governing banking companies in Bangladesh?
A. Companies Act, 1994, exclusively
B. Income Tax Act, exclusively
C. Bank Company Act, 1991
D. Public Procurement Act, exclusively
Correct Answer: C. Bank Company Act, 1991
Explanation: The Bank Company Act, 1991, is a principal statute governing banking companies. Other laws and Bangladesh Bank directives may apply depending on the activity and issue.
42. Which institution is the primary regulator of banks and finance companies in Bangladesh?
A. Bangladesh Bank
B. Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission
C. Bangladesh Public Service Commission
D. Election Commission of Bangladesh
Correct Answer: A. Bangladesh Bank
Explanation: Bangladesh Bank is the principal regulator of banks and finance companies under the applicable legal framework. BSEC regulates the securities market, while the other institutions have different mandates.
43. The Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission (BSEC) principally regulates:
A. Monetary-policy implementation
B. Issuance of Bangladesh's banknotes
C. Commercial-bank reserve accounts
D. Securities and capital markets within its statutory mandate
Correct Answer: D. Securities and capital markets within its statutory mandate
Explanation: BSEC oversees securities-market activities and relevant market participants under its governing legislation. It does not issue Bangladesh's banknotes or implement monetary policy.

C. Bangladesh Public Service Commission
D. Bangladesh Tourism Board

46. Which regulator is responsible for microcredit institutions licensed under the Microcredit Regulatory Authority framework?

C. Bangladesh Bank's Currency Department exclusively

47. Which institution principally regulates insurance companies in Bangladesh?
A. Bangladesh Financial Intelligence Unit
B. Bangladesh Bank's foreign-reserve department
C. Dhaka Stock Exchange
D. Insurance Development and Regulatory Authority (IDRA)
Correct Answer: D. Insurance Development and Regulatory Authority (IDRA)
Explanation: IDRA is responsible for insurance-sector regulation. Bangladesh Bank supervises banks and finance companies, while the Dhaka Stock Exchange operates within the securities-market framework.
48. Which statement correctly describes the relationship between Bangladesh Bank and BFIU?
A. They are both stock exchanges
B. Bangladesh Bank houses BFIU, which performs specialised financial-intelligence functions
C. BFIU issues Bangladesh's banknotes
D. BFIU replaces all commercial banks' compliance departments
Correct Answer: B. Bangladesh Bank houses BFIU, which performs specialised financial-intelligence functions
Explanation: BFIU operates within Bangladesh Bank's institutional framework and performs specialised financial-intelligence functions. Banks nevertheless retain their own AML/CFT compliance responsibilities.
49. Which law is particularly relevant to preventing money laundering in Bangladesh?
A. Bangladesh Labour Act, exclusively
B. Trade Marks Act, exclusively
C. Money Laundering Prevention Act, 2012, as applicable and amended
D. Copyright Act, exclusively
Correct Answer: C. Money Laundering Prevention Act, 2012, as applicable and amended
Explanation: The Money Laundering Prevention Act, 2012, forms a central part of Bangladesh's AML framework. It must be read with applicable amendments, rules and related legislation.
50. Which institution is responsible for supervising stock-exchange operators and securities-market intermediaries within its mandate?
A. BSEC
B. Bangladesh Krishi Bank
C. Bangladesh Bank's currency-issuing function
D. Sonali Bank PLC
Correct Answer: A. BSEC
Explanation: BSEC regulates securities-market activities and relevant intermediaries under its statutory authority. Commercial banks and specialised banks do not replace the securities regulator.
51. What is the main purpose of bank prudential regulation?

C. Ensuring that banks lend only to government entities
D. Promoting safety, soundness and depositor protection through applicable requirements
Correct Answer: D. Promoting safety, soundness and depositor protection through applicable requirements
Explanation: Prudential regulation addresses financial strength, capital, liquidity and risk management. It reduces vulnerabilities but cannot guarantee that every bank will remain profitable or avoid failure.
52. Why are capital adequacy requirements imposed on banks?

Correct Answer: B. To provide a loss-absorbing financial buffer against risks

D. The Governor heads Bangladesh Bank and serves as its chief executive officer
Correct Answer: D. The Governor heads Bangladesh Bank and serves as its chief executive officer

64. Why is a bank's Board of Directors important?

B. It oversees strategy, risk governance and management accountability
C. It replaces the external auditor
D. It determines national fiscal policy
Correct Answer: B. It oversees strategy, risk governance and management accountability

65. Which responsibility is most appropriately assigned to a bank's senior management?

Correct Answer: C. Implementing approved strategy and managing day-to-day operations
Explanation: Senior management implements the bank's approved strategy and manages daily operations within delegated authority. The board retains its oversight responsibilities.
66. What is the primary role of a bank's internal audit function?

B. Sell investment products to every customer
C. Guarantee that no employee will ever commit fraud
D. Replace Bangladesh Bank's supervisory authority
Correct Answer: A. Independently assess internal controls, risk management and governance processes
Explanation: Internal audit evaluates the effectiveness of controls and governance arrangements. It provides assurance but cannot guarantee the complete absence of fraud or replace external supervision.
67. What is the purpose of segregation of duties in banking?

B. To allow one employee to initiate, approve and conceal every transaction
C. To eliminate the need for reconciliation
D. To reduce errors and fraud by separating incompatible responsibilities
Correct Answer: D. To reduce errors and fraud by separating incompatible responsibilities
Explanation: Segregation of duties separates activities such as transaction initiation, approval and reconciliation. This reduces opportunities for unauthorised activity and concealment.
68. Which practice best supports sound credit governance?

C. Ignoring connected-party lending risks
D. Allowing borrowers to set their own risk classifications
Correct Answer: B. Using documented appraisal, appropriate approval authority and ongoing monitoring
Explanation: Sound credit governance requires proper assessment, documentation, authorised approval and monitoring. Verbal recommendations alone are insufficient substitutes for the bank's credit process.
69. A bank director has a personal financial interest in a proposed transaction. What is the most appropriate governance response?

B. Approve the transaction without documentation
C. Disclose and manage the conflict in accordance with applicable law and policy
D. Transfer the transaction to an unrecorded account
Correct Answer: C. Disclose and manage the conflict in accordance with applicable law and policy
Explanation: Disclosure and appropriate management of conflicts help protect the integrity of decision-making. The exact restrictions and procedures depend on the applicable law and governance rules.
70. What is the purpose of an independent external audit of a bank's financial statements?
A. To provide an independent opinion on the financial statements under the applicable auditing framework
B. To guarantee that the bank will never fail
C. To determine the national policy rate
D. To approve every new customer account
Correct Answer: A. To provide an independent opinion on the financial statements under the applicable auditing framework
Explanation: External auditors express an opinion on financial statements under the relevant auditing framework. An audit is not a guarantee against every fraud, error or future financial failure.
71. Why should a bank maintain an effective risk-management framework?
A. To eliminate the need for capital
B. To avoid documenting credit decisions
C. To ensure every investment produces a profit
D. To identify, assess, monitor and control material risks
Correct Answer: D. To identify, assess, monitor and control material risks
Explanation: Banks face credit, market, liquidity, operational and other risks. An effective framework helps identify and mitigate these risks rather than assuming they can be eliminated completely.
72. Which example best illustrates operational risk?

B. A payment-processing system fails because of an internal systems error
C. A bank's government bond increases in value
D. A customer deposits money lawfully
Correct Answer: B. A payment-processing system fails because of an internal systems error
Explanation: Operational risk arises from failed or inadequate processes, people, systems or external events. A systems failure disrupting payments is a typical example.
73. Which example best illustrates credit risk?
A. A bank's printer runs out of paper
B. A power failure temporarily interrupts office lighting
C. A borrower defaults on a loan
D. A bank changes its logo
Correct Answer: C. A borrower defaults on a loan
Explanation: Credit risk is the risk of financial loss when a borrower or counterparty fails to meet its obligations.
74. What is liquidity risk for a bank?

92. What is the main difference between a bank deposit and a share investment?
