Batch 1: Questions 1–50
Inflation and monetary-policy fundamentals
1. What is the primary objective of contractionary monetary policy when inflation is persistently high?
A. Increase aggregate demand rapidly
B. Reduce inflationary pressure
C. Increase government expenditure
D. Eliminate all unemployment
Correct Answer: B. Reduce inflationary pressure
Explanation: Contractionary monetary policy aims to moderate aggregate demand and reduce inflationary pressure. A central bank may raise policy rates, tighten liquidity or use other restrictive instruments. It cannot eliminate all unemployment or directly control every component of government expenditure.
2. Which institution is responsible for conducting monetary policy in Bangladesh?
A. Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission
B. Ministry of Commerce
C. Bangladesh Bank
D. National Board of Revenue
Correct Answer: C. Bangladesh Bank
Explanation: Bangladesh Bank is the central bank of Bangladesh. It conducts monetary policy, manages official foreign-exchange reserves, oversees monetary and payment-system operations, and performs regulatory and supervisory functions under its legal mandate.
3. Demand-pull inflation primarily occurs when—
A. Aggregate demand exceeds the economy's productive capacity
B. Production costs decline sharply
C. Aggregate demand falls below aggregate supply
D. The money supply remains unchanged under all conditions
Correct Answer: A. Aggregate demand exceeds the economy's productive capacity
Explanation: Demand-pull inflation occurs when aggregate demand grows faster than the economy's ability to supply goods and services. Strong consumption, investment or government spending can contribute to this pressure. Falling production costs generally reduce cost pressure rather than create it.
4. Cost-push inflation may result from—
A. A decline in energy prices
B. A fall in production wages
C. A sharp rise in productivity
D. An increase in fuel and transportation costs
Correct Answer: D. An increase in fuel and transportation costs
Explanation: Higher fuel and transportation costs increase production and distribution expenses. Businesses may pass these additional costs to consumers, raising the general price level. The effect depends on competition, demand, exchange rates and the extent to which firms absorb the higher costs.
5. The Consumer Price Index (CPI) primarily measures—
A. The stock-market value of listed companies
B. Changes in the prices of a representative basket of consumer goods and services
C. Total government borrowing
D. The quantity of currency notes issued
Correct Answer: B. Changes in the prices of a representative basket of consumer goods and services
Explanation: CPI measures changes in consumer prices over time using a representative basket of goods and services. CPI inflation is commonly calculated as the percentage change in the index over a specified period. It is different from stock-market movements, government borrowing and currency issuance.
6. Disinflation means—
A. A sustained decline in the general price level
B. An increase in the rate of inflation
C. A decline in the rate of inflation while prices may still rise
D. The complete elimination of money
Correct Answer: C. A decline in the rate of inflation while prices may still rise
Explanation: Disinflation occurs when inflation slows. For example, if annual inflation falls from 10% to 7%, prices are still rising, but at a slower rate. A sustained decline in the general price level is called deflation.
7. Deflation refers to—
A. A persistent fall in the general price level
B. A persistent rise in the general price level
C. A temporary increase in one commodity's price
D. A reduction in the fiscal deficit only
Correct Answer: A. A persistent fall in the general price level
Explanation: Deflation means a sustained decline in the overall price level. It differs from disinflation, which means a reduction in the rate of inflation. Deflation can increase the real burden of fixed nominal debt and may discourage spending if consumers expect prices to fall further.
8. If nominal interest is 10% and expected inflation is 7%, the approximate expected real interest rate is—
A. 17%
B. 10%
C. −3%
D. 3%
Correct Answer: D. 3%
Explanation: The approximate Fisher relationship is:
Expected real interest rate = Nominal interest rate − Expected inflation
Therefore, \(10\%-7\%=\boxed{3\%}\). The exact Fisher calculation produces approximately 2.80%, but the standard approximation gives 3%.
9. Under the Fisher relationship, the nominal interest rate is approximately equal to—
A. The real interest rate minus expected inflation
B. The real interest rate plus expected inflation
C. Inflation multiplied by the money supply
D. GDP growth minus unemployment
Correct Answer: B. The real interest rate plus expected inflation
Explanation: The approximate Fisher relationship is:
Nominal interest rate ≈ Real interest rate + Expected inflation.
For example, if the expected real return is 4% and expected inflation is 6%, the nominal interest rate is approximately 10%. The exact relationship is .
10. Which policy combination is most consistent with reducing demand-driven inflation?
A. Lower policy rates and expand liquidity
B. Reduce reserve requirements and buy securities
C. Raise policy rates and withdraw excess liquidity
D. Increase unrestricted central-bank lending
Correct Answer: C. Raise policy rates and withdraw excess liquidity
Explanation: Raising policy rates and absorbing excess liquidity are generally contractionary measures. They can increase borrowing costs, restrain credit growth and moderate aggregate demand. The impact on inflation depends on transmission lags, inflation expectations and the source of the price pressure.
11. Monetary-policy transmission describes—
A. The transmission of tax revenue to local governments
B. The conversion of foreign banknotes into domestic currency only
C. The physical transportation of banknotes
D. How policy decisions affect financial conditions and economic activity
Correct Answer: D. How policy decisions affect financial conditions and economic activity
Explanation: Monetary-policy transmission describes how central-bank decisions influence market interest rates, bank lending, exchange rates, asset prices, spending, output and inflation. These effects usually occur with a lag and vary with economic and financial conditions.
12. A central bank's policy rate primarily influences—
A. Short-term money-market interest rates and broader financing conditions
B. The statutory rate of VAT directly
C. The number of commercial banks automatically
D. The face value of all existing currency notes
Correct Answer: A. Short-term money-market interest rates and broader financing conditions
Explanation: The policy rate is a key monetary-policy instrument. Changes in it influence short-term money-market rates and can affect deposit rates, lending rates and investment decisions. It does not directly determine VAT rates or the face value of currency notes.
13. Which statement about monetary policy is most accurate?
A. It can eliminate every supply shock immediately
B. It affects economic activity through multiple channels and time lags
C. It has no relationship with credit conditions
D. It determines every commercial bank's lending rate exactly
Correct Answer: B. It affects economic activity through multiple channels and time lags
Explanation: Monetary policy works through interest rates, credit availability, exchange rates, expectations and asset prices. Its effects are not instantaneous, and commercial banks' lending rates also depend on funding costs, credit risk, competition and other factors.
14. When inflation expectations become entrenched, a central bank may need to—
A. Stop publishing economic data
B. Guarantee negative real interest rates
C. Maintain or tighten restrictive policy until inflation expectations are better anchored
D. Eliminate reserve requirements permanently
Correct Answer: C. Maintain or tighten restrictive policy until inflation expectations are better anchored
Explanation: Persistent inflation expectations can influence wage negotiations, business pricing and household spending. A credible monetary-policy stance can help bring expectations under control. The appropriate degree of tightening depends on inflation trends, economic conditions and the central bank's mandate.
15. Which is a potential limitation of monetary tightening?
A. It always raises output immediately
B. It guarantees lower unemployment in the short run
C. It eliminates all exchange-rate fluctuations
D. Its effects may be delayed and may weaken output and credit growth
Correct Answer: D. Its effects may be delayed and may weaken output and credit growth
Explanation: Monetary tightening can reduce inflationary pressure, but higher borrowing costs may also restrain consumption, investment and credit growth. Because monetary policy works with lags, policymakers must assess both its intended benefits and possible costs.
Money supply and monetary aggregates
16. Reserve money generally consists of—
A. Currency in circulation plus banks' reserves at the central bank
B. All shares and corporate bonds
C. Only foreign-exchange reserves
D. Only loans to private businesses
Correct Answer: A. Currency in circulation plus banks' reserves at the central bank
Explanation: Reserve money, also called the monetary base, generally comprises currency in circulation and commercial-bank reserve balances at the central bank. Its exact statistical presentation depends on the monetary survey's definitions. It is narrower than broad money.
17. Broad money is generally broader than narrow money because it includes—
A. Only physical banknotes
B. Only the central bank's gold holdings
C. Currency and qualifying bank deposits and other included monetary instruments
D. Only government tax receipts
Correct Answer: C. Currency and qualifying bank deposits and other included monetary instruments
Explanation: Broad money includes currency held outside the banking system and qualifying deposits and other instruments defined in the relevant monetary statistics. It is broader than narrow money because it includes additional forms of liquid financial assets.
18. In standard monetary terminology, M1 commonly includes—
A. Only long-term government bonds
B. Currency held by the public and qualifying transactions deposits
C. All fixed assets owned by banks
D. All foreign direct investment
Correct Answer: B. Currency held by the public and qualifying transactions deposits
Explanation: M1 is a narrow monetary aggregate that commonly includes currency held by the public and readily spendable transactions deposits. Exact definitions vary across countries and statistical frameworks. Long-term bonds, fixed assets and foreign direct investment are not normally components of M1.
19. If a customer deposits cash into a demand-deposit account, what normally happens immediately, ignoring other transactions?
A. Currency held by the public falls while bank deposits rise
B. Both currency held by the public and bank deposits rise by the same amount
C. Both automatically become zero
D. The central bank's foreign reserves necessarily rise
Correct Answer: A. Currency held by the public falls while bank deposits rise
Explanation: A cash deposit transfers money from currency held by the public into a bank deposit. The composition of narrow money changes, but the transaction does not by itself necessarily increase the total amount of broad money. It also does not automatically affect foreign-exchange reserves.
20. Which transaction can directly create a commercial bank deposit?
A. A bank's purchase of office furniture from its existing cash balance, with no deposit payment
B. A customer's withdrawal of cash from a deposit
C. A bank's approval of a loan that has not yet been disbursed or credited
D. A bank's disbursement of a loan by crediting the borrower's deposit account
Correct Answer: D. A bank's disbursement of a loan by crediting the borrower's deposit account
Explanation: When a bank disburses a loan by crediting a customer's account, it records a loan asset and a corresponding deposit liability. The deposit is created at that point. Mere approval of an undisbursed loan does not necessarily create a deposit.

A. Reserve ratio divided by the policy rate
B. Currency ratio multiplied by inflation
C. One divided by the required reserve ratio
D. GDP divided by the exchange rate
Correct Answer: C. One divided by the required reserve ratio

This model assumes, among other things, that banks lend all available excess reserves and that the public does not hold additional currency outside the model.
22. Under the simplest textbook assumptions, if the required reserve ratio is 10%, the maximum theoretical deposit multiplier is—
A. 0.1
B. 10
C. 5
D. 100
Correct Answer: B. 10
Explanation: The simple multiplier is . With a reserve ratio of 10%, , so \(m=1/0.10=\boxed{10}\). This is a theoretical maximum, not a guarantee that actual bank deposits will multiply by exactly ten times.
23. Why is the simple money multiplier not a reliable fixed predictor of actual broad-money growth?
A. Banks never hold reserves
B. All loans must be made in foreign currency
C. Cash has no monetary role
D. Actual lending depends on capital, liquidity, credit demand, risk and other behavioural factors
Correct Answer: D. Actual lending depends on capital, liquidity, credit demand, risk and other behavioural factors
Explanation: Actual bank lending is constrained by capital adequacy, liquidity, creditworthiness, risk appetite, regulation and demand for loans. Borrowers may also repay debt or hold cash. Consequently, the simple textbook multiplier does not provide a fixed mechanical prediction of broad-money growth.
24. An increase in a central bank's net foreign assets can, other things equal, increase reserve money when—
A. The corresponding domestic-currency impact is not fully sterilised
B. All foreign assets are automatically destroyed
C. Commercial-bank deposits are prohibited
D. Government expenditure becomes zero
Correct Answer: A. The corresponding domestic-currency impact is not fully sterilised
Explanation: When a central bank acquires foreign assets and pays domestic currency, reserve money may increase. Sterilisation offsets some or all of that domestic-liquidity effect through other operations, such as selling securities. The actual result depends on the transaction and other balance-sheet movements.
25. Sterilisation of a foreign-exchange intervention means—
A. Banning all foreign trade
B. Converting every foreign asset into gold
C. Offsetting the intervention's domestic-liquidity effect through other policy operations
D. Cancelling all commercial-bank loans
Correct Answer: C. Offsetting the intervention's domestic-liquidity effect through other policy operations
Explanation: Sterilisation means offsetting the effect of a foreign-exchange transaction on domestic monetary liquidity. For example, a central bank may purchase foreign currency and inject domestic currency, then sell domestic securities to absorb the injected liquidity.

29. A repo transaction generally involves—
A. An unsecured permanent grant to a commercial bank
B. A commercial bank issuing shares to the central bank
C. A sale of securities with an agreement to repurchase them later
D. A mandatory conversion of deposits into equity
Correct Answer: C. A sale of securities with an agreement to repurchase them later
Explanation: A repurchase agreement, or repo, involves a securities transaction with a contractual agreement to reverse the transaction later at an agreed price. In monetary operations, a central bank can use repo to provide short-term liquidity against eligible securities.
30. When Bangladesh Bank conducts a repo operation to inject liquidity, it generally—
A. Sells securities outright without any repurchase agreement
B. Provides funds against eligible securities under a repurchase agreement
C. Collects income tax from depositors
D. Cancels the borrowing bank's capital
Correct Answer: B. Provides funds against eligible securities under a repurchase agreement
Explanation: In a liquidity-injecting repo, the central bank provides funds against eligible securities under an agreement to reverse the securities transaction. This provides temporary liquidity to eligible counterparties. It is not an outright securities sale or a cancellation of bank capital.
31. A reverse-repo-type liquidity absorption operation generally aims to—
A. Increase bank lending immediately in all circumstances
B. Finance household consumption directly
C. Increase the fiscal deficit automatically
D. Absorb liquidity from the banking system against securities under the applicable arrangement
Correct Answer: D. Absorb liquidity from the banking system against securities under the applicable arrangement
Explanation: A reverse-repo-type operation generally withdraws liquidity from the banking system. The precise transaction structure and terminology depend on the central bank's operational framework. It is important to distinguish such operations from liquidity-injecting repos and outright securities purchases.
32. The Standing Deposit Facility primarily allows eligible counterparties to—

D. Exchange shares for banknotes
Correct Answer: A. Place funds with the central bank under specified terms
Explanation: The SDF provides eligible counterparties with a facility for placing funds at the central bank under prescribed conditions. It can help absorb surplus liquidity and support the lower boundary of the interest-rate corridor.
33. The Standing Lending Facility primarily provides—

Explanation: The SLF is a central-bank lending facility for eligible institutions. It can help them address short-term liquidity shortages, subject to the applicable eligibility, collateral and operational rules. It is not a retail lending product or a permanent capital injection.

41. CRR stands for—
A. Capital Recovery Ratio
B. Credit Risk Reserve
C. Cash Reserve Ratio

Explanation: CRR stands for Cash Reserve Ratio. It specifies the reserve requirement applicable to a bank under the relevant regulatory framework. The actual calculation must use the prescribed reservable liabilities and applicable rate.
42. SLR stands for—

Correct Answer: B. Statutory Liquidity Ratio
Explanation: SLR means Statutory Liquidity Ratio. It concerns the prescribed liquidity-asset requirement under applicable regulations. The eligible assets and liability base depend on the relevant rules and banking category.

50. To absorb excess banking-system liquidity through an outright open-market sale, a central bank would generally—

Batch 2: Questions 51–100
Monetary operations and liquidity management
51. When Bangladesh Bank purchases securities outright from a commercial bank, the immediate intended liquidity effect is generally to—
A. Reduce the purchasing bank's reserves
B. Increase its reserve balances, other things equal
C. Reduce the government's tax revenue automatically
D. Increase the CRR percentage automatically
Correct Answer: B. Increase its reserve balances, other things equal
Explanation: When Bangladesh Bank purchases securities and settles the transaction through the banking system, the seller generally receives funds that increase its reserve balance. This injects liquidity, assuming other transactions do not offset the effect.
52. Which best distinguishes an outright OMO purchase from a repo liquidity injection?
A. An outright purchase is always reversed the next day
B. A repo cannot involve securities
C. An outright purchase is a securities transaction without a contractual repurchase leg, whereas repo includes one
D. Both terms always mean exactly the same contract
Correct Answer: C. An outright purchase is a securities transaction without a contractual repurchase leg, whereas repo includes one
Explanation: An outright purchase transfers securities without a contractual obligation to reverse the transaction on a specified date. A repo involves a sale with an agreement to repurchase the securities later. Both can inject liquidity, but their contractual structures differ.
53. Which is the most direct purpose of routine liquidity-management operations by a central bank?
A. Fix every company's profit margin
B. Set the statutory income-tax rate
C. Guarantee a trade surplus
D. Steer money-market liquidity and short-term interest rates toward the policy stance
Correct Answer: D. Steer money-market liquidity and short-term interest rates toward the policy stance
Explanation: Central banks manage liquidity to keep short-term market conditions consistent with their monetary-policy objectives. These operations can reduce temporary liquidity imbalances and help guide money-market rates. They do not directly determine corporate profit margins, taxation or the trade balance.
54. A temporary shortage of bank reserves may cause—
A. Upward pressure on overnight funding rates, other things equal
B. Automatic elimination of inflation
C. A permanent rise in every bank's equity
D. A fall in all reserve requirements without a policy decision
Correct Answer: A. Upward pressure on overnight funding rates, other things equal
Explanation: When banks need reserves to meet settlement obligations and available liquidity is scarce, they may compete for overnight funding. This can raise interbank rates. The result depends on liquidity distribution, central-bank operations and access to standing facilities.
55. If a central bank raises CRR while the deposit base remains unchanged, the required cash reserves will generally—
A. Decline
B. Remain unchanged in every case
C. Increase
D. Become equal to the bank's share capital
Correct Answer: C. Increase
Explanation: Required reserves equal the applicable reservable base multiplied by the CRR. With the base unchanged, a higher CRR increases the required reserve amount. The effect on excess liquidity depends on how many eligible reserves the bank already holds.
56. A reduction in SLR may give a bank more flexibility to—
A. Ignore capital adequacy rules
B. Hold fewer qualifying liquid assets and allocate funds elsewhere, subject to other constraints
C. Avoid all credit risk
D. Stop maintaining accounting records
Correct Answer: B. Hold fewer qualifying liquid assets and allocate funds elsewhere, subject to other constraints
Explanation: A lower SLR can reduce the amount of assets a bank must maintain in qualifying liquid forms. This may create greater flexibility in allocating funds. However, capital requirements, liquidity-risk rules and creditworthiness constraints continue to apply.
57. Which statement about CRR and SLR is correct?
A. They are both identical measures of bank profitability
B. CRR is a tax on consumers, while SLR is a tax on exports
C. Neither has any possible effect on liquidity management
D. They are distinct regulatory reserve/liquidity requirements, with different definitions and eligible assets
Correct Answer: D. They are distinct regulatory reserve/liquidity requirements, with different definitions and eligible assets
Explanation: CRR concerns the prescribed cash-reserve requirement, while SLR concerns the prescribed liquidity-asset requirement under the relevant framework. Their calculation bases and eligible assets differ. Neither is a measure of bank profitability.
58. If the central bank sells securities and receives settlement funds from banks, the direct effect, other things equal, is to—
A. Drain reserve liquidity from the banking system
B. Increase reserve liquidity
C. Eliminate bank liabilities
D. Increase foreign reserves by definition
Correct Answer: A. Drain reserve liquidity from the banking system
Explanation: Banks pay for securities purchased from the central bank. Settlement transfers funds to the central bank and generally reduces the banks' reserve balances. This is a liquidity-absorbing operation, assuming other transactions do not offset the effect.
59. Which operation would normally inject liquidity into the banking system?
A. An outright sale of securities by the central bank
B. An increase in CRR
C. An outright purchase of securities by the central bank
D. A rise in the SDF rate alone, with no transaction
Correct Answer: C. An outright purchase of securities by the central bank
Explanation: An outright purchase generally injects liquidity because the central bank pays the seller through the settlement system. An outright sale generally absorbs liquidity. An increase in CRR tends to increase reserve requirements rather than inject liquidity.
60. What is the most appropriate interpretation of an increase in the weighted-average call money rate?
A. It proves that CPI inflation has fallen
B. It may indicate tighter conditions in the overnight interbank lending market
C. It proves that all banks have stopped lending
D. It automatically raises the SLR
Correct Answer: B. It may indicate tighter conditions in the overnight interbank lending market
Explanation: The weighted-average call money rate reflects the cost of short-term interbank borrowing under the applicable calculation method. An increase may indicate tighter overnight liquidity or stronger demand for funds, but it does not by itself prove that inflation has fallen or that all banks have stopped lending.
Monetary aggregates and credit

A. Net foreign assets and net domestic assets

62. If broad money grows rapidly while real output growth remains weak, a possible macroeconomic risk is—
A. Guaranteed deflation

131. What is the zero-lower-bound problem in conventional monetary policy?

Explanation: The zero-lower-bound problem arises when nominal interest rates are so low that conventional further reductions become difficult or less effective. Some central banks have used negative policy rates, so zero is not an absolute legal or technical boundary in every monetary system.

191. Which policy action is most likely to be contractionary, other things equal?
A. An outright purchase of securities
B. An increase in the policy repo rate
C. A reduction in CRR
D. A liquidity injection through repo
Correct Answer: B. An increase in the policy repo rate
Explanation: Raising the policy repo rate generally increases borrowing costs and tightens financial conditions. By contrast, outright securities purchases, CRR reductions and liquidity-injecting repos generally ease liquidity conditions, other things equal.
192. Which action is most likely to inject liquidity, other things equal?
A. An outright sale of securities
B. An increase in CRR
C. An outright purchase of securities by the central bank
D. A liquidity-absorbing operation
Correct Answer: C. An outright purchase of securities by the central bank
Explanation: When the central bank purchases securities, it generally pays the seller through the settlement system, increasing reserve liquidity. Securities sales and liquidity-absorbing operations generally have the opposite effect.
193. A central bank sells foreign currency and buys domestic currency in the foreign-exchange market. Without sterilisation, the domestic-liquidity effect is generally—
A. Contractionary, because domestic currency is absorbed
B. Expansionary in every case
C. Unrelated to settlement flows
D. Guaranteed to increase broad money by a fixed multiplier
Correct Answer: A. Contractionary, because domestic currency is absorbed
Explanation: When the central bank sells foreign currency in exchange for domestic currency, domestic currency is generally absorbed from the banking system. This tends to reduce domestic liquidity unless the effect is offset by other operations. The impact on broad money is not fixed.
194. If the central bank buys foreign currency and pays for it in domestic currency, without sterilisation, the immediate domestic-liquidity effect is generally—
A. A decline in domestic-currency reserve balances
B. No balance-sheet effect
C. A compulsory increase in CRR
D. An increase in domestic-currency liquidity
Correct Answer: D. An increase in domestic-currency liquidity
Explanation: The central bank pays domestic currency when it purchases foreign currency. This generally increases domestic-currency liquidity in the banking system. Sterilisation through other operations can offset some or all of the injection.
195. Which combination best describes a credible disinflation strategy?
A. Ignoring inflation expectations and changing rates randomly
B. A coherent policy stance, credible communication, appropriate liquidity management and monitoring of incoming data
C. Guaranteeing that prices never change
D. Using foreign reserves as the only policy instrument under every circumstance
Correct Answer: B. A coherent policy stance, credible communication, appropriate liquidity management and monitoring of incoming data
Explanation: Disinflation is more likely to be sustained when monetary-policy decisions, communication and liquidity management are consistent with the inflation objective. Policymakers must monitor incoming data and adjust their stance as conditions change. No single instrument guarantees that prices will stop rising.
196. Why might a central bank refrain from cutting rates even when growth is weak?
A. Growth has no relevance to monetary policy
B. All rate cuts are prohibited by definition
C. Inflation risks or unanchored expectations may make premature easing costly
D. A rate cut always causes immediate deflation
Correct Answer: C. Inflation risks or unanchored expectations may make premature easing costly
Explanation: Weak growth may support the case for easing, but high inflation or poorly anchored expectations can make a rate cut premature. Policymakers must weigh output conditions against inflation risks and the consequences of weakening policy credibility.
197. Which is the strongest reason to distinguish gross reserves from usable or net reserve measures?
A. Different measures answer different questions about assets, liabilities and availability for intervention or payments
B. Gross reserves never change
C. Usable reserves are always greater than gross reserves
D. Net reserves are identical to tax revenue
Correct Answer: A. Different measures answer different questions about assets, liabilities and availability for intervention or payments
Explanation: Gross reserves measure a specified stock of reserve assets, while net or usable measures may account for relevant liabilities, restrictions or other adjustments. The exact definitions depend on the methodology. This distinction is important when assessing external-payment capacity and reserve adequacy.
198. A bank's loans increase, but deposits do not rise by the same amount during the observation period. Which explanation is plausible?
A. All bank lending must be fictional
B. The accounting identity has ceased to operate
C. Every loan must be financed only with new currency notes
D. Loan proceeds may be transferred to other banks, converted to cash or offset by other balance-sheet movements
Correct Answer: D. Loan proceeds may be transferred to other banks, converted to cash or offset by other balance-sheet movements
Explanation: A bank loan may initially create a deposit when credited to the borrower's account. Subsequently, the borrower may transfer funds to another bank, withdraw cash or make payments that change the bank's balance sheet. Other transactions can also offset deposit growth during the observation period.
199. Which statement about the central bank's control over money supply is most accurate?
A. It determines broad money with perfect precision every day
B. It influences monetary conditions, but broad-money outcomes also depend on banks, borrowers, government transactions and external flows
C. It has no influence on money-market liquidity
D. It directly determines every consumer's spending
Correct Answer: B. It influences monetary conditions, but broad-money outcomes also depend on banks, borrowers, government transactions and external flows
Explanation: The central bank influences monetary conditions through policy rates, reserve requirements, liquidity operations and other instruments. However, broad-money outcomes also depend on bank lending, borrower behaviour, government transactions and external flows. Its influence does not amount to perfect daily control over every monetary aggregate.
