Batch 1 — Questions 1–50
Section A: Core banking terminology and full forms
1. What does KYC stand for in banking?
A. Keep Your Cash B. Know Your Customer C. Key Yield Calculation D. Know Your Credit
Correct Answer: B. Know Your Customer
Explanation: KYC is the process of identifying and verifying customers and understanding the nature of their banking relationships. It supports customer due diligence and AML/CFT compliance.
2. What is the full form of AML?
A. Automated Money Lending B. Annual Monetary Limit C. Anti-Money Laundering D. Asset Management Liability
Correct Answer: C. Anti-Money Laundering
Explanation: AML refers to laws, regulations, and controls intended to prevent criminals from disguising the origin of illegally obtained funds.
3. What does CFT mean?
A. Combating the Financing of Terrorism B. Central Finance Transaction C. Credit Fund Transfer D. Cash Flow Tracking
Correct Answer: A. Combating the Financing of Terrorism
Explanation: CFT measures prevent funds or financial services from being used to support terrorism. AML and CFT are closely related but address distinct threats.
4. What is the full form of NPL in banking?
A. Net Profit Liability B. National Payment Limit C. Non-Personal Loan D. Non-Performing Loan
Correct Answer: D. Non-Performing Loan
Explanation: An NPL is a loan classified as non-performing under the applicable regulatory framework. Classification criteria and applicable thresholds depend on the relevant rules.
5. What does CAR stand for?
A. Cash Allocation Ratio B. Capital Adequacy Ratio C. Credit Assessment Return D. Current Asset Requirement
Correct Answer: B. Capital Adequacy Ratio
Explanation: CAR measures regulatory capital relative to risk-weighted assets. It indicates a bank's capacity to absorb losses against its risk exposures.
6. What is the full form of CAMELS?
A. Capital, Assets, Management, Earnings, Liquidity, Sensitivity B. Capital, Accounts, Money, Equity, Lending, Solvency C. Credit, Assets, Market, Earnings, Liabilities, Security D. Capital, Audit, Management, Equity, Liquidity, Settlement
Correct Answer: A. Capital, Assets, Management, Earnings, Liquidity, Sensitivity
Explanation: CAMELS is a supervisory framework for assessing a financial institution's condition. The six components cover capital adequacy, asset quality, management, earnings, liquidity, and sensitivity to market risk.
7. What does NIM stand for?
A. National Investment Margin B. Net Insurance Measure C. Net Interest Margin D. Nominal Income Multiplier
Correct Answer: C. Net Interest Margin
Explanation: NIM generally measures net interest income relative to average interest-earning assets. It helps assess the profitability of a bank's interest-generating activities.
8. What is the full form of CASA?
A. Capital and Securities Account B. Current and Savings Account C. Credit and Settlement Arrangement D. Cash Allocation and Savings Authority
Correct Answer: B. Current and Savings Account
Explanation: CASA refers to current and savings deposits, commonly considered together when analysing a bank's deposit mix and funding costs.
9. What does LCR stand for under Basel liquidity standards?
A. Loan Classification Requirement B. Liability Coverage Return C. Lending Capital Ratio D. Liquidity Coverage Ratio
Correct Answer: D. Liquidity Coverage Ratio
Explanation: LCR assesses whether a bank holds sufficient high-quality liquid assets to cover its projected net cash outflows over a 30-calendar-day stress scenario under the Basel framework.
10. What is the full form of NSFR?
A. Net Settlement Funding Return B. National Savings Finance Ratio C. Net Stable Funding Ratio D. Non-Standard Financial Requirement
Correct Answer: C. Net Stable Funding Ratio
Explanation: NSFR promotes stable funding over a one-year horizon relative to the liquidity characteristics of assets and off-balance-sheet exposures.
11. What does ROA mean?
A. Return on Assets B. Rate of Allocation C. Return on Advances D. Reserve of Accounts
Correct Answer: A. Return on Assets
Explanation: ROA measures profitability relative to assets, commonly calculated as net profit divided by average total assets. The precise numerator and averaging convention should be stated when comparing published ratios.
12. What is the full form of ROE?
A. Rate of Equity B. Return on Earnings C. Reserve of Equity D. Return on Equity
Correct Answer: D. Return on Equity
Explanation: ROE measures profit relative to shareholders' equity. It should be interpreted alongside leverage and risk because a higher ROE does not automatically mean a safer or better-capitalised bank.
13. What does CIB stand for in Bangladesh banking?
A. Central Investment Board B. Credit Information Bureau C. Commercial Insurance Bureau D. Capital Issuance Board
Correct Answer: B. Credit Information Bureau
Explanation: Bangladesh Bank's Credit Information Bureau maintains and provides credit-related information used by reporting institutions to assess borrowers and credit exposure.
14. What is the full form of BFIU?
A. Bangladesh Financial Institutions Union B. Banking Finance Investigation Unit C. Bangladesh Financial Intelligence Unit D. Bangladesh Fiscal Inspection Unit
Correct Answer: C. Bangladesh Financial Intelligence Unit
Explanation: BFIU is Bangladesh's financial intelligence unit. It receives and analyses relevant financial intelligence and supports action against money laundering and terrorist financing.
15. What does STR stand for in AML compliance?
A. Suspicious Transaction Report B. Standard Transfer Receipt C. Secured Trade Return D. Settlement Tracking Record
Correct Answer: A. Suspicious Transaction Report
Explanation: An STR concerns transactions suspected of being connected to illicit activity. Suspicion, rather than simply the transaction amount, is central to the reporting concept.
16. What does PEP mean in customer due diligence?
A. Protected Equity Portfolio B. Public Exchange Participant C. Personal Earnings Profile D. Politically Exposed Person
Correct Answer: D. Politically Exposed Person
Explanation: A PEP is a person entrusted with a prominent public function. PEP relationships can require enhanced scrutiny under applicable AML/CFT rules; PEP status alone does not establish criminal conduct.
17. What is the full form of EDD?
A. Electronic Deposit Data B. Enhanced Due Diligence C. External Debt Declaration D. Equity Development Division
Correct Answer: B. Enhanced Due Diligence
Explanation: EDD involves additional checks and scrutiny where a customer, transaction, or relationship presents elevated money-laundering or terrorist-financing risk.
18. What does UBO mean?
A. Universal Banking Operation B. Unsecured Borrowing Obligation C. Ultimate Beneficial Owner D. Unified Bank Organisation
Correct Answer: C. Ultimate Beneficial Owner
Explanation: A UBO is the natural person who ultimately owns or controls a customer or on whose behalf a transaction is conducted, according to the applicable legal definition.
19. What does SME stand for?
A. Small and Medium Enterprise B. Secured Monetary Exchange C. Standard Management Entity D. Savings and Mortgage Establishment
Correct Answer: A. Small and Medium Enterprise
Explanation: SMEs are small and medium-sized businesses. Bangladesh banking and policy documents may use CMSME, adding cottage and micro enterprises; the applicable classification criteria matter.
20. What is the full form of FDI?
A. Financial Deposit Insurance B. Fixed Debt Instrument C. Foreign Deposit Income D. Foreign Direct Investment
Correct Answer: D. Foreign Direct Investment
Explanation: FDI involves cross-border investment associated with a lasting interest and significant influence in an enterprise, rather than merely purchasing a short-term financial asset.
Section B: Bangladesh Bank and monetary policy
21. Which legal instrument established Bangladesh Bank?
A. Bank Company Act, 1991 B. Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972 C. Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 D. Financial Institutions Act, 1993
Correct Answer: B. Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972
Explanation: The Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972, is the foundational legal instrument establishing the central bank. The Bank Company Act, 1991, separately governs important aspects of banking companies.
22. As of 29 October 2026, what is Bangladesh Bank's policy repo rate under its October–December 2026 monetary policy statement?
A. 7.50% B. 8.00% C. 9.50% D. 11.00%
Correct Answer: C. 9.50%
Explanation: Bangladesh Bank retained the policy repo rate at 9.50% for October–December 2026. This is a dated official policy rate, not a timeless fact. Source: Bangladesh Bank, Quarterly Monetary Policy Statement, 30 September 2026.
23. What is the Standing Lending Facility (SLF) rate in Bangladesh Bank's October–December 2026 policy statement?
A. 11.00% B. 9.50% C. 7.50% D. 4.00%
Correct Answer: A. 11.00%
Explanation: SLF is a standing facility through which eligible banks can obtain liquidity from the central bank subject to the applicable terms. The published SLF rate is 11.00%.
24. What is the Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) rate under the same monetary policy statement?
A. 11.00% B. 9.50% C. 4.00% D. 7.50%
Correct Answer: D. 7.50%
Explanation: SDF allows eligible banks to place funds with the central bank under the facility's terms. The October–December 2026 statement sets the SDF rate at 7.50%.
25. Which option correctly orders Bangladesh Bank's policy repo, SLF, and SDF rates for October–December 2026?
A. Repo 7.50%, SLF 9.50%, SDF 11.00% B. Repo 9.50%, SLF 11.00%, SDF 7.50% C. Repo 11.00%, SLF 7.50%, SDF 9.50% D. Repo 9.50%, SLF 7.50%, SDF 11.00%
Correct Answer: B. Repo 9.50%, SLF 11.00%, SDF 7.50%
Explanation: The policy repo is the benchmark rate, SLF is the lending-facility rate, and SDF is the deposit-facility rate. Memorise the three rates together and attach the October–December 2026 date.
26. Under Bangladesh Bank's March 2025 CRR circular, what is the minimum daily CRR requirement cited for scheduled banks' demand and time liabilities?
A. 2.00% B. 4.00% C. 3.00% D. 5.00%
Correct Answer: C. 3.00%
Explanation: Bangladesh Bank reduced the minimum daily CRR requirement from 3.50% to 3.00%. This must not be confused with the separate biweekly average requirement. Source: MPD Circular No. 01, 4 March 2025.
27. What is the biweekly average CRR requirement stated in Bangladesh Bank's March 2025 circular?
A. 4.00% B. 3.00% C. 2.50% D. 5.00%
Correct Answer: A. 4.00%
Explanation: The biweekly average CRR remained 4.00%, even after the minimum daily requirement was reduced to 3.00%. An exam question may test this distinction directly.
28. What does CRR stand for?
A. Credit Recovery Rate B. Capital Reserve Return C. Cash Repayment Requirement D. Cash Reserve Ratio
Correct Answer: D. Cash Reserve Ratio
Explanation: CRR refers to the required cash reserve maintained by a bank under central-bank rules, calculated using the applicable reserve base and averaging or daily-maintenance conditions.
29. What does SLR stand for?
A. Secured Lending Rate B. Statutory Liquidity Ratio C. Standard Liability Return D. Settlement Liquidity Reserve
Correct Answer: B. Statutory Liquidity Ratio
Explanation: SLR is a statutory liquidity requirement. Its permitted assets, calculation base, and applicable percentage depend on the relevant regulatory framework. Do not infer its current rate from the policy repo rate.
30. What is the principal purpose of an open-market operation (OMO)?
A. To set every commercial bank's deposit rate directly B. To audit borrowers' financial statements C. To influence liquidity and monetary conditions through market transactions D. To guarantee bank deposits against all losses
Correct Answer: C. To influence liquidity and monetary conditions through market transactions
Explanation: A central bank can use purchases and sales of eligible securities to influence banking-system liquidity and monetary conditions. Purchases generally inject liquidity, while sales generally absorb it, all else equal.
31. What is a repo transaction in conventional central-bank operations?
A. A sale of securities with an agreement to repurchase them later B. A permanent transfer of bank ownership C. An unsecured personal loan D. A compulsory deposit by a customer
Correct Answer: A. A sale of securities with an agreement to repurchase them later
Explanation: A repo is structured as a securities sale with a commitment to repurchase at an agreed date and price. Economically, it commonly functions as secured short-term funding.
32. What does MPC stand for in monetary policy?
A. Monetary Payments Council B. Market Pricing Commission C. Money Protection Code D. Monetary Policy Committee
Correct Answer: D. Monetary Policy Committee
Explanation: An MPC is the committee responsible for monetary-policy deliberation and decisions within its institutional mandate. Bangladesh Bank's September 2026 policy decision was taken by its MPC.
33. What is the primary objective of contractionary monetary policy when inflation is persistently high?
A. To maximise consumer borrowing regardless of inflation B. To reduce inflationary pressure by tightening monetary conditions C. To eliminate all taxation D. To guarantee a rise in every asset price
Correct Answer: B. To reduce inflationary pressure by tightening monetary conditions
Explanation: Tighter monetary conditions can raise financing costs and restrain credit demand and aggregate demand, helping reduce inflationary pressure. The effect depends on transmission, expectations, supply conditions, and other economic factors.

34. What is the usual meaning of basis point (bp) in interest-rate reporting?
A. One percentage point B. One-tenth of one percent C. One-hundredth of one percentage point D. Ten percentage points
Correct Answer: C. One-hundredth of one percentage point
Explanation: One basis point equals 0.01 percentage point. Thus, a 50-basis-point reduction from 10.00% results in a rate of 9.50%.
35. Bangladesh Bank's policy repo rate fell from 10.00% to 9.50%. What was the reduction in basis points?
A. 50 basis points B. 5 basis points C. 100 basis points D. 150 basis points
Correct Answer: A. 50 basis points
Explanation: The change is 10.00% − 9.50% = 0.50 percentage point. Since each basis point is 0.01 percentage point, the reduction equals 50 basis points. Bangladesh Bank reported this reduction effective 2 August 2026.
36. What does OMO stand for?
A. Official Money Reserve B. Open Market Operations C. Overnight Mortgage Obligation D. Outstanding Monetary Output
Correct Answer: B. Open Market Operations
Explanation: OMO is a standard central-bank tool for influencing liquidity and monetary conditions through transactions in securities or other eligible instruments.
37. What does MPS stand for in Bangladesh Bank publications?
A. Money Protection Scheme B. Market Performance Survey C. Monetary Payment System D. Monetary Policy Statement
Correct Answer: D. Monetary Policy Statement
Explanation: The MPS explains the central bank's monetary-policy stance, assessment of economic conditions, and relevant policy decisions. Bangladesh Bank published its October–December 2026 quarterly MPS on 30 September 2026.
38. What is the most direct distinction between a policy repo rate and a commercial bank's loan rate?
A. They are legally required to be identical B. The loan rate is always below the repo rate C. The repo rate is a central-bank policy rate, while the loan rate is charged by a lender to a borrower D. The repo rate applies only to household deposits
Correct Answer: C. The repo rate is a central-bank policy rate, while the loan rate is charged by a lender to a borrower
Explanation: Commercial lending rates depend on funding costs, credit risk, operating costs, market conditions, and applicable regulation. Policy-rate changes may influence them but do not make every loan rate identical to the repo rate.
39. Which institution publishes Bangladesh's official monetary policy statements?
A. Bangladesh Bank B. Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission C. National Board of Revenue D. Dhaka Stock Exchange
Correct Answer: A. Bangladesh Bank
Explanation: Bangladesh Bank publishes monetary-policy statements through its official website and communicates the central bank's policy stance and related decisions.
40. What does DOMMR stand for in Bangladesh Bank's money-market reference-rate reporting?
A. Deposit Operations and Money Management Return B. Domestic Official Monetary Market Requirement C. Daily Overnight Monetary Market Reserve D. Dhaka Overnight Money Market Rate
Correct Answer: D. Dhaka Overnight Money Market Rate
Explanation: DOMMR is a money-market reference rate published by Bangladesh Bank. It is distinct from the policy repo rate and from the Bangladesh Overnight Financing Rate (BOFR).
Section C: Payment systems and digital banking
41. What does BEFTN stand for?
A. Bangladesh Electronic Finance Transfer Network B. Bangladesh Electronic Funds Transfer Network C. Bank Exchange and Fund Transaction Network D. Bangladesh External Financial Transfer Network
Correct Answer: B. Bangladesh Electronic Funds Transfer Network
Explanation: BEFTN facilitates electronic credit and debit transfers between participating banks. It is designed for batch-based electronic payments rather than the real-time gross settlement of each transaction.
42. What is the full form of BACPS?
A. Bangladesh Automated Clearing Payment Service B. Banking Automated Cash Processing System C. Bangladesh Automated Cheque Processing System D. Bangladesh Account Clearing and Payment Standard
Correct Answer: C. Bangladesh Automated Cheque Processing System
Explanation: BACPS automates cheque clearing through image-based processing. It reduces dependence on the physical transportation and manual processing of cheques.
43. What does BD-RTGS stand for?
A. Bangladesh Real Time Gross Settlement B. Bangladesh Retail Transfer Guarantee System C. Bank Deposit and Remittance Tracking Gateway D. Bangladesh Regional Trade Guarantee Service
Correct Answer: A. Bangladesh Real Time Gross Settlement
Explanation: RTGS settles eligible transactions individually and in real time, subject to system rules and liquidity availability. Unlike net settlement, it does not wait to offset all payments in a batch before settling them.
44. What is the full form of NPSB?
A. National Payment Settlement Board B. Network Processing Service of Bangladesh C. National Public Savings Bank D. National Payment Switch Bangladesh
Correct Answer: D. National Payment Switch Bangladesh
Explanation: NPSB provides interoperability among participating financial institutions and payment channels, including supported card and electronic payment transactions.
45. What does MFS stand for?
A. Monetary Funding Service B. Mobile Financial Services C. Managed Financial Settlement D. Merchant Finance System
Correct Answer: B. Mobile Financial Services
Explanation: MFS enables financial transactions through mobile-based channels. In Bangladesh, mobile financial services support activities such as transfers, cash-in, cash-out, and merchant payments, subject to provider permissions and applicable rules.
46. What is the full form of QR in QR-code payments?
A. Quick Registration B. Quality Recognition C. Quick Response D. Query Resolution
Correct Answer: C. Quick Response
Explanation: A QR code encodes information that a compatible device or payment application can read. Bangla QR is Bangladesh's national QR-code standard for interoperable merchant payments.
47. What does POS mean in card payments?
A. Point of Sale B. Payment Operating Standard C. Personal Online Settlement D. Proof of Savings
Correct Answer: A. Point of Sale
Explanation: A POS terminal or system allows merchants to initiate and process payment transactions at the point where goods or services are purchased.
48. What is the full form of ATM?
A. Automated Transfer Mechanism B. Account Transaction Machine C. Automatic Teller Management D. Automated Teller Machine
Correct Answer: D. Automated Teller Machine
Explanation: An ATM provides self-service banking functions, typically including cash withdrawals and balance enquiries, with available services depending on the institution and network.
49. What does PIN stand for in payment-card security?
A. Personal Internet Number B. Personal Identification Number C. Payment Information Network D. Protected Institution Name
Correct Answer: B. Personal Identification Number
Explanation: A PIN is a secret numeric credential used to authenticate certain transactions. It should not be disclosed to callers, messages, or unverified websites.
50. What is the full form of OTP?
A. Online Transaction Procedure B. Official Transfer Password C. One-Time Password D. Open Transfer Protocol
Correct Answer: C. One-Time Password
Explanation: An OTP is a temporary authentication code intended for a particular authentication event or limited period. Criminals may use social engineering to persuade customers to disclose OTPs.
51. What does POS-to-POS interoperability principally enable?
A. Supported payment acceptance across compatible systems or institutions B. Automatic approval of every credit application C. Elimination of all transaction fees D. Guaranteed reversal of every disputed transaction
Correct Answer: A. Supported payment acceptance across compatible systems or institutions
Explanation: Interoperability allows compatible payment systems to communicate and process transactions across participating institutions. It does not guarantee that every transaction will be approved or fee-free.
52. What is the principal difference between clearing and settlement?
A. Clearing always involves foreign currency, while settlement involves domestic currency B. Clearing applies only to cheques, while settlement applies only to cards C. The two terms always mean exactly the same thing D. Clearing determines, reconciles, or prepares obligations; settlement discharges those obligations
Correct Answer: D. Clearing determines, reconciles, or prepares obligations; settlement discharges those obligations
Explanation: Clearing establishes the obligations arising from payment instructions. Settlement is the actual discharge of the resulting payment obligations, using the relevant settlement assets and system arrangements.
53. What does EFT stand for?
A. Equity Financing Transfer B. Electronic Funds Transfer C. External Finance Transaction D. Estimated Funding Target
Correct Answer: B. Electronic Funds Transfer
Explanation: EFT is the electronic movement of money between accounts or institutions. It is a broad term covering multiple payment methods and system designs.
54. What does P2P mean in digital payments?
A. Payment-to-Provider only B. Public-to-Private C. Person-to-Person D. Portfolio-to-Portfolio
Correct Answer: C. Person-to-Person
Explanation: P2P payments transfer funds between individuals. The term describes the payment relationship rather than a single technical network.
55. What is the full form of API in financial technology?
A. Application Programming Interface B. Automated Payment Institution C. Account Protection Index D. Advanced Processing Instrument
Correct Answer: A. Application Programming Interface
Explanation: An API defines how software systems communicate. Banking APIs can support authorised services such as account information retrieval or payment initiation under applicable security and consent arrangements.
Section D: Credit, risk, and bank supervision
56. What does CIB stand for in Bangladesh Bank's credit-information context?
A. Capital Investment Bureau B. Central Insurance Board C. Commercial Information Base D. Credit Information Bureau
Correct Answer: D. Credit Information Bureau
Explanation: CIB information supports credit-risk assessment by providing relevant borrowing and credit-exposure information. It helps lenders evaluate applicants alongside other appraisal evidence.
57. What does NPL stand for?
A. National Payment Liability B. Non-Performing Loan C. Net Profit Lending D. Non-Personal Liability
Correct Answer: B. Non-Performing Loan
Explanation: An NPL is a credit exposure classified as non-performing under the applicable rules. NPL ratios are indicators of asset quality, but classification definitions and reporting dates must be checked.
58. What does ECL mean under expected-credit-loss accounting?
A. Estimated Capital Leverage B. Effective Credit Limit C. Expected Credit Loss D. External Cash Liability
Correct Answer: C. Expected Credit Loss
Explanation: ECL estimates credit losses using forward-looking information and relevant probability-weighted scenarios under the applicable accounting standard. It differs from recognising losses only after a default has occurred.
59. What is the full form of LGD in credit-risk measurement?
A. Loss Given Default B. Lending Growth Distribution C. Liability Guarantee Document D. Loan Grading Decision
Correct Answer: A. Loss Given Default
Explanation: LGD estimates the proportion of an exposure expected to be lost if default occurs, after considering recoveries, collateral proceeds, and relevant costs.
60. What does PD mean in credit-risk modelling?
A. Provisioning Date B. Payment Duration C. Portfolio Diversification D. Probability of Default
Correct Answer: D. Probability of Default
Explanation: PD estimates the likelihood that a borrower or exposure will default over a defined horizon. It is a key input in many credit-risk models.
61. What does EAD stand for in credit-risk management?
A. Expected Annual Deposit B. Exposure at Default C. Equity Allocation Decision D. External Asset Declaration
Correct Answer: B. Exposure at Default
Explanation: EAD estimates the amount exposed when default occurs. It can include drawn balances and, where applicable, expected utilisation of undrawn commitments.
62. Which set correctly identifies the traditional 5 Cs of credit?
A. Cash, Cost, Currency, Capital, Collection B. Customer, Cheque, Collateral, Contract, Cash flow C. Character, Capacity, Capital, Collateral, Conditions D. Credit, Control, Compliance, Currency, Cost
Correct Answer: C. Character, Capacity, Capital, Collateral, Conditions
Explanation: The 5 Cs are a traditional credit-appraisal framework. Character concerns willingness to repay, capacity concerns repayment ability, capital concerns the borrower's financial stake, collateral concerns security, and conditions concern the economic and transaction environment.
63. What does LTV stand for in secured lending?
A. Loan-to-Value B. Liability Transfer Verification C. Lending Term Variation D. Liquidity-to-Volume
Correct Answer: A. Loan-to-Value
Explanation: LTV expresses the loan amount relative to the relevant asset value. For example, a Tk 6 lakh loan against property valued at Tk 10 lakh gives an LTV of 60%, assuming those are the applicable measurement amounts.
64. What is the primary purpose of provisioning for credit losses?
A. To eliminate every possibility of borrower default B. To increase a bank's share price automatically C. To guarantee that all collateral can be sold immediately D. To recognise estimated or required losses associated with credit exposures
Correct Answer: D. To recognise estimated or required losses associated with credit exposures
Explanation: Provisioning recognises credit-loss expense or allowances according to the applicable accounting and regulatory framework. Provisions do not eliminate the underlying credit risk.
65. What does RBS mean in banking supervision?
A. Reserve Banking Standard B. Risk-Based Supervision C. Retail Banking Settlement D. Risk Borrowing Schedule
Correct Answer: B. Risk-Based Supervision
Explanation: RBS prioritises supervisory attention according to risk, potential impact, and the condition of institutions. It is intended to allocate supervisory resources more effectively than relying solely on uniform checks.
66. What does ICAAP stand for?
A. International Credit Assessment and Approval Process B. Internal Cash Allocation and Accounting Policy C. Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process D. Institutional Credit Audit and Assurance Programme
Correct Answer: C. Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process
Explanation: ICAAP is a bank's internal process for assessing capital adequacy in relation to its risk profile, strategy, and planning horizon. It is associated with the Basel supervisory framework.
67. What does RWA stand for?
A. Risk-Weighted Assets B. Reserve and Working Assets C. Regulatory Withdrawal Amount D. Risk Withholding Account
Correct Answer: A. Risk-Weighted Assets
Explanation: RWA reflects assets and relevant exposures adjusted by regulatory risk weights. Regulatory capital ratios compare eligible capital with the relevant risk-weighted exposure measure.
68. What does PDCA stand for in management and control improvement?
A. Payment, Deposit, Credit, Audit B. Profit, Debt, Capital, Assets C. Planning, Development, Compliance, Approval D. Plan, Do, Check, Act
Correct Answer: D. Plan, Do, Check, Act
Explanation: PDCA is a continuous-improvement cycle: plan a change, implement it, check results, and act on the findings. It is a management methodology rather than a banking-specific prudential ratio.
69. What is the primary function of a bank's internal audit?
A. To approve every loan personally B. To independently evaluate controls, risk management, and governance processes C. To set the central bank's policy rate D. To guarantee a profit every quarter
Correct Answer: B. To independently evaluate controls, risk management, and governance processes
Explanation: Internal audit provides independent assurance on governance, risk management, and internal controls. It does not replace management's responsibility for running the bank.
70. What does KRI stand for in risk management?
A. Key Reserve Instrument B. Known Repayment Index C. Key Risk Indicator D. Kredit Recovery Information
Correct Answer: C. Key Risk Indicator
Explanation: A KRI is a measure used to monitor changes in exposure to a particular risk. It can provide an early warning when a defined threshold is breached.
71. What does VaR stand for in financial risk management?
A. Value at Risk B. Variable Asset Return C. Verified Account Reconciliation D. Volume at Repayment
Correct Answer: A. Value at Risk
Explanation: VaR estimates a loss threshold over a specified horizon at a stated confidence level, subject to model assumptions. It does not represent the maximum possible loss.
72. What is operational risk?
A. Only the risk of foreign-exchange losses B. Only the risk of falling stock prices C. Only the risk that a borrower refuses to repay D. Risk arising from inadequate or failed processes, people, systems, or external events
Correct Answer: D. Risk arising from inadequate or failed processes, people, systems, or external events
Explanation: Operational risk includes process failures, fraud, system outages, human error, and certain external events. The precise regulatory definition may specify exclusions such as strategic or reputational risk.
73. What does ALCO commonly stand for in banking?
A. Asset Lending Compliance Office B. Asset-Liability Committee C. Annual Liquidity Control Operation D. Account Liability Clearing Organisation
Correct Answer: B. Asset-Liability Committee
Explanation: ALCO oversees matters such as liquidity, interest-rate risk in the banking book, funding, and balance-sheet structure. Its mandate varies by institution.
74. What is the principal purpose of stress testing?
A. To guarantee that no financial crisis will occur B. To calculate the exact future share price C. To assess how adverse hypothetical or historical scenarios could affect financial resilience D. To replace all regular risk monitoring
Correct Answer: C. To assess how adverse hypothetical or historical scenarios could affect financial resilience
Explanation: Stress testing examines the impact of severe but plausible scenarios on capital, liquidity, earnings, or other risk measures. Results depend on scenario design and model assumptions.
75. What is a bank's liquidity risk?
A. The risk of being unable to meet obligations when due without unacceptable loss B. The risk of every customer demanding a loan C. The risk that the bank's currency has no official name D. The risk of an accounting entry containing a spelling mistake only
Correct Answer: A. The risk of being unable to meet obligations when due without unacceptable loss
Explanation: Liquidity risk concerns the ability to fund assets and meet cash-flow obligations. A bank can be solvent yet experience liquidity stress if it cannot access funds in time.
Section E: Financial markets, instruments, and international banking
76. What does T-bill stand for?
A. Trade Balance Instrument B. Treasury Bill C. Transfer Banking Invoice D. Term Borrowing Index
Correct Answer: B. Treasury Bill
Explanation: Treasury bills are short-term government securities. They are generally issued at a discount or under another permitted pricing structure and redeemed according to their terms.
77. What does T-bond stand for?
A. Transaction Bond B. Treasury Banking Order C. Term Bill of Exchange D. Treasury Bond
Correct Answer: D. Treasury Bond
Explanation: Treasury bonds are government debt securities generally issued for longer maturities than Treasury bills. Their coupon, maturity, and market price depend on the instrument's terms and market conditions.
78. What does IPO stand for?
A. Initial Public Offering B. International Payment Order C. Internal Portfolio Operation D. Institutional Purchase Option
Correct Answer: A. Initial Public Offering
Explanation: An IPO is a company's initial public offering of securities to investors. In Bangladesh, public offerings fall within the applicable securities-law and BSEC regulatory framework.
79. What is the full form of DSE?
A. Deposit Settlement Exchange B. Development Securities Entity C. Dhaka Stock Exchange D. Domestic Savings Establishment
Correct Answer: C. Dhaka Stock Exchange
Explanation: DSE is one of Bangladesh's stock exchanges. It provides a market infrastructure for trading eligible securities under applicable rules.
80. What does CSE stand for in Bangladesh's securities market?
A. Central Settlement Entity B. Chittagong Stock Exchange C. Commercial Savings Exchange D. Capital Securities Establishment
Correct Answer: B. Chittagong Stock Exchange
Explanation: CSE is Bangladesh's other principal stock exchange. Its current corporate and operational details should be checked through official exchange and BSEC publications.
81. What does BSEC stand for?
A. Bangladesh Securities and Equity Council B. Banking and Savings Enforcement Commission C. Bangladesh Stock Exchange Corporation D. Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission
Correct Answer: D. Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission
Explanation: BSEC regulates Bangladesh's capital market within its statutory mandate, including relevant securities-market participants and activities.
82. What is the full form of NAV in investment-fund terminology?
A. Net Asset Value B. National Account Volume C. Net Advance Variation D. Nominal Asset Verification
Correct Answer: A. Net Asset Value
Explanation: NAV is the value of assets minus liabilities. For a fund, NAV per unit is typically calculated by dividing net assets by the number of outstanding units, subject to applicable valuation rules.
83. What does EPS stand for in corporate finance?
