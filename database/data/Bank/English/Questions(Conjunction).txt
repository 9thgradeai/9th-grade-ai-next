Section A: Fundamentals and Types of Conjunctions (1–25)
1. Which of the following is a coordinating conjunction?
A. Although
B. Because
C. But
D. Unless
Correct Answer: C. But
Explanation: Coordinating conjunctions join grammatically equivalent words, phrases or clauses. The seven common coordinating conjunctions are for, and, nor, but, or, yet, and so (FANBOYS).
2. Choose the correct conjunction: He is poor ___ honest.
A. because
B. but
C. unless
D. since
Correct Answer: B. but
Explanation: But expresses contrast between poverty and honesty. It joins two contrasting descriptions of the same person.
3. She stayed at home ___ she was ill.
A. although
B. unless
C. because
D. whereas
Correct Answer: C. because
Explanation: Because introduces a subordinate clause expressing reason. Her illness is the reason she stayed at home.
4. Which conjunction expresses a choice between alternatives?
A. Nor
B. Yet
C. So
D. Or
Correct Answer: D. Or
Explanation: Or presents alternatives or choices. Example: “Would you prefer tea or coffee?”
5. Identify the subordinating conjunction in the sentence: “Although it was raining, we continued playing.”
A. It
B. Although
C. Continued
D. Playing
Correct Answer: B. Although
Explanation: Although introduces a subordinate clause expressing concession. The main clause, “we continued playing,” remains grammatically independent.
6. Which pair is correctly used as correlative conjunctions?
A. Either—or
B. Although—but
C. Because—so
D. Unless—or
Correct Answer: A. Either—or
Explanation: Correlative conjunctions work in pairs. Common examples include either—or, neither—nor, both—and, and not only—but also.
7. Choose the correct option: You must hurry, ___ you will miss the train.
A. but
B. or
C. because
D. although
Correct Answer: B. or
Explanation: Or can introduce the negative consequence of failing to do something. The sentence means that if you do not hurry, you will miss the train.
8. Which conjunction expresses a result?
A. Though
B. Unless
C. So
D. While
Correct Answer: C. So
Explanation: So can connect a cause or situation with its result. Example: “It was late, so we went home.”
9. Select the appropriate conjunction: ___ he is wealthy, he is not happy.
A. Because
B. Although
C. Unless
D. So
Correct Answer: B. Although
Explanation: Although introduces a contrast between wealth and unhappiness. Do not normally add but to the same clause structure: “Although he is wealthy, he is not happy.”
10. Which of the following is a subordinating conjunction?
A. And
B. Yet
C. Nor
D. Unless
Correct Answer: D. Unless
Explanation: Unless introduces a condition and usually means “if … not.” Example: “You will fail unless you work hard.”
11. Choose the correct conjunction: I will wait here ___ you return.
A. until
B. because
C. although
D. whereas
Correct Answer: A. until
Explanation: Until indicates the endpoint of a period of waiting. The speaker will remain there up to the time the other person returns.
12. Which conjunction expresses time?
A. Because
B. If
C. When
D. Though
Correct Answer: C. When
Explanation: When introduces a time clause. Example: “Call me when you arrive.”
13. He neither smokes ___ drinks.
A. or
B. nor
C. but
D. and
Correct Answer: B. nor
Explanation: The standard correlative construction is neither … nor. It connects two negative alternatives.
14. Choose the correct conjunction: ___ you work hard, you will succeed.
A. If
B. Though
C. Whereas
D. Unless
Correct Answer: A. If
Explanation: If introduces a condition. The sentence predicts success on the condition that the person works hard.
15. Which conjunction expresses concession?
A. Since
B. Because
C. Though
D. Therefore
Correct Answer: C. Though
Explanation: Though introduces an unexpected contrast or concession. Example: “Though he was tired, he continued working.”
16. Select the correct option: He is not only intelligent ___ hardworking.
A. and
B. but also
C. as well
D. yet
Correct Answer: B. but also
Explanation: The correlative pair is not only … but also. It emphasizes the presence of two qualities or facts.
17. Which conjunction is used to express an alternative negative idea?
A. And
B. So
C. Nor
D. Yet
Correct Answer: C. Nor
Explanation: Nor adds another negative statement or alternative. It commonly follows neither or a preceding negative clause.
18. Choose the correct conjunction: Wait here ___ I come back.
A. until
B. because
C. unless
D. whereas
Correct Answer: A. until
Explanation: Until identifies how long the instruction remains in effect. It is appropriate when an action continues up to a particular time.
19. Which sentence contains a coordinating conjunction?
A. I left because it was late.
B. Although tired, he worked.
C. She studied, and she passed.
D. If you agree, sign here.
Correct Answer: C. She studied, and she passed.
Explanation: And coordinates two independent clauses. The other options use subordinate clauses or a reduced concessive construction.
20. Choose the appropriate conjunction: He ran fast, ___ he missed the bus.
A. so
B. because
C. yet
D. since
Correct Answer: C. yet
Explanation: Yet expresses an unexpected contrast, similar to but. Running fast would normally help someone catch a bus, making the outcome surprising.
21. Which conjunction means “provided that”?
A. Unless
B. As long as
C. Although
D. Whereas
Correct Answer: B. As long as
Explanation: As long as can introduce a condition equivalent to “provided that.” Example: “You may stay as long as you remain quiet.”
22. Select the correct option: No sooner had the bell rung ___ the students rushed out.
A. when
B. than
C. then
D. while
Correct Answer: B. than
Explanation: The standard construction is no sooner … than. It is usually followed by inversion in the first clause: “No sooner had … than …”.
23. Choose the correct conjunction: Scarcely had I reached the station ___ the train departed.
A. than
B. while
C. when
D. then
Correct Answer: C. when
Explanation: The conventional construction is scarcely … when. Hardly … when is another common pattern, while no sooner … than uses a different pairing.
24. Which conjunction expresses comparison or contrast between two facts?
A. Whereas
B. Therefore
C. Because
D. Unless
Correct Answer: A. Whereas
Explanation: Whereas can contrast two people, things or situations. Example: “Rina likes tea, whereas Mina prefers coffee.”
25. Choose the correct conjunction: He cannot attend the meeting ___ he is invited.
A. if
B. unless
C. because
D. although
Correct Answer: B. unless
Explanation: Unless means “if … not.” The sentence means he cannot attend if he is not invited.
Section B: Correlative Conjunctions and Paired Structures (26–50)
26. Choose the correct pair: ___ the principal ___ the teachers attended the ceremony.
A. Neither—and
B. Either—nor
C. Both—and
D. Whether—nor
Correct Answer: C. Both—and
Explanation: Both … and joins two people or things together. Both the principal and the teachers attended.
27. Neither Rahim nor his friends ___ present.
A. was
B. is
C. has been
D. were
Correct Answer: D. were
Explanation: With neither … nor, the verb generally agrees with the nearer subject. Here, friends is plural, so were is correct.
28. Either the students or the teacher ___ responsible for the mistake.
A. are
B. is
C. were
D. have been
Correct Answer: B. is
Explanation: In the standard proximity rule, the verb agrees with the nearer subject, the teacher. Therefore, the singular verb is is correct.
29. Not only the manager but also the employees ___ dissatisfied.
A. was
B. is
C. were
D. has been
Correct Answer: C. were
Explanation: In not only … but also, the verb generally agrees with the nearer subject. Since employees is plural, were is appropriate.
30. He is as intelligent ___ his brother.
A. than
B. like
C. as
D. so
Correct Answer: C. as
Explanation: Equality is expressed by as … as. The standard construction is “as intelligent as his brother.”
31. The more you practise, ___ you become.
A. better
B. the better
C. best
D. the best
Correct Answer: B. the better
Explanation: The comparative-correlative structure is the + comparative … the + comparative. It expresses a relationship in which one change accompanies another.
32. She is neither a doctor ___ a nurse.
A. or
B. and
C. but
D. nor
Correct Answer: D. nor
Explanation: Neither must be paired with nor in this construction. It indicates that neither of the two alternatives applies.
33. He would rather resign ___ accept a bribe.
A. than
B. to
C. instead
D. but
Correct Answer: A. than
Explanation: Would rather … than expresses preference. The base form of the verb follows would rather: “would rather resign than accept.”
34. I do not know whether he will come ___ stay at home.
A. nor
B. or
C. but
D. so
Correct Answer: B. or
Explanation: Whether … or introduces alternatives or possibilities. It is used when the outcome is uncertain.
35. She is not so much lazy ___ careless.
A. but
B. than
C. as
D. and
Correct Answer: C. as
Explanation: Not so much … as corrects or refines an initial description. The sentence suggests that carelessness, rather than laziness, is the main problem.
36. He is both honest ___ diligent.
A. but
B. or
C. nor
D. and
Correct Answer: D. and
Explanation: The correlative pair both … and combines two parallel qualities. The adjectives honest and diligent have the same grammatical function.
37. Choose the correct sentence.
A. Either he or his brothers is coming.
B. Either he or his brothers are coming.
C. Either he nor his brothers are coming.
D. Either he and his brothers are coming.
Correct Answer: B. Either he or his brothers are coming.
Explanation: The verb agrees with the nearer subject, brothers, which is plural. The correct pair is either … or.
38. The book is not only informative ___ entertaining.
A. and
B. or
C. but also
D. nor
Correct Answer: C. but also
Explanation: Not only … but also emphasizes two qualities. Parallel adjectives—informative and entertaining—follow the paired conjunction.
39. No sooner did he arrive ___ it began to rain.
A. when
B. then
C. as
D. than
Correct Answer: D. than
Explanation: The correct pairing is no sooner … than. When no sooner begins a clause, subject–auxiliary inversion is required: “No sooner did he arrive …”.
40. Hardly had the meeting begun ___ the electricity went out.
A. when
B. than
C. then
D. that
Correct Answer: A. when
Explanation: The standard pattern is hardly … when. It indicates that the second event occurred almost immediately after the first.
41. Choose the correct pair: He is ___ a good speaker ___ a good writer.
A. either—and
B. neither—or
C. both—and
D. whether—nor
Correct Answer: C. both—and
Explanation: Both … and joins two positive descriptions. The speaker is described as both a good speaker and a good writer.
42. You must decide ___ to accept the offer or reject it.
A. neither
B. whether
C. both
D. although
Correct Answer: B. whether
Explanation: Whether … or presents alternatives requiring a decision. The structure is especially common with verbs such as decide, know, and consider.
43. The weather was so bad ___ we cancelled the trip.
A. because
B. although
C. than
D. that
Correct Answer: D. that
Explanation: The result construction is so … that. It links the degree of the bad weather to its consequence.
44. She is such a capable leader ___ everyone respects her.
A. that
B. as
C. so
D. because
Correct Answer: A. that
Explanation: Such … that introduces a result. Compare “such a capable leader that …” with “so capable that …”.
45. The sooner we start, ___ we will finish.
A. soon
B. soonest
C. the sooner
D. the soonest
Correct Answer: C. the sooner
Explanation: The pattern the sooner … the sooner connects two related outcomes. Both clauses use comparative forms.
46. He is more diligent ___ his brother.
A. as
B. than
C. then
D. to
Correct Answer: B. than
Explanation: Comparatives ending in -er or formed with more are generally followed by than. Do not confuse than with then, which usually refers to time or sequence.
47. Neither the director nor the secretary ___ available yesterday.
A. are
B. is
C. were
D. was
Correct Answer: D. was
Explanation: The nearer subject, secretary, is singular, so was is used. The past-time expression yesterday also requires a past-tense verb.
48. Choose the correct sentence.
A. Both the proposal and the budget were approved.
B. Both the proposal as well as the budget were approved.
C. Both the proposal nor the budget was approved.
D. Both the proposal or the budget were approved.
Correct Answer: A. Both the proposal and the budget were approved.
Explanation: The correct correlative structure is both … and. Since the two subjects form a plural subject, the plural verb were is appropriate.
49. He would sooner die ___ surrender.
A. but
B. to
C. than
D. rather
Correct Answer: C. than
Explanation: Would sooner … than expresses a strong preference. It is followed by parallel verb forms: die and surrender.
50. The question is not whether he is qualified ___ whether he is willing.
A. and
B. but
C. nor
D. or
Correct Answer: B. but
Explanation: Not whether … but whether contrasts two issues. The question concerns willingness rather than qualification.
Section C: Subordinating Conjunctions—Time, Cause, Condition and Contrast (51–75)
51. ___ he was exhausted, he continued working.
A. Because
B. Since
C. Although
D. Unless
Correct Answer: C. Although
Explanation: Although introduces a concessive clause. It highlights the contrast between exhaustion and continued work.
52. We cancelled the match ___ it was raining heavily.
A. because
B. although
C. unless
D. whereas
Correct Answer: A. because
Explanation: Because introduces the reason for cancelling the match. It answers the question “Why did we cancel it?”
53. ___ you submit the form today, your application will be considered.
A. Although
B. Unless
C. Whereas
D. If
Correct Answer: D. If
Explanation: If introduces a condition. The application will be considered on the stated condition of submitting the form today.
54. You will not pass ___ you study regularly.
A. if
B. unless
C. although
D. because
Correct Answer: B. unless
Explanation: Unless means “if … not.” The sentence means you will not pass if you do not study regularly.
55. I will call you ___ I reach Dhaka.
A. although
B. because
C. when
D. unless
Correct Answer: C. when
Explanation: When introduces a time clause. In a future-time clause, the present simple is normally used: “when I reach,” not “when I will reach.”
56. Wait here ___ I return.
A. until
B. because
C. whereas
D. although
Correct Answer: A. until
Explanation: Until marks the endpoint of waiting. The action continues up to the time the speaker returns.
57. ___ you had told me earlier, I could have helped you.
A. Unless
B. Although
C. If
D. Because
Correct Answer: C. If
Explanation: This is a third conditional sentence about an unreal past situation. The pattern is if + past perfect, could/would have + past participle.
58. He speaks as though he ___ everything.
A. knows
B. knew
C. has known
D. will know
Correct Answer: B. knew
Explanation: As though introduces a comparison that may be unreal or contrary to fact. The past form knew can express present-time unreality.
59. ___ I was walking home, I met an old friend.
A. Because
B. Unless
C. Though
D. While
Correct Answer: D. While
Explanation: While indicates that one event occurred during another. The meeting happened during the speaker's walk home.
60. Take an umbrella ___ it rains.
A. in case
B. although
C. because
D. whereas
Correct Answer: A. in case
Explanation: In case introduces a precaution against a possible future event. The umbrella is taken as a precaution, not necessarily because rain has already begun.
61. ___ he had little money, he helped the poor.
A. Because
B. Although
C. Unless
D. Since
Correct Answer: B. Although
Explanation: Although introduces a contrast between having little money and helping others. The action is unexpected given the stated circumstance.
62. The match will continue ___ the rain becomes too heavy.
A. if
B. because
C. unless
D. although
Correct Answer: C. unless
Explanation: Unless means “if … not.” The match will continue if the rain does not become too heavy.
63. ___ you finish the assignment, you may leave.
A. Once
B. Although
C. Unless
D. Whereas
Correct Answer: A. Once
Explanation: Once means “as soon as” or “after the point at which.” It indicates that leaving is permitted after the assignment is finished.
64. He left early ___ he could catch the first train.
A. although
B. whereas
C. because
D. so that
Correct Answer: D. so that
Explanation: So that introduces purpose. It explains why he left early: to make catching the first train possible.
65. ___ you are careful, you may still make mistakes.
A. Because
B. Even if
C. Unless
D. Since
Correct Answer: B. Even if
Explanation: Even if introduces a condition whose result remains possible despite that condition. Carefulness does not guarantee freedom from mistakes.
66. He waited ___ everyone had left.
A. unless
B. although
C. until
D. because
Correct Answer: C. until
Explanation: Until indicates the endpoint of waiting. The waiting continued up to the time everyone left.
67. ___ the road was narrow, the bus passed safely.
A. Although
B. Because
C. Unless
D. Since
Correct Answer: A. Although
Explanation: Although signals that the narrow road might have made passage difficult, yet the bus passed safely.
68. I will not go out ___ the rain stops.
A. because
B. although
C. if
D. until
Correct Answer: D. until
Explanation: Until indicates that the speaker's decision will remain in force up to the time the rain stops.
69. He talks ___ he were an expert.
A. because
B. as if
C. unless
D. so that
Correct Answer: B. as if
Explanation: As if introduces a comparison or appearance. The subjunctive-like form were is conventional in formal English when describing an unreal situation.
70. ___ you need assistance, please contact the office.
A. Although
B. Whereas
C. Should
D. Because
Correct Answer: C. Should
Explanation: Should you need assistance is an inverted conditional equivalent to “If you should need assistance.” The conjunction if is omitted when should is placed before the subject.
71. He remained calm ___ everyone else was panicking.
A. whereas
B. because
C. unless
D. so that
Correct Answer: A. whereas
Explanation: Whereas contrasts two situations. His calmness is contrasted with everyone else's panic.
72. ___ the examination was difficult, she performed well.
A. Because
B. Since
C. Unless
D. Though
Correct Answer: D. Though
Explanation: Though introduces concession. Despite the difficulty of the examination, she performed well.
73. I will lend you the book ___ you return it tomorrow.
A. although
B. provided that
C. whereas
D. because
Correct Answer: B. provided that
Explanation: Provided that introduces a condition, meaning “on the condition that.” Returning the book tomorrow is a requirement for borrowing it.
74. ___ the teacher entered the room, the students stood up.
A. Unless
B. Although
C. As soon as
D. Because
Correct Answer: C. As soon as
Explanation: As soon as means immediately after a particular event. It emphasizes the close timing of the teacher's entrance and the students' response.
75. He succeeded ___ he lacked formal qualifications.
A. despite the fact that
B. because
C. unless
D. so that
Correct Answer: A. despite the fact that
Explanation: Despite the fact that introduces a concessive clause. Unlike despite alone, it can be followed directly by a subject and verb.
Section D: Conjunctions in Sentence Completion and Meaning (76–100)
76. He is intelligent, ___ he often makes careless mistakes.
A. because
B. yet
C. so
D. for
Correct Answer: B. yet
Explanation: Yet expresses an unexpected contrast. Intelligence would normally be associated with fewer mistakes, so the contrast is appropriate.
77. I could not attend the meeting ___ I was ill.
A. although
B. whereas
C. because
D. unless
Correct Answer: C. because
Explanation: Because introduces the reason for not attending. It connects the illness with the inability to attend the meeting.
78. Work hard, ___ you will regret it later.
A. or
B. and
C. because
D. although
Correct Answer: A. or
Explanation: Or introduces an undesirable consequence if the instruction is ignored. The implied meaning is “If you do not work hard, you will regret it.”
79. He studied hard, ___ he failed to secure the position.
A. therefore
B. so
C. because
D. but
Correct Answer: D. but
Explanation: But contrasts the effort made with the disappointing outcome. Therefore and so would normally suggest a result consistent with the first clause.
80. She was absent ___ she had an important appointment.
A. though
B. because
C. unless
D. whereas
Correct Answer: B. because
Explanation: Because introduces the reason for her absence. It establishes a cause-and-effect relationship.
81. You may borrow my laptop ___ you handle it carefully.
A. although
B. because
C. provided that
D. whereas
Correct Answer: C. provided that
Explanation: Provided that expresses a condition. Permission is granted on the condition that the laptop is handled carefully.
82. He is poor, ___ he is contented.
A. yet
B. because
C. so
D. for
Correct Answer: A. yet
Explanation: Yet expresses a contrast between poverty and contentment. It is similar in meaning to but, though it often emphasizes the unexpected nature of the second clause.
83. She worked overtime ___ she could complete the report.
A. although
B. unless
C. whereas
D. so that
Correct Answer: D. so that
Explanation: So that introduces purpose. Her overtime work was intended to enable her to complete the report.
84. He will not be promoted ___ he improves his performance.
A. if
B. unless
C. although
D. because
Correct Answer: B. unless
Explanation: Unless means “if … not.” Improvement is a necessary condition for promotion in the sentence.
85. I will support your proposal ___ it is financially viable.
A. although
B. whereas
C. if
D. because
Correct Answer: C. if
Explanation: If introduces a condition under which support will be given. Financial viability determines whether the proposal will receive support.
86. He failed ___ he had worked hard.
A. although
B. because
C. since
D. so
Correct Answer: A. although
Explanation: Although introduces a concession. His failure is contrasted with his hard work.
87. The road was blocked, ___ we took another route.
A. yet
B. because
C. although
D. so
Correct Answer: D. so
Explanation: So introduces the consequence of the road being blocked. Taking another route was the result of the obstruction.
88. He is not only a good teacher ___ an excellent researcher.
A. and
B. but also
C. or
D. nor
Correct Answer: B. but also
Explanation: The correct pair is not only … but also. It connects two noun phrases describing the same person.
89. You can either submit the report today ___ request an extension.
A. nor
B. but
C. or
D. and
Correct Answer: C. or
Explanation: Either … or presents two alternatives. The alternatives here are submitting the report and requesting an extension.
90. He neither apologised ___ explained his behaviour.
A. nor
B. or
C. and
D. but
Correct Answer: A. nor
Explanation: Neither … nor joins two negative alternatives. Both verbs, apologised and explained, are in the past tense.
91. ___ he apologises, I will not forgive him.
A. Although
B. Because
C. Since
D. Unless
Correct Answer: D. Unless
Explanation: Unless means “if … not.” The sentence indicates that an apology is required before forgiveness will be considered.
92. The committee rejected the proposal ___ it was too expensive.
A. although
B. because
C. whereas
D. unless
Correct Answer: B. because
Explanation: Because provides the reason for the rejection. The proposal was rejected on financial grounds.
93. He speaks English ___ he had lived in London for years.
A. because
B. unless
C. as if
D. so that
Correct Answer: C. as if
Explanation: As if introduces a comparison with an assumed situation. It suggests that his manner of speaking resembles that of someone who has lived in London for years.
94. I will finish the work ___ you arrive.
A. before
B. because
C. although
D. unless
Correct Answer: A. before
Explanation: Before introduces a time clause. The intended sequence is that the work will be finished prior to the other person's arrival.
95. ___ the sun had set, the travellers continued their journey.
A. Because
B. Since
C. Unless
D. Even though
Correct Answer: D. Even though
Explanation: Even though expresses a strong concession. The travellers continued despite the fact that the sun had set.
96. He is taller ___ I expected.
A. as
B. than
C. then
D. like
Correct Answer: B. than
Explanation: Comparative adjectives are followed by than. The sentence compares his actual height with the speaker's expectation.
97. She will succeed ___ she perseveres.
A. although
B. whereas
C. if
D. unless
Correct Answer: C. if
Explanation: If introduces the condition necessary for the predicted success. The sentence expresses a conditional relationship between perseverance and success.
98. ___ he was inexperienced, he handled the task well.
A. Although
B. Because
C. Since
D. So
Correct Answer: A. Although
Explanation: Although contrasts his lack of experience with his successful performance. The sentence highlights an unexpected result.
99. The manager spoke slowly ___ everyone could understand the instructions.
A. because
B. unless
C. although
D. so that
Correct Answer: D. so that
Explanation: So that expresses purpose. The manager spoke slowly with the intention of making the instructions understandable.
100. I will stay here ___ you need me.
A. although
B. as long as
C. unless
D. whereas
Correct Answer: B. as long as
Explanation: As long as can mean “for the period during which” or “provided that.” Here, it indicates how long the speaker will stay.
Section E: Error Detection and Sentence Correction (101–125)
101. Choose the grammatically correct sentence.
A. Although he was tired, but he continued working.
B. Although he was tired, he continued working.
C. Though he was tired, yet but he continued working.
D. Despite he was tired, he continued working.
Correct Answer: B. Although he was tired, he continued working.
Explanation: In standard exam grammar, although or though does not normally require but in the same clause structure. Also, despite is followed by a noun phrase or gerund, not directly by a finite clause.
102. Choose the correct sentence.
A. Because he was ill, so he stayed home.
B. Because he was ill, he stayed home.
C. Because of he was ill, he stayed home.
D. Because he was ill, therefore he stayed home.
Correct Answer: B. Because he was ill, he stayed home.
Explanation: Because introduces the reason, so so or therefore is unnecessary in this construction. Use “because he was ill” or “he was ill, so he stayed home.”
103. Select the correct sentence.
A. Neither Rahim or Karim attended.
B. Neither Rahim nor Karim attended.
C. Neither Rahim and Karim attended.
D. Neither Rahim but Karim attended.
Correct Answer: B. Neither Rahim nor Karim attended.
Explanation: The correct negative correlative pair is neither … nor. The alternatives are both excluded.
104. Choose the correct sentence.
A. No sooner he arrived than the meeting began.
B. No sooner did he arrived than the meeting began.
C. No sooner did he arrive than the meeting began.
D. No sooner had he arrive when the meeting began.
Correct Answer: C. No sooner did he arrive than the meeting began.
Explanation: When no sooner begins the clause, inversion is required. After did, use the base form arrive, and pair no sooner with than.
105. Choose the correct sentence.
A. Hardly had I reached home when it started raining.
B. Hardly had I reached home than it started raining.
C. Hardly I had reached home when it started raining.
D. Hardly had I reached home then it started raining.
Correct Answer: A. Hardly had I reached home when it started raining.
Explanation: The standard pattern is hardly … when, with subject–auxiliary inversion after the initial negative adverb. This structure describes two events occurring in quick succession.
106. Choose the correct sentence.
A. Either the manager or the clerks is responsible.
B. Either the manager nor the clerks are responsible.
C. Either the manager and the clerks are responsible.
D. Either the manager or the clerks are responsible.
Correct Answer: D. Either the manager or the clerks are responsible.
Explanation: The standard pair is either … or. The verb agrees with the nearer subject, clerks, which is plural.
107. Which sentence is correct?
A. He is both intelligent as well as hardworking.
B. He is both intelligent and hardworking.
C. He is both intelligent but hardworking.
D. He is both intelligent nor hardworking.
Correct Answer: B. He is both intelligent and hardworking.
Explanation: The correct correlative pair is both … and. The adjectives form a parallel structure.
108. Select the correct sentence.
A. Unless you do not work hard, you will fail.
B. Unless you work hard, you will not fail.
C. Unless you work hard, you will fail.
D. Unless you will work hard, you will fail.
Correct Answer: C. Unless you work hard, you will fail.
