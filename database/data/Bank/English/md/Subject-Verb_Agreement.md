# Bank English • Subject-Verb_Agreement

Q: Choose the correct verb: Each of the candidates _____ required to submit an application.
A) are
B) is
C) were
D) have been
Ans: B
Exp: Explanation: The subject is each, which takes a singular verb. The plural noun candidates is part of the prepositional phrase and does not determine the verb.

Q: Neither of the two proposals _____ acceptable to the committee.
A) are
B) were
C) is
D) have been
Ans: C
Exp: Explanation: In formal examination grammar, neither of + plural noun generally takes a singular verb. Remember that the subject is neither, not proposals.

Q: The quality of these products _____ improved considerably.
A) have
B) are
C) were
D) has
Ans: D
Exp: Explanation: The head noun is quality, which is singular. The phrase of these products does not change the number of the subject.

Q: One of my closest friends _____ in Dhaka.
A) lives
B) live
C) are living
D) have lived
Ans: A
Exp: Explanation: One is the subject and takes a singular verb. In the simple present tense, a third-person singular subject takes a verb ending in -s or -es.

Q: The list of selected candidates _____ on the notice board.
A) are
B) have been
C) is
D) were
Ans: C
Exp: Explanation: The head noun list is singular. Do not mistakenly use a plural verb because candidates is plural.

Q: Every boy and every girl _____ given an identity card.
A) were
B) was
C) have been
D) are
Ans: B
Exp: Explanation: When every precedes each member of a compound subject separately, the verb is generally singular. The intended meaning is that each individual received a card.

Q: The teacher, along with her students, _____ attending the seminar.
A) are
B) were
C) have been
D) is
Ans: D
Exp: Explanation: Expressions such as along with, together with, and as well as do not make a singular subject plural. The main subject is teacher.

Q: Bread and butter _____ my usual breakfast.
A) is
B) are
C) have been
D) were
Ans: A
Exp: Explanation: When two nouns joined by and express a single combined item or concept, they can take a singular verb. Here, bread and butter is treated as one breakfast.

Q: The poet and philosopher _____ dead.
A) are
B) have
C) is
D) were
Ans: C
Exp: Explanation: The singular article the before the combined title indicates one person who was both a poet and a philosopher. If two different people were intended, the wording would normally be the poet and the philosopher are dead.

Q: The poet and the philosopher _____ invited to the ceremony.
A) was
B) were
C) has been
D) is
Ans: B
Exp: Explanation: Repeating the article the ordinarily indicates two distinct people. The compound subject is plural, so it takes were.

Q: Either Rahim or his brothers _____ responsible for the mistake.
A) is
B) was
C) has been
D) are
Ans: D
Exp: Explanation: With either...or, the verb generally agrees with the nearer subject. Here, brothers is plural, so are is correct.

Q: Neither the officers nor the manager _____ willing to accept the proposal.
A) was
B) were
C) are
D) have been
Ans: A
Exp: Explanation: In neither...nor constructions, the verb generally agrees with the nearer subject. The nearer subject, manager, is singular.

Q: Not only the students but also the teacher _____ present at the meeting.
A) were
B) are
C) was
D) have been
Ans: C
Exp: Explanation: With not only...but also, the verb generally agrees with the subject nearest to it. Since teacher is singular, the singular verb was is appropriate.

Q: A number of applicants _____ waiting outside the examination hall.

Exp: Explanation: The number of refers to one numerical total and takes a singular verb. This is a frequently tested contrast with a number of.

Q: Mathematics _____ considered a difficult subject by many students.

Ans: A
Exp: Explanation: The name of a subject ending in -s, such as mathematics, normally takes a singular verb. The same principle applies to physics and economics when referring to academic disciplines.

Exp: Explanation: Scissors is a plural-form noun that ordinarily takes a plural verb. To refer to one item, use a pair of scissors is lying on the table.
