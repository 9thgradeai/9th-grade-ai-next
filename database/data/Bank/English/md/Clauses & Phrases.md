# Bank English · Clauses & Phrases

Q: The board of directors objected to *his presenting the audit report without prior review*. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Present Participle Phrase
B) Adverbial Phrase
C) Gerund Phrase
D) Adjective Clause
Ans: C
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** Preposition ("to") + possessive adjective ("his") MUST be followed by a noun/gerund. The "-ing" phrase acts as a noun object here.

Q: *That the inflation rate would double* was anticipated by the leading economists. The underlined part functions as a/an:
A) Adverbial Clause of Reason
B) Noun Clause
C) Adjective Clause
D) Absolute Phrase
Ans: B
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** It occupies the subject position before the main verb "was". Only Noun Clauses (or noun phrases/pronouns) can act as the subject of a sentence.

Q: The financial analyst, *a veteran of the 2008 market crash*, advised against speculative trading. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Adjective Clause
B) Appositive Phrase
C) Absolute Phrase
D) Adverb Phrase
Ans: B
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** It is a noun phrase set off by commas that renames or identifies the adjacent noun ("analyst") without using a verb.

Q: Which of the following sentences contains a dangling modifier?
A) Having reviewed the balance sheet, the auditor found several discrepancies.
B) To secure the loan, collateral must be provided by the applicant.
C) While drafting the policy, the committee considered all risks.
D) Burdened by debt, the company filed for bankruptcy.
Ans: B
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** "To secure the loan" (infinitive phrase) modifies the subject. "Collateral" cannot secure a loan; the *applicant* secures it. The modifier is dangling.

Q: The central bank will intervene in the forex market *whenever the currency depreciates beyond the threshold*. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Noun Clause
B) Adjective Clause
C) Adverb Clause of Condition
D) Adverb Clause of Time
Ans: D
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** "Whenever" answers the question "When?" regarding the main verb "intervene". Thus, it's an Adverb Clause of Time.

Q: The CEO demanded *that the quarterly targets be met by Friday*. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Adverb Clause of Purpose
B) Adjective Clause
C) Noun Clause
D) Infinitive Phrase
Ans: C
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** Demanded *what*? Answers to "what" after a transitive verb indicate a Noun Clause. (Note the subjunctive mood "be met").

Q: The contract contains a strict clause, *violating which* will result in immediate termination. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Adverbial Clause
B) Adjective Clause
C) Participle Phrase
D) Absolute Phrase
Ans: B
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** "Violating which" contains a relative pronoun ("which") relating back to "clause" and a verb structure, making it an Adjective/Relative Clause.

Q: *Weather permitting*, the treasury department will host the annual summit outdoors. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Adverb Clause
B) Appositive Phrase
C) Present Participle Phrase
D) Absolute Phrase
Ans: D
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** Noun ("Weather") + Participle ("permitting") with no grammatical link to the main clause subject = Absolute Phrase.

Q: The HR manager is looking for a candidate *who has a strong background in risk management*. The underlined clause is:
A) Restrictive Adjective Clause
B) Non-restrictive Adjective Clause
C) Noun Clause
D) Adverbial Clause
Ans: A
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** It defines *which* candidate is needed and has no commas. Restrictive clauses are essential to the sentence's meaning.

Q: *To mitigate credit risk during the recession* was the bank's primary objective. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Adverb Phrase
B) Infinitive Phrase acting as a Subject
C) Prepositional Phrase
D) Gerund Phrase
Ans: B
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** It starts with "To + V1" and sits directly in the subject slot before the linking verb "was".

Q: We are fully aware of *why the syndicate manipulated the stock prices*. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Adjective Clause
B) Adverb Clause of Reason
C) Noun Clause
D) Prepositional Phrase
Ans: C
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** It follows a preposition ("of"). Only a Noun Clause can act as the object of a preposition.

Q: The government introduced subsidies *so that the agricultural sector could survive the drought*. The underlined part is an Adverb Clause of:
A) Reason
B) Condition
C) Result
D) Purpose
Ans: D
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** "So that + modal (could/might/would)" always indicates Purpose (the intention behind the action).

Q: *Exhausted from the relentless auditing process*, the accountant finally submitted his resignation. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Past Participle Phrase
B) Absolute Phrase
C) Gerund Phrase
D) Adjective Clause
Ans: A
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** Starts with a V3 ("Exhausted") and modifies the subject "accountant". It acts as an adjective, making it a Participle Phrase.

Q: Identify the sentence with a Noun Clause acting as an Appositive:
A) The rumor that the bank is insolvent caused a panic.
B) The bank that went insolvent caused a panic.
C) He told me that the bank is insolvent.
D) That the bank is insolvent is a known fact.
Ans: A
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** "That the bank is insolvent" is a fact that directly renames "The rumor" (Rumor = insolvency). Option B is an Adjective Clause.

Q: The manager is the only person *whom the board trusts with the confidential files*. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Noun Clause
B) Adjective Clause
C) Adverb Clause
D) Appositive Phrase
Ans: B
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** "Whom" refers back to the noun "person". Any clause modifying a noun is an Adjective Clause.

Q: *Standing by the printer*, the quarterly financial report was accidentally shredded. What is the grammatical error here?
A) Misplaced Noun Clause
B) Dangling Participle Phrase
C) Faulty Parallelism
D) Split Infinitive
Ans: B
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** "Standing by the printer" modifies the subject "report." A report cannot stand. The modifier is dangling.

Q: The investors were hesitant to fund the startup, *for it lacked a sustainable revenue model*. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Noun Clause
B) Adverb Clause of Reason
C) Independent Clause
D) Adjective Clause
Ans: C
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** "For" is a coordinating conjunction (FANBOYS). It joins two Independent Clauses, creating a compound sentence.

Q: *Whatever decision the monetary committee makes today* will impact the inflation rate for years. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Adverb Clause of Concession
B) Adjective Clause
C) Noun Clause
D) Absolute Phrase
Ans: C
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** Substitute the whole clause with "It". ("It will impact..."). If "It" fits as the subject, it's a Noun Clause.

Q: She spoke in such a low voice *that the stenographer could not record her testimony*. The underlined part is an Adverb Clause of:
A) Purpose
B) Result/Consequence
C) Manner
D) Comparison
Ans: B
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** "Such... that" or "So... that" always introduces a Result/Consequence clause.

Q: The officer was suspended for *accepting bribes from loan defaulters*. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Infinitive Phrase
B) Present Participle Phrase
C) Gerund Phrase
D) Adverb Phrase
Ans: C
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** Preposition ("for") MUST be followed by an object (noun). "Accepting" is an -ing verb acting as a noun, hence a Gerund Phrase.

Q: *Whoever leaked the sensitive customer data* must face immediate legal action. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Adverb Clause
B) Noun Clause
C) Adjective Clause
D) Relative Phrase
Ans: B
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** Acts as the subject of the verb "must face". Clauses functioning as subjects are always Noun Clauses.

Q: The new software is highly efficient; *nevertheless, it requires extensive employee training*. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Dependent Adverb Clause
B) Independent Clause
C) Noun Clause
D) Adjective Clause
Ans: B
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** "Nevertheless" is a conjunctive adverb joining two Independent Clauses separated by a semicolon.

Q: *Given the volatile market conditions*, we advise against heavy equity investments. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Prepositional Phrase
B) Absolute Phrase
C) Participle Phrase
D) Adverb Clause
Ans: A
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** "Given" acts as a preposition here (meaning "considering"). It is followed by a noun phrase, making it a Prepositional Phrase.

Q: The candidate answered the interview questions *as if he had memorized the corporate manual*. The underlined part is an Adverb Clause of:
A) Condition
B) Time
C) Manner
D) Concession
Ans: C
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** "As if" and "as though" answer the question "How?" regarding the verb, indicating Manner.

Q: My primary responsibility is *approving all outward remittances*. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Gerund Phrase acting as Subject Complement
B) Present Participle Phrase acting as Adjective
C) Infinitive Phrase
D) Noun Clause

Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** Follows a linking verb ("is") and renames the subject ("responsibility" = "approving"). It acts as a noun complement.

Q: The building *where the central bank's headquarters is located* was constructed in 1960. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Adverb Clause of Place
B) Noun Clause
C) Adjective Clause
D) Prepositional Phrase
Ans: C
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** It modifies the noun "building". Even though it starts with "where" (often an adverb marker), when it modifies a noun, it's an Adjective Clause.

Q: *Provided that the collateral is verified*, the loan will be disbursed within 24 hours. The underlined part is an Adverb Clause of:

B) Reason
C) Condition
D) Concession

Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** "Provided that" is synonymous with "If". "If" dictates an Adverb Clause of Condition.

Q: *Their application having been rejected*, the promoters sought alternative financing. The underlined part is a/an:

B) Gerund Phrase
C) Appositive Phrase
D) Absolute Phrase
Ans: D
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** Noun ("Their application") + Participle ("having been rejected") modifying the whole sentence without being the subject = Absolute Phrase.

Q: The notion *that cryptocurrency will replace fiat money* is highly debated. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Adjective Clause
B) Noun Clause in Apposition
C) Adverb Clause of Result
D) Participle Phrase

Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** It answers "what is the notion?" It renames the abstract noun "notion" (Notion = crypto will replace fiat).

Q: She will not sign the merger agreement *unless all her demands are met*. The underlined part is an Adverb Clause of:
A) Concession
B) Purpose
C) Condition
D) Time
Ans: C
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** "Unless" means "if not". It sets a condition for the main clause.

Q: The firm hired a consultant *to streamline their supply chain operations*. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Infinitive Phrase acting as an Adverb
B) Infinitive Phrase acting as an Adjective
C) Gerund Phrase
D) Prepositional Phrase
Ans: A
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** It answers "Why did they hire?" Answering "Why?" modifies the verb, making it an Adverbial Infinitive Phrase.

Q: Identify the correct sentence regarding clause structure:
A) The client asked when will the funds be transferred?
B) The client asked when the funds would be transferred.
C) The client asked that when the funds would be transferred.
D) The client asked when would the funds be transferred.
Ans: B
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** In indirect speech (Noun Clause as object), the structure must be affirmative (Subject + Verb), not interrogative (Verb + Subject). Drop the "that" before wh-words.

Q: The board members, *most of whom are senior economists*, voted against the proposal. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Noun Clause
B) Absolute Phrase
C) Non-restrictive Adjective Clause
D) Adverb Clause
Ans: C
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** "Whom" relates to "board members" (modifies the noun). It is set off by commas, meaning it adds non-essential info (Non-restrictive).

Q: *Before signing the legally binding contract*, you must read the terms and conditions. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Adverb Clause
B) Gerund Phrase
C) Prepositional Phrase
D) Present Participle Phrase
Ans: C
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** "Before" is a preposition here, followed by a gerund ("signing"). A preposition + its object forms a Prepositional Phrase. (Note: Not a clause because there is no subject-verb pair).

Q: The company's profits plunged, *which resulted in massive layoffs*. The underlined part is a/an:
A) Noun Clause
B) Adjective Clause modifying the previous clause
C) Adverb Clause of Result
D) Absolute Phrase
Ans: B
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** The relative pronoun "which" modifies the entire preceding independent clause. It acts as an Adjective Clause.

Q: Which of the following is a complex sentence?
A) The market crashed, and many investors lost their savings.
B) Due to the market crash, many investors lost their savings.
C) Many investors lost their savings because the market crashed.
D) The market having crashed, many investors lost their savings.
Ans: C
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** Complex sentences require one independent clause and at least one dependent clause. "Because" introduces a dependent Adverb Clause.
