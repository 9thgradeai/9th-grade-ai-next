# Bank English · Idioms & Phrases

Q: The compliance department had to erect a *Chinese wall* between the advisory and trading divisions. The underlined idiom means:
A) A strong physical barrier to prevent eavesdropping.
B) An ethical barrier preventing the exchange of confidential information.
C) A bureaucratic delay that slows down operations.
D) A strategy to block foreign investments.
Ans: B
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** In corporate/banking contexts, a "Chinese wall" strictly refers to an ethical information barrier, not a physical or foreign concept.

Q: The startup is in a *Catch-22* situation; they cannot get a loan without a credit history, but cannot build a credit history without a loan. The idiom implies:
A) A highly profitable dual-investment scheme.
B) A paradoxical situation from which an individual cannot escape.
C) A legal loophole used by financial institutions.
D) A scenario where two parties mutually benefit.
Ans: B
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** "Catch-22" implies a logical paradox where the solution to a problem relies on a condition that is impossible to meet.

Q: Despite the massive losses, the CEO told the board to *take it on the chin* and move forward. What does the phrase mean?
A) To fight back aggressively against competitors.
B) To accept an unpleasant situation bravely without complaining.
C) To deflect blame onto junior staff members.
D) To hide financial records from the auditors.
Ans: B
Exp: **Speed Shortcut:** Eliminate literal physical actions. Taking a hit "on the chin" metaphorically means absorbing a blow or difficulty with stoic courage.
