Section 1: Basic Prepositions of Time (1–30)
1. The meeting will begin ___ 10 a.m.
A. in
B. on
C. at
D. by
Correct Answer: C. at
Explanation: Use at for a specific clock time: at 10 a.m., at noon, at midnight.
2. Bangladesh became independent ___ 1971.
A. at
B. in
C. on
D. by
Correct Answer: B. in
Explanation: Use in with years, months, centuries and long periods. Example: in 2020, in December, in the twenty-first century.
3. The examination will be held ___ Monday.
A. at
B. in
C. on
D. by
Correct Answer: C. on
Explanation: Use on with days and dates: on Friday, on 21 February, on 16 December.
4. He has been working here ___ 2018.
A. for
B. since
C. from
D. during
Correct Answer: B. since
Explanation: Since identifies the starting point of an action continuing to the present. Use for to indicate its duration.
5. She has been studying ___ three hours.
A. since
B. from
C. during
D. for
Correct Answer: D. for
Explanation: Use for with a period of time: for three hours, for two days, for several years.
6. You must submit the application ___ Friday.
A. until
B. by
C. since
D. during
Correct Answer: B. by
Explanation: By Friday means no later than Friday. It sets a deadline rather than describing an action continuing until that day.
7. I waited for him ___ noon.
A. until
B. by
C. since
D. within
Correct Answer: A. until
Explanation: Until indicates that an action continues up to a particular time. Example: We worked until midnight.
8. The office remains closed ___ the lunch break.
A. while
B. during
C. since
D. for
Correct Answer: B. during
Explanation: Use during before a noun referring to a period or event. Example: during the meeting, during the holidays.
9. Please finish the assignment ___ two hours.
A. at
B. on
C. within
D. since
Correct Answer: C. within
Explanation: Within two hours means before the two-hour period expires. It expresses a time limit.
10. He arrived ___ the appointed time.
A. in
B. on
C. at
D. by
Correct Answer: C. at
Explanation: Use at for a particular point in time: at the appointed time, at the right moment, at dawn.
11. The train arrived ___ time, so we did not miss it.
A. at
B. on
C. in
D. by
Correct Answer: B. on
Explanation: On time means punctual or at the scheduled time. In time means early enough for a purpose.
12. We reached the station just ___ time to catch the train.
A. on
B. at
C. in
D. by
Correct Answer: C. in
Explanation: In time means early enough to do something before it is too late. This contrasts with on time, which means punctual.
13. The bank operates ___ Sunday to Thursday.
A. since
B. from
C. for
D. during
Correct Answer: B. from
Explanation: Use from ... to to express the beginning and end of a period. Example: from January to March.
14. He usually reads the newspaper ___ the morning.
A. at
B. on
C. in
D. by
Correct Answer: C. in
Explanation: Use in with parts of the day: in the morning, in the afternoon, in the evening. The usual exception is at night.
15. We often go for a walk ___ night.
A. on
B. at
C. in
D. by
Correct Answer: B. at
Explanation: The standard expression is at night. Compare: in the morning, in the afternoon, at noon.
16. The contract will remain valid ___ the end of December.
A. by
B. since
C. until
D. for
Correct Answer: C. until
Explanation: Until the end of December indicates that the contract remains valid up to that point. By the end of December sets a deadline.
17. He completed the work ___ the deadline.
A. before
B. during
C. since
D. until
Correct Answer: A. before
Explanation: Before means earlier than a specified time or event. It is commonly used to indicate completion ahead of a deadline.
18. The new policy will come into effect ___ 1 January.
A. at
B. in
C. on
D. by
Correct Answer: C. on
Explanation: Use on before a specific calendar date. Example: on 26 March, on 1 January.
19. The company was established ___ the nineteenth century.
A. at
B. on
C. in
D. by
Correct Answer: C. in
Explanation: Use in with centuries and historical periods: in the nineteenth century, in the Middle Ages.
20. He has not visited his village ___ last Eid.
A. for
B. since
C. during
D. by
Correct Answer: B. since
Explanation: Since last Eid identifies the point from which the absence of visits began. The present perfect is appropriate for a situation continuing up to the present.
21. I will return ___ an hour.
A. at
B. since
C. in
D. for
Correct Answer: C. in
Explanation: In an hour can mean one hour from now. It is also used for future time intervals, as in The results will be published in two days.
22. The manager was absent ___ the entire week.
A. since
B. during
C. for
D. by
Correct Answer: C. for
Explanation: For the entire week emphasizes duration. During the week refers to something happening within that period.
23. We have to complete the project ___ the next six months.
A. within
B. since
C. until
D. at
Correct Answer: A. within
Explanation: Within the next six months means before that six-month period ends. It is frequently used for deadlines.
24. The shop opens ___ sunrise.
A. on
B. in
C. at
D. by
Correct Answer: C. at
Explanation: Use at for specific points of time, including at sunrise, at sunset and at dawn.
25. She worked continuously ___ the night.
A. throughout
B. since
C. by
D. from
Correct Answer: A. throughout
Explanation: Throughout the night means during the whole night. It emphasizes the entire duration of a period.
26. He left the room ___ the lecture began.
A. until
B. before
C. since
D. during
Correct Answer: B. before
Explanation: Before indicates that one event happened earlier than another. The sentence places his departure earlier than the beginning of the lecture.
27. The report must be submitted ___ 5 p.m. tomorrow.
A. until
B. since
C. by
D. during
Correct Answer: C. by
Explanation: By 5 p.m. establishes the latest acceptable submission time. The work may be submitted earlier.
28. She has lived in Dhaka ___ 2015.
A. during
B. for
C. since
D. from
Correct Answer: C. since
Explanation: Use since with a starting point such as 2015. For eleven years would express the corresponding duration.
29. The meeting was postponed ___ the following day.
A. at
B. until
C. since
D. during
Correct Answer: B. until
Explanation: Until the following day indicates that the postponement extends to that time. Postpone until is a common construction.
30. He usually takes a short nap ___ lunchtime.
A. in
B. on
C. at
D. by
Correct Answer: C. at
Explanation: Use at with meal-time expressions such as at lunchtime and at dinnertime. Compare during lunch, which emphasizes the period in which something occurs.
Section 2: Prepositions of Place and Position (31–60)
31. The keys are ___ the table.
A. at
B. on
C. in
D. into
Correct Answer: B. on
Explanation: Use on for something resting on a surface. Example: The documents are on the desk.
32. The students are sitting ___ the classroom.
A. into
B. at
C. in
D. onto
Correct Answer: C. in
Explanation: Use in when something or someone is inside an enclosed space. Example: The files are in the cabinet.
33. He is waiting ___ the bus stop.
A. in
B. on
C. at
D. into
Correct Answer: C. at
Explanation: Use at for a particular point or location, such as at the bus stop, at the gate or at the entrance.
34. The picture is hanging ___ the wall.
A. at
B. in
C. on
D. over
Correct Answer: C. on
Explanation: Use on the wall when something is attached to or displayed on its surface. In the wall usually suggests something embedded inside it.
35. The village lies ___ two rivers.
A. among
B. between
C. beside
D. across
Correct Answer: B. between
Explanation: Between is typically used for two distinct people or things. It can also be used for more than two when the individual items are considered separately.
36. The teacher distributed the prizes ___ the five winners.
A. between
B. beside
C. among
D. across
Correct Answer: C. among
Explanation: Among is commonly used when referring to members of a group. The prizes were distributed within a group of five winners.
37. The bank is situated ___ the post office.
A. beside
B. into
C. across
D. beneath
Correct Answer: A. beside
Explanation: Beside means next to or at the side of. Do not confuse it with besides, which usually means in addition to.
38. A small bridge stands ___ the river.
A. through
B. over
C. into
D. beneath
Correct Answer: B. over
Explanation: Over indicates a position higher than something, often with space beneath it. A bridge commonly extends over a river.
39. The cat is hiding ___ the bed.
A. under
B. above
C. onto
D. across
Correct Answer: A. under
Explanation: Under means directly or generally below something. Beneath is a more formal alternative in many contexts.
40. The plane flew ___ the clouds.
A. among
B. between
C. above
D. inside
Correct Answer: C. above
Explanation: Above indicates a higher position. It does not necessarily imply direct vertical alignment.
41. The temperature remained ___ zero throughout the night.
A. below
B. beside
C. among
D. across
Correct Answer: A. below
Explanation: Below zero means lower than zero. The phrase is commonly used in descriptions of temperature.
42. The child stood ___ his parents.
A. between
B. among
C. through
D. beyond
Correct Answer: A. between
Explanation: Use between when the child is positioned in the space separating two parents. The number of people involved makes this usage straightforward.
43. The hotel is directly ___ the railway station.
A. opposite
B. within
C. beneath
D. among
Correct Answer: A. opposite
Explanation: Opposite means facing or situated on the other side of something. It does not require a preposition after it in this construction.
44. The river flows ___ the city.
A. through
B. among
C. beside
D. onto
Correct Answer: A. through
Explanation: Through expresses movement or extension from one side to another within an area. A river flowing through a city passes within its boundaries.
45. They built a fence ___ the garden.
A. around
B. through
C. between
D. beneath
Correct Answer: A. around
Explanation: Around means surrounding something. A fence around a garden marks or encloses its boundaries.
46. The restaurant is ___ the second floor.
A. at
B. in
C. on
D. into
Correct Answer: C. on
Explanation: Use on with floors of a building: on the first floor, on the third floor.
47. He lives ___ 25 Green Road.
A. at
B. in
C. on
D. into
Correct Answer: A. at
Explanation: At is commonly used with a specific street address. Use on with a street name and in with a city or country.
48. My uncle lives ___ Rajshahi.
A. at
B. on
C. in
D. into
Correct Answer: C. in
Explanation: Use in with cities, countries and larger geographical areas. Example: in Bangladesh, in Dhaka.
49. The notice is displayed ___ the entrance.
A. at
B. into
C. among
D. through
Correct Answer: A. at
Explanation: At the entrance identifies a particular location. On the entrance would generally be inappropriate unless referring to its physical surface.
50. The children ran ___ the playground.
A. among
B. across
C. beneath
D. beside
Correct Answer: B. across
Explanation: Across indicates movement from one side of an area to the other. Example: She walked across the field.
51. The lamp is hanging ___ the dining table.
A. under
B. among
C. into
D. beneath
Correct Answer: A. under
Explanation: Under describes a lower position relative to the table. Beneath can also be grammatically correct, but it is not offered as an option here.
52. The road runs ___ the two buildings.
A. between
B. among
C. through
D. beyond
Correct Answer: A. between
Explanation: Between identifies the space separating two distinct buildings. It is commonly used for roads, paths and boundaries between places.
53. The bird was perched ___ a branch.
A. in
B. on
C. into
D. through
Correct Answer: B. on
Explanation: Use on a branch when a bird is resting on its surface. In a tree generally refers to a position among its branches or foliage.
54. The documents are kept ___ a locked drawer.
A. at
B. on
C. in
D. over
Correct Answer: C. in
Explanation: Use in to express containment inside a drawer, box or cupboard.
55. The stadium is located ___ the outskirts of the city.
A. at
B. on
C. in
D. by
Correct Answer: B. on
Explanation: The established expression is on the outskirts of a city or town. It refers to the outer parts of a settlement.
56. The cottage stands ___ the foot of the hill.
A. in
B. at
C. on
D. by
Correct Answer: B. at
Explanation: The conventional expression is at the foot of the hill. Similarly, we say at the top of the stairs.
57. The climbers reached the summit ___ sunset.
A. in
B. at
C. on
D. within
Correct Answer: B. at
Explanation: Use at with sunset, sunrise and similar points in time. This sentence combines a place-related event with a time expression.
58. There is a narrow path ___ the house and the lake.
A. among
B. between
C. beside
D. across
Correct Answer: B. between
Explanation: Between identifies the position separating two places. The path lies in the space separating the house and the lake.
59. The monument is ___ the centre of the square.
A. at
B. on
C. in
D. into
Correct Answer: C. in
Explanation: The usual expression is in the centre of a place. Use at the centre when emphasizing a particular point or position.
60. The passengers gathered ___ the ticket counter.
A. at
B. into
C. through
D. across
Correct Answer: A. at
Explanation: At the ticket counter refers to a specific location where people gather or conduct transactions.
Section 3: Direction, Movement and Position (61–90)
61. He walked ___ the room and sat down.
A. into
B. onto
C. across
D. above
Correct Answer: A. into
Explanation: Into expresses movement from outside to inside. Compare in the room, which describes location rather than movement.
62. The boy jumped ___ the swimming pool.
A. in
B. into
C. at
D. over
Correct Answer: B. into
Explanation: Use into when movement enters a space or container. In can also describe a location, but into emphasizes the movement.
63. She climbed ___ the ladder carefully.
A. on
B. onto
C. over
D. across
Correct Answer: B. onto
Explanation: Onto expresses movement to a surface or position on something. On the ladder describes a location.
64. The thief escaped ___ the window.
A. through
B. across
C. above
D. beside
Correct Answer: A. through
Explanation: Through indicates passage within or by way of an opening. The thief used the window as the route of escape.
65. The horse jumped ___ the fence.
A. beneath
B. over
C. into
D. beside
Correct Answer: B. over
Explanation: Over is used when movement passes above an obstacle. Example: The athlete jumped over the hurdle.
66. The swimmer dived ___ the water.
A. onto
B. into
C. across
D. beyond
Correct Answer: B. into
Explanation: Dive into the water is the standard expression for entering the water by diving.
67. He drove ___ the tunnel.
A. through
B. above
C. between
D. beside
Correct Answer: A. through
Explanation: Through describes movement from one end of an enclosed passage to the other. A tunnel is a typical example.
68. The train passed ___ the bridge.
A. across
B. under
C. between
D. into
Correct Answer: A. across
Explanation: Across the bridge indicates movement from one side to the other. Under the bridge would mean passing beneath it.
69. The child ran ___ his mother when he saw her.
A. towards
B. beneath
C. among
D. within
Correct Answer: A. towards
Explanation: Towards indicates movement in the direction of someone or something. It does not necessarily mean the destination is reached.
70. The soldier marched ___ the enemy lines.
A. into
B. beside
C. among
D. above
Correct Answer: A. into
Explanation: Into emphasizes entering an area or position. Here, the soldier moves from outside the enemy lines to within them.
71. The road leads ___ the market.
A. to
B. at
C. on
D. in
Correct Answer: A. to
Explanation: Lead to means to provide a route to a destination. Example: This road leads to the airport.
72. She took the book ___ the shelf.
A. off
B. in
C. into
D. across
Correct Answer: A. off
Explanation: Off indicates removal from a surface. Take a book off the shelf means remove it from the shelf.
73. He fell ___ the bicycle and injured his knee.
A. of
B. off
C. from
D. out
Correct Answer: B. off
Explanation: The usual expression is fall off a bicycle. Fall from is also possible in some contexts, but off is the most natural choice here.
74. The cat sprang ___ the table.
A. onto
B. in
C. through
D. below
Correct Answer: A. onto
Explanation: Onto indicates movement to a surface. The cat moves from another position to the top of the table.
75. The passengers got ___ the bus at the final stop.
A. of
B. off
C. from
D. out
Correct Answer: B. off
Explanation: We generally say get off a bus, train, plane or bicycle. We say get out of a car or taxi.
76. She got ___ the car and closed the door.
A. off
B. out of
C. from
D. away
Correct Answer: B. out of
Explanation: Get out of a car is the standard expression for leaving it. Contrast this with get off a bus.
77. The plane took ___ at dawn.
A. of
B. off
C. out
D. away
Correct Answer: B. off
Explanation: Take off means to leave the ground and begin flying. It is a phrasal verb rather than a literal preposition of place.
78. The ship sailed ___ the harbour.
A. out of
B. off
C. over
D. beneath
Correct Answer: A. out of
Explanation: Out of expresses movement from inside to outside. The ship leaves the harbour and moves into open water.
79. The hikers walked ___ the mountain path.
A. along
B. into
C. above
D. between
Correct Answer: A. along
Explanation: Along describes movement following the length or course of something. Example: They walked along the riverbank.
80. He moved ___ the crowd to reach the exit.
A. through
B. above
C. beside
D. onto
Correct Answer: A. through
Explanation: Through the crowd means moving within the crowd from one point to another. It emphasizes passage among people.
81. The dog ran ___ the road to chase the ball.
A. across
B. through
C. into
D. above
Correct Answer: A. across
Explanation: Across the road means from one side to the other. The sentence describes movement across a surface.
82. The child crawled ___ the table to retrieve the toy.
A. under
B. above
C. onto
D. beyond
Correct Answer: A. under
Explanation: Under the table describes movement through the space below the table. The preposition refers to the route taken.
83. The boat drifted ___ the shore.
A. towards
B. into
C. through
D. above
Correct Answer: A. towards
Explanation: Towards the shore indicates movement in the shore's direction. It does not guarantee that the boat reaches it.
84. He came ___ the room carrying a briefcase.
A. out
B. out of
C. off
D. away
Correct Answer: B. out of
Explanation: Come out of the room means to leave the room. The phrase requires of after out when followed by the noun identifying the place.
85. The cyclist rode ___ the hill and reached the valley.
A. down
B. across
C. into
D. above
Correct Answer: A. down
Explanation: Down the hill indicates movement from a higher position to a lower one. The opposite expression is up the hill.
86. The climbers slowly made their way ___ the steep slope.
A. up
B. under
C. across
D. beside
Correct Answer: A. up
Explanation: Up the slope indicates movement towards a higher position. The preposition expresses direction rather than a fixed location.
87. The car went ___ the crossroads without stopping.
A. past
B. between
C. beneath
D. within
Correct Answer: A. past
Explanation: Past means beyond or alongside a point while continuing movement. Example: He walked past the bank.
88. The path extends ___ the forest to the village.
A. through
B. above
C. under
D. beside
Correct Answer: A. through
Explanation: Through the forest describes a route that passes within the forest. The destination is the village beyond it.
89. She leaned ___ the wall while waiting.
A. against
B. across
C. into
D. between
Correct Answer: A. against
Explanation: Against indicates contact with a surface, often for support. Example: He rested his bicycle against the wall.
90. The ball rolled ___ the stairs.
A. down
B. between
C. among
D. beside
Correct Answer: A. down
Explanation: Down the stairs describes movement from a higher level to a lower one. The expression is common with objects and people moving downward.
Section 4: Common Verb–Preposition Combinations (91–120)
91. She is interested ___ learning English.
A. on
B. at
C. in
D. for
Correct Answer: C. in
Explanation: The adjective interested takes in. Example: He is interested in banking and finance.
92. He insisted ___ paying the bill.
A. at
B. on
C. for
D. with
Correct Answer: B. on
Explanation: Insist on is followed by a noun or gerund. Example: She insisted on attending the meeting.
93. The committee consists ___ ten members.
A. in
B. from
C. of
D. with
Correct Answer: C. of
Explanation: Consist of identifies the components or members of something. Example: The team consists of five players.
94. Success depends ___ hard work and perseverance.
A. at
B. on
C. for
D. by
Correct Answer: B. on
Explanation: The verb depend takes on or, in some contexts, upon. Example: The result depends on your preparation.
95. You should apologize ___ your mistake.
A. for
B. with
C. at
D. from
Correct Answer: A. for
Explanation: Apologize for introduces the offence or mistake. Use apologize to to identify the person receiving the apology.
96. He apologized ___ his teacher for being late.
A. with
B. at
C. to
D. on
Correct Answer: C. to
Explanation: Apologize to someone for something is the standard construction. Here, to his teacher identifies the person receiving the apology.
97. She suffers ___ a serious illness.
A. of
B. from
C. with
D. by
Correct Answer: B. from
Explanation: Suffer from is commonly used with illnesses and conditions. Example: He suffers from asthma.
98. The officer accused him ___ corruption.
A. for
B. with
C. of
D. about
Correct Answer: C. of
Explanation: The correct construction is accuse someone of something. Example: The employee was accused of fraud.
99. I agree ___ your proposal.
A. to
B. with
C. on
D. at
Correct Answer: A. to
Explanation: Agree to a proposal, plan or suggestion means accept it. Use agree with a person or an opinion.
100. I completely agree ___ you on this matter.
A. to
B. with
C. for
D. at
Correct Answer: B. with
Explanation: Agree with someone expresses agreement with that person. The expression can also refer to agreeing with an opinion.
101. The manager congratulated her ___ her promotion.
A. at
B. for
C. on
D. with
Correct Answer: C. on
Explanation: The standard construction is congratulate someone on something. Example: They congratulated him on his success.
102. The police are looking ___ the matter.
A. after
B. into
C. for
D. at
Correct Answer: B. into
Explanation: Look into means investigate or examine. Example: The authorities are looking into the complaint.
103. She looks ___ her younger brother after school.
A. into
B. for
C. after
D. at
Correct Answer: C. after
Explanation: Look after means take care of. It differs from look into, which means investigate.
104. I am looking ___ my missing passport.
A. after
B. for
C. into
D. at
Correct Answer: B. for
Explanation: Look for means search for something or someone. It does not necessarily mean the object will be found.
105. The child takes ___ his father in appearance.
A. after
B. over
C. off
D. up
Correct Answer: A. after
Explanation: Take after means resemble a family member, especially in appearance or character. Example: She takes after her mother.
106. He succeeded ___ solving the problem.
A. at
B. in
C. on
D. with
Correct Answer: B. in
Explanation: Succeed in is followed by a noun or gerund. Example: She succeeded in passing the examination.
107. We should abstain ___ unhealthy habits.
A. of
B. with
C. from
D. for
Correct Answer: C. from
Explanation: Abstain from means deliberately refrain from something. Example: Candidates should abstain from unfair practices.
108. He prevented me ___ entering the room.
A. to
B. from
C. for
D. at
Correct Answer: B. from
Explanation: The structure is prevent someone from doing something. The verb after from takes the gerund form.
109. The child is afraid ___ the dark.
A. from
B. with

D. at
Correct Answer: C. of
Explanation: Afraid of is the standard adjective–preposition combination. Example: She is afraid of snakes.
110. We must comply ___ the rules.
A. to
B. by
C. with
D. at
Correct Answer: C. with
Explanation: Comply with means act in accordance with a rule, order or requirement. Example: All employees must comply with the regulations.
111. He objected ___ the proposed changes.
A. against
B. to
C. for
D. with
Correct Answer: B. to
Explanation: Object to means oppose something. When followed by a verb, to takes a gerund: object to paying extra fees.
112. The student is capable ___ solving difficult problems.
A. to

Explanation: Capable of is followed by a noun or gerund. Example: She is capable of managing the project.
113. He deprived the children ___ their basic rights.
A. from
B. of

D. by
Correct Answer: B. of
Explanation: The construction is deprive someone of something. Example: The policy deprived them of equal opportunities.
114. She relies ___ her colleagues for support.
A. in
B. at

D. for

Explanation: Rely on means depend on or trust someone or something. Rely upon is also correct.
115. The teacher warned the students ___ the dangers of cheating.
A. about
B. for
C. with
D. at
Correct Answer: A. about
Explanation: Warn someone about something alerts them to a danger or problem. Warn someone against doing something advises them not to do it.
116. She complained ___ the manager about the poor service.
A. to
B. with
C. at
D. from
Correct Answer: A. to
Explanation: Complain to someone about something identifies both the recipient and the subject of the complaint.
117. The patient recovered ___ the illness gradually.
A. of
B. from
C. with
D. by
Correct Answer: B. from
Explanation: Recover from is used with illnesses, injuries and setbacks. Example: He recovered from the accident.
118. He borrowed a pen ___ his colleague.
A. to
B. from
C. with
D. of
Correct Answer: B. from
Explanation: Borrow something from someone means receive something temporarily. The opposite action is lend something to someone.
119. She applied ___ the post of Senior Officer.
A. to
B. with
C. for
D. at
Correct Answer: C. for
Explanation: Apply for a job, position or scholarship. Use apply to an institution or organization: apply to the bank for a position.
120. The director agreed ___ the terms of the contract.
A. with
B. on
C. to
D. at
Correct Answer: C. to
Explanation: Agree to the terms means accept them. Agree on is used when parties reach a shared decision about a matter.
Section 5: Adjective–Preposition Combinations (121–150)
121. He is very good ___ mathematics.
A. in
B. at
C. on

240. He is confident ___ his ability to pass the examination.
A. for
B. at
C. of
D. with
Correct Answer: C. of
Explanation: Confident of is used when expressing certainty about an ability or outcome. Confident in his ability is also grammatical, but of is the intended answer here.
Section 9: Advanced Prepositional Usage and Error Detection (241–270)
241. Choose the grammatically correct sentence.
A. He is senior than me.
B. He is senior to me.
C. He is senior from me.
D. He is senior over me.
Correct Answer: B. He is senior to me.
Explanation: Senior to is the standard construction in formal English. The adjective senior does not normally take than.
242. Choose the correct sentence.
A. She is capable to do the job.
B. She is capable for doing the job.
C. She is capable of doing the job.
D. She is capable in do the job.
Correct Answer: C. She is capable of doing the job.
Explanation: Capable of takes a noun or gerund. The construction capable to do is incorrect in standard usage.
243. Choose the correct sentence.
A. We entered into the room.
B. We entered at the room.
C. We entered the room.
D. We entered in the room.
Correct Answer: C. We entered the room.
Explanation: Enter normally takes a direct object without a preposition. Say enter the room, but go into the room.
244. Choose the correct sentence.
A. He discussed about the proposal.
B. He discussed on the proposal.
C. He discussed the proposal.
D. He discussed over the proposal.
Correct Answer: C. He discussed the proposal.
Explanation: Discuss is normally transitive and does not require about, on or over before its object.
245. Choose the correct sentence.
A. She is good in mathematics.
B. She is good at mathematics.
C. She is good on mathematics.
D. She is good for mathematics.
Correct Answer: B. She is good at mathematics.
Explanation: Good at expresses skill in an activity or subject. Good for generally means beneficial or suitable.
246. Choose the correct sentence.
A. He has been absent since three days.
B. He has been absent for three days.
C. He has been absent during three days.
D. He has been absent from three days.
Correct Answer: B. He has been absent for three days.
Explanation: Use for with a duration and since with a starting point. Thus, for three days contrasts with since Monday.
247. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The book comprises of ten chapters.
B. The book comprises ten chapters.
C. The book comprises in ten chapters.
D. The book comprises with ten chapters.
Correct Answer: B. The book comprises ten chapters.
Explanation: Comprise normally takes a direct object without of. Although is comprised of is widely used, the direct construction is preferred in formal exam questions.
248. Choose the correct sentence.
A. He is married with a teacher.
B. He is married by a teacher.
C. He is married to a teacher.
D. He is married from a teacher.
Correct Answer: C. He is married to a teacher.
Explanation: The established expression is married to someone. This is a frequently tested adjective–preposition combination.
249. Choose the correct sentence.
A. I prefer coffee than tea.
B. I prefer coffee over than tea.
C. I prefer coffee to tea.
D. I prefer coffee from tea.
Correct Answer: C. I prefer coffee to tea.
Explanation: The standard comparative construction is prefer A to B. Do not confuse it with would rather A than B.
250. Choose the correct sentence.
A. He insisted to pay the bill.
B. He insisted on paying the bill.
C. He insisted for paying the bill.
D. He insisted at paying the bill.
Correct Answer: B. He insisted on paying the bill.
Explanation: Insist on + gerund is the correct construction when insisting on doing something. Example: She insisted on checking the figures herself.
251. The teacher divided the class ___ four groups.
A. in
B. into
C. among
D. between
Correct Answer: B. into
Explanation: Divide something into groups describes the process of separating a whole into parts. Among is used when distributing something within a group.
252. The money was divided ___ the four brothers.
A. between
B. among
C. across
D. through
Correct Answer: B. among
Explanation: Among is the conventional choice for distributing something within a group of four. Between can also be used for more than two clearly distinct individuals, but among is the intended general-group construction here.
253. He was accused ___ having leaked the confidential information.
A. for
B. with
C. of
D. about
Correct Answer: C. of
Explanation: Accuse someone of doing something takes of followed by a gerund. Example: She was accused of falsifying records.
254. The new regulations came ___ effect last month.
A. to
B. into
C. in
D. at
Correct Answer: B. into
Explanation: Come into effect means begin to operate or be enforced. It is a fixed expression frequently used in administrative and legal writing.
255. He is blind ___ one eye.
A. on
B. at
C. in
D. of
Correct Answer: C. in
Explanation: The conventional expression is blind in one eye. The preposition of appears in other expressions, such as deaf of one ear in older or less common usage.
256. The accused was acquitted ___ the charge of theft.
A. from
B. with
C. of
D. by
Correct Answer: C. of
Explanation: Acquitted of a charge is the standard legal construction. Example: The court acquitted him of all charges.
257. The committee arrived ___ a unanimous decision.
A. at
B. to
C. on
D. for
Correct Answer: A. at
Explanation: Arrive at a decision means reach a conclusion. Use arrive at for a decision or conclusion, but arrive in or arrive at for geographical locations depending on their size.
258. The company has embarked ___ an ambitious expansion programme.
A. at
B. in
C. on
D. to
Correct Answer: C. on
Explanation: Embark on means begin a significant project or course of action. Example: The government embarked on an economic reform programme.
259. The employee was entrusted ___ the responsibility of managing the accounts.
A. by
B. to
C. with
D. for
Correct Answer: C. with
Explanation: Entrust someone with something means assign them responsibility for it. The alternative structure is entrust something to someone.
260. The officer was entrusted ___ the confidential documents.
A. with
B. by
C. for
D. at
Correct Answer: A. with
Explanation: Entrusted with introduces the responsibility or object placed in someone's care. Example: She was entrusted with sensitive financial records.
261. The manager presided ___ the meeting.
A. on
B. at
C. over
D. for
Correct Answer: C. over
Explanation: Preside over means chair or lead a meeting or formal gathering. Example: The chairman presided over the annual meeting.
262. The witness testified ___ the accused.
A. for
B. to
C. with
D. by
Correct Answer: A. for
Explanation: Testify for someone means give testimony supporting that person. Testify against someone means provide testimony adverse to that person.
263. The witness testified ___ the truth of the statement.
A. for
B. to
C. against
D. with
Correct Answer: B. to
Explanation: Testify to something means provide evidence or state that it is true. Example: Several witnesses testified to the accuracy of the report.
264. His actions are contrary ___ the rules.
A. with
B. from
C. to
D. against
Correct Answer: C. to
Explanation: Contrary to means in conflict with or opposite to something. Example: The decision was contrary to the established policy.
265. The accused was released ___ bail.
A. in
B. on
C. at
D. by
Correct Answer: B. on
Explanation: The standard legal expression is released on bail. Similarly, someone may be granted bail or released without bail, depending on the circumstances.
266. The two companies entered ___ a partnership agreement.
A. into
B. in
C. at
D. on
Correct Answer: A. into
Explanation: Enter into an agreement means formally make or undertake an agreement. Contrast this with enter an office, which normally takes no preposition.
267. The committee is composed ___ experts from different fields.
A. from
B. with
C. of
D. by
Correct Answer: C. of
Explanation: Be composed of identifies the members or elements that make up a group. Example: The panel is composed of five specialists.
268. The new law is applicable ___ all financial institutions.
A. for
B. with
C. to
D. at
Correct Answer: C. to
Explanation: Applicable to means relevant to or capable of being applied to something. Example: The rule is applicable to all applicants.
269. He was preoccupied ___ thoughts of the upcoming examination.
A. by
B. with
C. at
D. from
Correct Answer: B. with
Explanation: Preoccupied with means absorbed in or dominated by particular thoughts or concerns. Example: She was preoccupied with the results.
270. The decision rests ___ the discretion of the authority.
A. at
B. on
C. in
D. with
Correct Answer: D. with
Explanation: Rest with someone means be the responsibility or authority of that person. The phrase rest on more commonly means depend on a particular factor.

278. The employee was held accountable ___ the financial loss.

280. The company has embarked ___ a comprehensive restructuring programme.

283. The officer was commended ___ his outstanding performance.
