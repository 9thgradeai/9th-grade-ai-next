Section 1: Common and High-Frequency Idioms (1–25)
1. What does the idiom “at sixes and sevens” mean?
A. In perfect order
B. In a state of confusion
C. In great excitement
D. In complete agreement
Correct Answer: B. In a state of confusion
Explanation: The idiom means being confused or disorganized. Example: The office was at sixes and sevens after the manager resigned.
2. What is the meaning of “once in a blue moon”?
A. Every month
B. At the right moment
C. Very rarely
D. Unexpectedly
Correct Answer: C. Very rarely
Explanation: It refers to something that happens infrequently. Example: He visits his hometown once in a blue moon.
3. Choose the meaning of “a blessing in disguise.”
A. Something that appears bad but turns out beneficial
B. A reward for good behaviour
C. A secret religious ceremony
D. An unexpected punishment
Correct Answer: A. Something that appears bad but turns out beneficial
Explanation: An apparently unfortunate event may eventually produce a good result. Example: Losing that job was a blessing in disguise because she found a better one.
4. What does “to smell a rat” mean?
A. To detect a bad odour
B. To accuse someone publicly
C. To discover a hidden object
D. To suspect something is wrong
Correct Answer: D. To suspect something is wrong
Explanation: This idiom means to become suspicious that something dishonest or unusual is happening. Example: I smelled a rat when he refused to explain the transaction.
5. “To add fuel to the fire” means—
A. To solve a difficult problem
B. To make a bad situation worse
C. To encourage peaceful discussion
D. To start a new project
Correct Answer: B. To make a bad situation worse
Explanation: The expression describes an action that intensifies an existing conflict or problem. Example: His insulting remarks added fuel to the fire.
6. What does “to hit the nail on the head” mean?
A. To make an accurate point
B. To work very slowly
C. To make a serious mistake
D. To avoid a difficult question
Correct Answer: A. To make an accurate point
Explanation: It means identifying the exact cause of a problem or stating something precisely. Example: Your analysis hit the nail on the head.
7. The idiom “to spill the beans” means—
A. To waste food
B. To create unnecessary trouble
C. To reveal a secret
D. To disclose financial losses only
Correct Answer: C. To reveal a secret
Explanation: It means disclosing confidential information, intentionally or accidentally. Example: She spilled the beans about the surprise party.
8. What is meant by “to beat around the bush”?
A. To work in a garden
B. To defeat an opponent
C. To discuss a subject thoroughly
D. To avoid coming directly to the point
Correct Answer: D. To avoid coming directly to the point
Explanation: The idiom describes speaking indirectly instead of addressing the main issue. Example: Stop beating around the bush and tell me what happened.
9. “To burn the midnight oil” means—
A. To waste electricity at night
B. To work or study late into the night
C. To celebrate until midnight
D. To finish work early
Correct Answer: B. To work or study late into the night
Explanation: This expression refers to staying up late to work or study. Example: She burned the midnight oil before the recruitment examination.
10. What does “to turn over a new leaf” mean?
A. To change one's behaviour for the better
B. To change one's profession frequently
C. To abandon an unfinished task
D. To repeat an old mistake
Correct Answer: A. To change one's behaviour for the better
Explanation: It means making a fresh start by adopting better habits or conduct. Example: After the warning, he turned over a new leaf.
11. “To make both ends meet” means—
A. To finish two tasks simultaneously
B. To settle a dispute
C. To live within one's income
D. To earn an unusually large profit
Correct Answer: C. To live within one's income
Explanation: The idiom means having just enough money to cover one's expenses. Example: Many families struggle to make both ends meet.
12. What does “to bury the hatchet” mean?
A. To hide evidence of a crime
B. To postpone an argument
C. To avoid meeting an opponent
D. To end a quarrel and make peace
Correct Answer: D. To end a quarrel and make peace
Explanation: The expression refers to ending hostility between people. Example: The two colleagues finally buried the hatchet.
13. The phrase “a storm in a teacup” means—
A. A serious natural disaster
B. A great deal of fuss over a trivial matter
C. A sudden financial crisis
D. An unexpected change of plan
Correct Answer: B. A great deal of fuss over a trivial matter
Explanation: It describes an exaggerated reaction to something unimportant. Example: Their argument over a minor error was a storm in a teacup.
14. What does “to keep an eye on” mean?
A. To watch or monitor carefully
B. To ignore deliberately
C. To criticize constantly
D. To protect someone secretly
Correct Answer: A. To watch or monitor carefully
Explanation: It means observing someone or something attentively. Example: Please keep an eye on the documents while I am away.

A. To accept information as absolutely true
B. To reject every suggestion
C. To treat information with some scepticism
D. To make a difficult decision
Correct Answer: C. To treat information with some scepticism
Explanation: The expression advises against believing something completely without verification. Example: Take online rumours with a grain of salt.

Explanation: The expression advises against wasting time regretting an irreversible event. Example: The deadline has passed, so there is no use crying over spilt milk.
22. What does “to be on cloud nine” mean?
A. To be extremely happy
B. To be deeply confused
C. To feel physically unwell

Correct Answer: A. To be extremely happy
Explanation: It describes a state of great happiness or excitement. Example: She was on cloud nine after receiving the appointment letter.

Correct Answer: C. To take shortcuts, often at the expense of quality

24. What does “to get out of hand” mean?

B. To refuse to help someone

Correct Answer: D. To become uncontrollable

25. The expression “to put all one's eggs in one basket” means—
