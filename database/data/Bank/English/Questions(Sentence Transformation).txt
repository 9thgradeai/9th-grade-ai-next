Section 1: Affirmative and Negative Transformation (1–30)
1. Choose the correct negative form of “Everybody loves freedom.”
A. Nobody loves freedom.
B. There is nobody who does not love freedom.
C. Everybody does not love freedom.
D. Nobody does not love freedom.
Correct Answer: B. There is nobody who does not love freedom.
Explanation: A universal affirmative can be transformed into a negative construction without changing its meaning. “There is nobody who does not…” preserves the original assertion that all people love freedom.
2. Choose the affirmative form of “He is not dishonest.”
A. He is honest.
B. He is never honest.
C. He is dishonest.
D. He is not honest.
Correct Answer: A. He is honest.
Explanation: “Not dishonest” generally conveys the affirmative meaning “honest” in standard transformation exercises. The transformation removes the negative prefix and the negation.
3. Choose the negative form of “Only the brave deserve the fair.”
A. Nobody deserves the fair.
B. The brave do not deserve the fair.
C. None but the brave deserve the fair.
D. None but the brave do not deserve the fair.
Correct Answer: C. None but the brave deserve the fair.
Explanation: “Only” can often be replaced by “none but” when referring to people. The expression means that no one except the brave deserves the fair.
4. Choose the affirmative form of “He never tells a lie.”
A. He always tells a lie.
B. He always speaks the truth.
C. He does not speak the truth.
D. He seldom tells the truth.
Correct Answer: B. He always speaks the truth.
Explanation: “Never tells a lie” means that he consistently speaks the truth. The transformation preserves the original meaning.
5. Choose the negative form of “As soon as he saw the police, he ran away.”
A. No sooner did he see the police than he ran away.
B. No sooner had he seen the police than he ran away.
C. He did not see the police before running away.
D. He ran away because he never saw the police.
Correct Answer: B. No sooner had he seen the police than he ran away.
Explanation: “No sooner…than” expresses that one event followed another immediately. When the first event precedes a past simple event, the conventional pattern is “No sooner had + subject + past participle + than + past simple.”
6. Choose the negative form of “He is always punctual.”
A. He is never late.
B. He is not always punctual.
C. He is sometimes late.
D. He is rarely punctual.
Correct Answer: A. He is never late.
Explanation: “Always punctual” means that he is never late. Option B is weaker because “not always punctual” allows him to be late sometimes.
7. Choose the affirmative form of “Nobody can deny his honesty.”
A. Everyone must deny his honesty.
B. His honesty is undeniable.
C. His honesty cannot be denied by nobody.
D. Nobody believes in his honesty.
Correct Answer: B. His honesty is undeniable.
Explanation: “Undeniable” means impossible to deny. Replacing the negative construction with this adjective preserves the meaning.
8. Choose the negative form of “He is too weak to walk.”
A. He is so weak that he can walk.
B. He is so weak that he cannot walk.
C. He is not weak enough to walk.
D. He is strong enough to walk.
Correct Answer: B. He is so weak that he cannot walk.
Explanation: The pattern “too + adjective + to-infinitive” can be transformed into “so + adjective + that + subject + cannot” when it expresses inability.
9. Choose the affirmative form of “He did not fail to keep his promise.”
A. He broke his promise.
B. He kept his promise.
C. He forgot his promise.
D. He refused to keep his promise.
Correct Answer: B. He kept his promise.
Explanation: “Did not fail to” means “succeeded in” or simply “did.” Therefore, “did not fail to keep his promise” means “kept his promise.”
10. Choose the negative form of “Everyone admires a truthful person.”
A. No one admires a truthful person.
B. There is no one who does not admire a truthful person.
C. Everyone dislikes a truthful person.
D. There is someone who admires no truthful person.
Correct Answer: B. There is no one who does not admire a truthful person.
Explanation: The original sentence makes a general statement about everyone. The “no one who does not” construction expresses the same universal idea.
11. Choose the affirmative form of “He is not incapable of doing the work.”
A. He is incapable of doing the work.
B. He is capable of doing the work.
C. He cannot do the work.
D. He refuses to do the work.
Correct Answer: B. He is capable of doing the work.
Explanation: “Incapable” means unable. Removing the negative prefix and the external negation produces “capable,” which retains the intended meaning.
12. Choose the negative form of “Every mother loves her child.”
A. No mother loves her child.
B. There is no mother who does not love her child.
C. Every mother does not love her child.
D. Some mothers never love their children.
Correct Answer: B. There is no mother who does not love her child.
Explanation: This is the conventional meaning-preserving negative transformation of a universal affirmative. “Every mother does not…” may imply that not every mother loves her child, so it is unsuitable here.
13. Choose the affirmative form of “He never disobeys his parents.”
A. He always obeys his parents.
B. He sometimes disobeys his parents.
C. He rarely obeys his parents.
D. He does not always obey his parents.
Correct Answer: A. He always obeys his parents.
Explanation: “Never disobeys” means that he obeys on every relevant occasion. The affirmative sentence expresses the same habitual action.
14. Choose the negative form of “Only a fool would believe this story.”
A. A fool would never believe this story.
B. None but a fool would believe this story.
C. Nobody would believe this story.
D. Everyone except a fool would believe this story.
Correct Answer: B. None but a fool would believe this story.
Explanation: “None but” means “no one except.” It is a standard substitute for “only” when referring to people.
15. Choose the affirmative form of “There is no rule without an exception.”
A. Every rule has an exception.
B. No rule has an exception.
C. Every exception is a rule.
D. Rules never have exceptions.
Correct Answer: A. Every rule has an exception.
Explanation: The negative construction means that each rule has at least one exception. The affirmative sentence states this directly.
16. Choose the negative form of “He always remembers his duties.”
A. He never remembers his duties.
B. He never forgets his duties.
C. He does not remember his duties.
D. He sometimes forgets all his duties.
Correct Answer: B. He never forgets his duties.
Explanation: “Always remembers” is equivalent in meaning to “never forgets.” This transformation changes the expression without reversing the intended meaning.
17. Choose the affirmative form of “He cannot but accept the offer.”
A. He can refuse the offer.
B. He must accept the offer.
C. He may reject the offer.
D. He does not accept the offer.
Correct Answer: B. He must accept the offer.
Explanation: “Cannot but” means “cannot help but” or “must.” It indicates that accepting the offer is unavoidable.
18. Choose the negative form of “All men are mortal.”
A. No man is mortal.
B. There is no man who is not mortal.
C. Some men are immortal.
D. All men are not mortal.
Correct Answer: B. There is no man who is not mortal.
Explanation: The original sentence states that every man is mortal. “There is no man who is not mortal” expresses the same universal claim.
19. Choose the affirmative form of “It is not impossible to solve the problem.”
A. The problem is impossible to solve.
B. The problem is solvable.
C. Nobody can solve the problem.
D. The problem cannot be solved.
Correct Answer: B. The problem is solvable.
Explanation: “Not impossible” means “possible.” The adjective “solvable” concisely expresses that the problem can be solved.
20. Choose the negative form of “He is the best player in the team.”
A. No other player in the team is as good as he is.
B. Every player is better than he is.
C. No player can play the game.
D. He is not a good player.
Correct Answer: A. No other player in the team is as good as he is.
Explanation: A superlative can be transformed into a negative comparison using “no other…as…as.” The comparison retains the claim that he is the best.
21. Choose the affirmative form of “Nobody was absent from the meeting.”
A. Everyone was present at the meeting.
B. Everyone was absent from the meeting.
C. Someone was absent from the meeting.
D. No one attended the meeting.
Correct Answer: A. Everyone was present at the meeting.
Explanation: “Nobody was absent” means that not a single person was missing. The equivalent affirmative statement is that everyone was present.
22. Choose the negative form of “He is always willing to help others.”
A. He is never willing to help others.
B. He is never unwilling to help others.
C. He is unwilling to help everyone.
D. He does not help others.
Correct Answer: B. He is never unwilling to help others.
Explanation: “Always willing” means “never unwilling.” This transformation uses a negative adjective while retaining the original positive meaning.
23. Choose the affirmative form of “He did not hesitate to speak the truth.”
A. He spoke the truth without hesitation.
B. He refused to speak the truth.
C. He was afraid to speak the truth.
D. He did not speak the truth.
Correct Answer: A. He spoke the truth without hesitation.
Explanation: “Did not hesitate to” means “acted without hesitation.” The sentence therefore asserts that he spoke the truth readily.
24. Choose the negative form of “As soon as the bell rang, the students left.”
A. No sooner did the bell ring than the students left.
B. No sooner had the bell rung than the students left.
C. The students did not leave when the bell rang.
D. The bell rang after the students had left.
Correct Answer: B. No sooner had the bell rung than the students left.
Explanation: “No sooner had…than” is the standard pattern for expressing two closely successive past events. The first clause takes the past perfect, and the second takes the past simple.
25. Choose the affirmative form of “He is not unwilling to cooperate.”
A. He refuses to cooperate.
B. He is willing to cooperate.
C. He is unable to cooperate.
D. He never cooperates.
Correct Answer: B. He is willing to cooperate.
Explanation: “Unwilling” means reluctant. The double-negative construction “not unwilling” indicates willingness, though sometimes with less enthusiasm than a direct affirmative.
26. Choose the negative form of “Every cloud has a silver lining.”
A. No cloud has a silver lining.
B. There is no cloud that does not have a silver lining.
C. Every cloud is completely dark.
D. Some clouds have no silver lining.
Correct Answer: B. There is no cloud that does not have a silver lining.
Explanation: The sentence is a proverb expressing that every difficult situation has some positive aspect. The negative transformation preserves the universal meaning.
27. Choose the affirmative form of “He never fails to attend class.”
A. He sometimes attends class.
B. He always attends class.
C. He rarely attends class.
D. He avoids attending class.
Correct Answer: B. He always attends class.
Explanation: “Never fails to attend” means “always attends.” The negative verb “fails” does not make the overall statement negative.
28. Choose the negative form of “He is the wisest man in the village.”
A. No other man in the village is as wise as he is.
B. Every man in the village is wiser than he is.
C. No man in the village is wise.
D. He is not wiser than any man in the village.
Correct Answer: A. No other man in the village is as wise as he is.
Explanation: The superlative “wisest” is equivalent to saying that no other person reaches the same degree of wisdom. The negative comparison preserves the original ranking.
29. Choose the affirmative form of “He cannot avoid making mistakes.”
A. He is unable to make mistakes.
B. He must make mistakes.
C. He never makes mistakes.
D. He should avoid all mistakes.
Correct Answer: B. He must make mistakes.
Explanation: “Cannot avoid” means that something is unavoidable. In this context, “must make mistakes” conveys that mistakes cannot be prevented.
30. Choose the negative form of “Only an honest person can earn his trust.”
A. An honest person cannot earn his trust.
B. None but an honest person can earn his trust.
C. Every dishonest person can earn his trust.
D. Nobody can earn his trust.
Correct Answer: B. None but an honest person can earn his trust.
Explanation: “None but” means “no one except.” It preserves the restriction expressed by “only,” without suggesting that an honest person is unable to earn his trust.
Section 2: Assertive, Interrogative and Exclamatory Transformation (31–60)
31. Choose the interrogative form of “Nobody can deny the importance of education.”
A. Can anybody deny the importance of education?
B. Who can deny the importance of education?
C. Why can anybody deny the importance of education?
D. Does nobody understand the importance of education?
Correct Answer: B. Who can deny the importance of education?
Explanation: A rhetorical question can express the same meaning as an assertive statement. “Who can deny…?” implies that nobody can deny it.
32. Choose the assertive form of “Is there anyone who does not love their mother?”
A. Everyone hates their mother.
B. Nobody loves their mother.
C. Everyone loves their mother.
D. Some people dislike their mothers.
Correct Answer: C. Everyone loves their mother.
Explanation: This rhetorical question expects the answer “No.” Its intended meaning is that everyone loves their mother.
33. Choose the exclamatory form of “The scenery is very beautiful.”
A. How beautiful the scenery is!
B. What scenery is beautiful?
C. How the scenery is beautiful?
D. The scenery is not beautiful!
Correct Answer: A. How beautiful the scenery is!
Explanation: An adjective can be emphasized using “How + adjective + subject + verb!” The sentence expresses admiration for the scenery.
34. Choose the assertive form of “What a wonderful idea it is!”
A. It is not a wonderful idea.
B. It is a very wonderful idea.
C. Is it a wonderful idea?
D. What is a wonderful idea?
Correct Answer: B. It is a very wonderful idea.
Explanation: An exclamatory sentence beginning with “What a…” can be converted into an assertive sentence using “very.” The emotional emphasis is reduced, but the core meaning remains.
35. Choose the interrogative form of “Everyone wishes to be happy.”
A. Who does not wish to be happy?
B. Does everyone wish to be unhappy?
C. Who wishes to be unhappy?
D. Why does everyone wish to be happy?
Correct Answer: A. Who does not wish to be happy?
Explanation: A rhetorical negative question can express a universal affirmative. “Who does not…?” implies that everyone does.
36. Choose the assertive form of “How foolish I have been!”
A. I have not been foolish.
B. I have been very foolish.
C. Have I been foolish?
D. I will be foolish.
Correct Answer: B. I have been very foolish.
Explanation: “How + adjective” introduces an exclamatory sentence. In the assertive form, “how foolish” becomes “very foolish.”
37. Choose the exclamatory form of “It is a very pleasant surprise.”
A. What a pleasant surprise it is!
B. Is it a pleasant surprise?
C. How it is a pleasant surprise!
D. It is not a pleasant surprise!
Correct Answer: A. What a pleasant surprise it is!
Explanation: For an exclamatory sentence with a singular countable noun, use “What a/an + adjective + noun + subject + verb!” The noun “surprise” takes the article “a.”
38. Choose the interrogative form of “Nothing is more precious than time.”
A. Is anything as precious as time?
B. Is time less precious than anything?
C. Why is time precious?
D. Is nothing more precious than time?
Correct Answer: A. Is anything as precious as time?
Explanation: The statement means that nothing equals time in preciousness. A rhetorical question using “Is anything as precious as time?” expresses the same idea.
39. Choose the assertive form of “Who does not know him?”
A. Nobody knows him.
B. Everybody knows him.
C. Somebody does not know him.
D. Does everybody know him?
Correct Answer: B. Everybody knows him.
Explanation: This is a rhetorical question implying universal familiarity. The corresponding assertive form is “Everybody knows him.”
40. Choose the exclamatory form of “The night is very beautiful.”
A. What beautiful is the night!
B. How beautiful the night is!
C. How the night is beautiful!
D. Is the night beautiful?
Correct Answer: B. How beautiful the night is!
Explanation: Use “How” to emphasize an adjective or adverb in an exclamation. The usual structure is “How + adjective + subject + verb!”
41. Choose the assertive form of “Alas! The old man is dead.”
A. The old man may be dead.
B. It is regrettable that the old man is dead.
C. The old man is not dead.
D. Is the old man dead?
Correct Answer: B. It is regrettable that the old man is dead.
Explanation: “Alas!” expresses sorrow or regret. An assertive transformation can convey the same feeling through “It is regrettable that…”
42. Choose the exclamatory form of “It is a very exciting match.”
A. What an exciting match it is!
B. How an exciting match it is!
C. Is it an exciting match?
D. What exciting is the match!
Correct Answer: A. What an exciting match it is!
Explanation: “Match” is a singular countable noun, and “exciting” begins with a vowel sound. Therefore, the correct exclamatory structure uses “What an…”
43. Choose the interrogative form of “There is no use crying over spilt milk.”
A. Is there any use crying over spilt milk?
B. Is crying always useful?
C. Why should milk be spilt?
D. Is there no milk to cry over?
Correct Answer: A. Is there any use crying over spilt milk?
Explanation: The original proverb means that regretting an irreversible event is pointless. The rhetorical question “Is there any use…?” conveys the same meaning.
44. Choose the assertive form of “Would that I were a king!”
A. I am a king.
B. I wish that I were a king.
C. I do not want to be a king.
D. Will I become a king?
Correct Answer: B. I wish that I were a king.
Explanation: “Would that…” expresses a wish, often one that is unreal or unlikely. It can be transformed into “I wish that…”
45. Choose the exclamatory form of “The flower is extremely beautiful.”
A. What beautiful the flower is!
B. How beautiful the flower is!
C. How the flower is beautiful!
D. What is the flower beautiful?
Correct Answer: B. How beautiful the flower is!
Explanation: The adjective “beautiful” is emphasized with “How.” The correct order is “How + adjective + subject + verb!”
46. Choose the assertive form of “Bravo! You have done an excellent job.”
A. You have not done a good job.
B. It is praiseworthy that you have done an excellent job.
C. Have you done an excellent job?
D. You should do a job.
Correct Answer: B. It is praiseworthy that you have done an excellent job.
Explanation: “Bravo!” expresses approval or admiration. “It is praiseworthy that…” converts this emotional expression into an assertive construction.
47. Choose the interrogative form of “It is impossible to escape death.”
A. Is it possible to escape death?
B. Why is death impossible?
C. Is death unable to escape?
D. Can death escape everyone?
Correct Answer: A. Is it possible to escape death?
Explanation: The rhetorical question asks whether escaping death is possible, expecting a negative answer. It therefore conveys the same meaning as the original assertion.
48. Choose the assertive form of “What a terrible storm it was!”
A. It was a very terrible storm.
B. Was it a terrible storm?
C. It was not a terrible storm.
D. What was the terrible storm?
Correct Answer: A. It was a very terrible storm.
Explanation: “What a…!” expresses strong feeling about a noun. The assertive form uses “very” before the adjective to retain the emphasis.
49. Choose the exclamatory form of “The child is very innocent.”
A. How innocent the child is!
B. How the child is innocent!
C. What innocent the child is!
D. Is the child innocent?
Correct Answer: A. How innocent the child is!
Explanation: The adjective “innocent” follows “How” in the exclamatory pattern. No article is needed because the construction emphasizes an adjective rather than a noun.

B. How a difficult examination it was!
C. What difficult was the examination!
D. Was the examination difficult?
Correct Answer: A. What a difficult examination it was!
Explanation: The exclamatory pattern “What a/an + adjective + singular countable noun + subject + verb!” is appropriate here. “Examination” is singular and begins with a vowel sound, but the article depends on the noun's sound: “a difficult examination.”

D. Why does nobody like an honest person?

55. Choose the exclamatory form of “It is a very noble deed.”
A. What a noble deed it is!
B. How a noble deed it is!
C. What noble is the deed!
D. Is it a noble deed?
Correct Answer: A. What a noble deed it is!
Explanation: “Deed” is a singular countable noun, so the correct construction is “What a + adjective + noun + subject + verb!”
56. Choose the assertive form of “Would that I could fly!”

B. I wish that I could fly.
C. I will fly tomorrow.
D. I cannot wish to fly.
Correct Answer: B. I wish that I could fly.
Explanation: “Would that…” expresses a wish. The modal “could” is retained because the wish concerns an ability the speaker does not currently possess.

Section 3: Simple, Complex and Compound Sentences (61–90)
61. Choose the simple form of “As he was ill, he could not attend the meeting.”
A. Being ill, he could not attend the meeting.
B. He was ill, so he could not attend the meeting.
C. He could not attend the meeting because he was ill.
D. He was ill and he could not attend the meeting.
Correct Answer: A. Being ill, he could not attend the meeting.
Explanation: A simple sentence contains one independent clause. The subordinate clause “as he was ill” is reduced to the participial phrase “being ill.”
62. Choose the complex form of “In spite of his poverty, he is honest.”
A. He is poor but honest.
B. Though he is poor, he is honest.
C. He is honest despite his poverty.
D. He is poor and honest.
Correct Answer: B. Though he is poor, he is honest.
Explanation: A complex sentence contains an independent clause and at least one subordinate clause. “Though he is poor” is a subordinate concessive clause.
63. Choose the compound form of “Being tired, she went to bed early.”
A. As she was tired, she went to bed early.
B. She was tired, so she went to bed early.
C. Because of her tiredness, she went to bed early.
D. Tired by work, she went to bed early.
Correct Answer: B. She was tired, so she went to bed early.
Explanation: A compound sentence joins independent clauses using a coordinating conjunction. Here, “so” connects the cause and its result.
64. Choose the simple form of “When the sun set, the travellers stopped.”
A. At sunset, the travellers stopped.
B. When sunset came, the travellers stopped.
C. The sun set, and the travellers stopped.
D. The travellers stopped when the sun had set.
Correct Answer: A. At sunset, the travellers stopped.
Explanation: The subordinate time clause “when the sun set” becomes the prepositional phrase “at sunset.” The resulting sentence has one independent clause.
65. Choose the complex form of “Work hard to succeed.”
A. Work hard and succeed.
B. If you work hard, you will succeed.
C. Work hard for success.
D. Work hard; success will follow.
Correct Answer: B. If you work hard, you will succeed.
Explanation: The complex form uses the subordinate conditional clause “if you work hard.” It makes the implied condition explicit.
66. Choose the compound form of “Though he is rich, he is unhappy.”
A. In spite of being rich, he is unhappy.
B. He is rich, but he is unhappy.
C. Despite his riches, he is unhappy.
D. Although rich, he is unhappy.
Correct Answer: B. He is rich, but he is unhappy.
Explanation: “But” joins two independent clauses and expresses contrast. This produces a compound sentence without changing the central meaning.
67. Choose the simple form of “He finished his work before he went home.”
A. Before going home, he finished his work.
B. He finished his work, and he went home.
C. He went home after he had finished his work.
D. He finished his work because he went home.
Correct Answer: A. Before going home, he finished his work.
Explanation: The subordinate clause “before he went home” is reduced to the gerund phrase “before going home.” The sentence retains the sequence of events.
68. Choose the complex form of “Despite his best efforts, he failed.”
A. He tried hard, but he failed.
B. Although he tried his best, he failed.
C. He failed despite trying hard.
D. He tried hard and failed.
Correct Answer: B. Although he tried his best, he failed.
Explanation: “Although he tried his best” is a subordinate concessive clause. It expresses the contrast between his efforts and the outcome.
69. Choose the compound form of “Being hungry, the boy ate quickly.”
A. As the boy was hungry, he ate quickly.
B. The boy was hungry, so he ate quickly.
C. Because of hunger, the boy ate quickly.
D. Hungry, the boy ate quickly.
Correct Answer: B. The boy was hungry, so he ate quickly.
Explanation: The compound construction contains two independent clauses joined by “so.” The conjunction indicates that hunger led to quick eating.
70. Choose the simple form of “He is so weak that he cannot walk.”
A. He is too weak to walk.
B. He is weak, so he cannot walk.
C. He cannot walk because he is weak.
D. He is weak and cannot walk.
Correct Answer: A. He is too weak to walk.
Explanation: The “so…that…cannot” structure expressing inability can be transformed into “too + adjective + to-infinitive.” The two forms convey the same limitation.
71. Choose the complex form of “He worked hard and passed the examination.”
A. Working hard, he passed the examination.
B. He passed the examination because he worked hard.
C. He worked hard, so he passed the examination.
D. He worked hard to pass the examination.
Correct Answer: B. He passed the examination because he worked hard.
Explanation: The clause “because he worked hard” is subordinate and expresses reason. The main clause is “He passed the examination.”
72. Choose the compound form of “If you do not hurry, you will miss the train.”
A. Unless you hurry, you will miss the train.
B. Hurry, or you will miss the train.
C. Without hurrying, you will miss the train.
D. If you hurry, you will catch the train.
Correct Answer: B. Hurry, or you will miss the train.
Explanation: “Hurry, or…” expresses the warning contained in the original conditional sentence. The two independent clauses form a compound sentence.
73. Choose the simple form of “As soon as he reached the station, the train departed.”
A. On reaching the station, he found that the train had departed.
B. He reached the station, and the train departed.

Explanation: The “so…that…cannot” construction can be reduced to “too + adjective + to-infinitive.” This is a common sentence-transformation pattern.

Section 10: Mixed Sentence Transformation and Exam Challenge (271–300)
271. Choose the correct transformation of “He is so honest that nobody suspects him.”

C. He is so honest that everybody suspects him.
D. He is too honest for anybody to suspect him.
Correct Answer: D. He is too honest for anybody to suspect him.
Explanation: The “so…that” construction can be transformed into “too…to” when the intended meaning indicates that a quality prevents an action. His honesty makes suspicion unreasonable in this context.

Explanation: “None but” means “no one except.” It preserves the restriction expressed by “only” without asserting that every patient person necessarily solves the puzzle.

Explanation: “One of the most successful” places a person among a small group at the highest level. It does not mean that he is the single most successful entrepreneur.

C. What a success he achieved!
D. Alas! He failed the examination!
Correct Answer: D. Alas! He failed the examination!
Explanation: “It is a pity that…” expresses regret or sorrow. The exclamation “Alas!” conveys the same emotion while preserving the fact that he failed.
275. Choose the correct transformation of “He is too proud to beg.”

C. He is proud enough to beg.
D. He is too proud that he cannot beg.
Correct Answer: A. He is so proud that he cannot beg.
Explanation: “Too + adjective + to-infinitive” expresses an excessive degree that prevents an action. The corresponding pattern is “so + adjective + that + subject + cannot.”
