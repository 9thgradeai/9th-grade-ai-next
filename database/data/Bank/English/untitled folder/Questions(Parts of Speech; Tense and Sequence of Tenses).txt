Section A: Parts of Speech (Questions 1–70)
1. Identify the part of speech of the word “fast” in: “He drives fast.”
A. Adjective B. Adverb C. Noun D. Preposition
Correct Answer: B. Adverb Explanation: “Fast” modifies the verb “drives,” explaining how he drives. A word that modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb is an adverb.
2. Which of the following is an abstract noun?
A. Honesty B. Chair C. River D. Soldier
Correct Answer: A. Honesty Explanation: An abstract noun names a quality, state, or idea that cannot be physically touched. “Honesty” is a quality, whereas the other options name concrete things or people.
3. Identify the adjective in the sentence: “She bought an expensive watch.”
A. She B. Bought C. Expensive D. Watch
Correct Answer: C. Expensive Explanation: “Expensive” describes the noun “watch.” Adjectives commonly modify nouns or pronouns.
4. Which word is a pronoun in the sentence “They invited us to the ceremony”?
A. Invited B. Ceremony C. To D. They
Correct Answer: D. They Explanation: “They” is a personal pronoun referring to people. “Us” is also a pronoun in the sentence.
5. Identify the conjunction in: “He was tired, but he continued working.”
A. Tired B. But C. Continued D. Working
Correct Answer: B. But Explanation: “But” is a coordinating conjunction expressing contrast. It connects two clauses of equal grammatical status.
6. Which of the following is an interjection?
A. Alas! B. Slowly C. Beneath D. Honest
Correct Answer: A. Alas! Explanation: An interjection expresses a sudden feeling or emotion. “Alas!” commonly expresses sorrow or regret.
7. Identify the preposition in: “The documents are inside the drawer.”
A. Documents B. Are C. Inside D. Drawer
Correct Answer: C. Inside Explanation: “Inside” indicates the spatial relationship between the documents and the drawer. It functions as a preposition because it takes the noun phrase “the drawer” as its complement.
8. Which word is an adverb in “The manager spoke politely”?
A. Manager B. Spoke C. The D. Politely
Correct Answer: D. Politely Explanation: “Politely” modifies the verb “spoke” and tells us how the manager spoke. Many adverbs are formed by adding -ly to adjectives, although this is not a universal rule.
9. In “Swimming is good exercise,” the word “Swimming” functions as a—
A. Finite verb B. Gerund C. Adjective D. Preposition
Correct Answer: B. Gerund Explanation: “Swimming” is an -ing verb form functioning as a noun and serving as the subject. Such a form is called a gerund.
10. Identify the noun in “The poor deserve our sympathy.”
A. The B. Deserve C. Poor D. Our
Correct Answer: C. Poor Explanation: “Poor” is an adjective used substantively to mean poor people. In this construction, it functions as a plural noun phrase and takes the plural verb “deserve.”
11. Which word is an adjective in “The proposal seems reasonable”?
A. Proposal B. Seems C. The D. Reasonable
Correct Answer: D. Reasonable Explanation: “Reasonable” describes the subject through the linking verb “seems.” Adjectives can function as subject complements after linking verbs such as seem, become, and appear.
12. In “He arrived after the meeting,” the word “after” is a—
A. Preposition B. Adjective C. Pronoun D. Interjection
Correct Answer: A. Preposition Explanation: “After” expresses a time relationship and takes “the meeting” as its complement. Compare “We left after the meeting” with “We left after the meeting ended,” where “after” introduces a clause and functions as a subordinating conjunction.
13. Identify the adverb in “She is extremely intelligent.”
A. She B. Extremely C. Intelligent D. Is
Correct Answer: B. Extremely Explanation: “Extremely” modifies the adjective “intelligent” by indicating degree. Adverbs can modify adjectives as well as verbs.
14. Which of the following is a collective noun?
A. Wisdom B. Gold C. Committee D. Childhood
Correct Answer: C. Committee Explanation: A collective noun denotes a group considered as a unit. Other examples include team, jury, audience, and fleet.
15. In “This is my book,” the word “my” is a—
A. Possessive determiner B. Personal pronoun C. Adverb D. Conjunction
Correct Answer: A. Possessive determiner Explanation: “My” indicates possession and determines the noun “book.” In traditional school grammar, it is often called a possessive adjective.
16. Identify the finite verb in “She has completed the assignment.”
A. She B. Completed C. Assignment D. Has
Correct Answer: D. Has Explanation: “Has” is the finite auxiliary verb, marked for the third-person singular present tense. “Completed” is a past participle; together, they form the present perfect verb phrase.
17. Which word is a conjunction in “Although it rained, we went out”?
A. Rained B. Although C. Went D. Out
Correct Answer: B. Although Explanation: “Although” is a subordinating conjunction introducing a concessive subordinate clause. It shows that the rain did not prevent the action in the main clause.
18. Identify the noun form of “decide.”
A. Decisive B. Decided C. Decision D. Decidingly
Correct Answer: C. Decision Explanation: “Decision” is the noun corresponding to the verb “decide.” “Decisive” is an adjective, while “decided” is commonly a past-tense or past-participle form.
19. In “The train moved slowly,” “slowly” is formed from an adjective by adding—
A. -ness B. -ment C. -ful D. -ly
Correct Answer: D. -ly Explanation: “Slowly” is the adverb derived from the adjective “slow.” The suffix -ly frequently forms adverbs of manner.
20. Which of the following is a material noun?
A. Copper B. Freedom C. Army D. Student
Correct Answer: A. Copper Explanation: A material noun names a substance from which things may be made. “Copper” is a metal, while “freedom” is abstract and “army” is collective.
21. Identify the pronoun in “Everyone must submit the form.”
A. Must B. Everyone C. Submit D. Form
Correct Answer: B. Everyone Explanation: “Everyone” is an indefinite pronoun referring to every person in a group. Although it refers to multiple people conceptually, it normally takes a singular verb.
22. In “The child looks happy,” the word “happy” is a—
A. Noun B. Adverb C. Adjective D. Preposition
Correct Answer: C. Adjective Explanation: “Happy” describes the child and functions as a subject complement after the linking verb “looks.” Here, “looks” means “appears,” not “directs one's eyes.”
23. Which word is an adverb of frequency?
A. Often B. Beautiful C. Below D. Honest
Correct Answer: A. Often Explanation: “Often” indicates how frequently an action occurs. Other common adverbs of frequency include always, usually, sometimes, and rarely.
24. In “Neither answer is correct,” “Neither” functions as a—
A. Main verb B. Adverb C. Interjection D. Determiner
Correct Answer: D. Determiner Explanation: “Neither” determines the singular noun “answer” by indicating that not one of two alternatives is correct. When used alone, as in “Neither is correct,” it functions as a pronoun.
25. Identify the infinitive in “He wants to learn English.”
A. Wants B. To learn C. English D. He
Correct Answer: B. To learn Explanation: “To learn” is a to-infinitive consisting of “to” and the base form of the verb. It functions as the complement of “wants.”
26. In “The broken window was repaired,” “broken” functions as a—
A. Conjunction B. Preposition C. Participial adjective D. Personal pronoun
Correct Answer: C. Participial adjective Explanation: “Broken” is a past participle used to describe the noun “window.” Participles can function as adjectives or form parts of verb phrases.
27. Which sentence contains a transitive verb?
A. The baby slept. B. The sun shines. C. The guests arrived. D. She opened the door.
Correct Answer: D. She opened the door. Explanation: “Opened” is transitive because it takes the direct object “the door.” The other verbs in these sentences are used without direct objects.
28. In “He is a man of courage,” the word “courage” is a—
A. Abstract noun B. Proper noun C. Collective noun D. Countable noun
Correct Answer: A. Abstract noun Explanation: “Courage” names a quality rather than a physical object. It is normally uncountable in this meaning.
29. Identify the linking verb in “The soup tastes delicious.”
A. Soup B. Tastes C. Delicious D. The
Correct Answer: B. Tastes Explanation: “Tastes” links the subject “soup” to the adjective “delicious.” Verbs such as taste, smell, feel, and seem can function as linking verbs.
30. In “He ran across the field,” “across” is a—
A. Pronoun B. Conjunction C. Preposition D. Determiner
Correct Answer: C. Preposition Explanation: “Across” expresses movement in relation to “the field,” which is its complement. It can also function as an adverb when used without a complement, as in “Come across.”
31. Which word is an adjective formed from the noun “danger”?
A. Dangerously B. Endanger C. Dangerousness D. Dangerous
Correct Answer: D. Dangerous Explanation: “Dangerous” is an adjective meaning likely to cause harm. “Dangerously” is the corresponding adverb, and “endanger” is a verb.
32. In “What a beautiful day!”, “What” is a—
A. Exclamative determiner B. Personal pronoun C. Preposition D. Main verb
Correct Answer: A. Exclamative determiner Explanation: “What” determines the noun phrase “a beautiful day” in an exclamative construction. In “What did you buy?”, however, “what” functions as an interrogative pronoun.
33. Identify the conjunction in “You may stay here or leave now.”
A. May B. Or C. Here D. Now
Correct Answer: B. Or Explanation: “Or” is a coordinating conjunction expressing an alternative. It joins the two coordinated verb phrases “stay here” and “leave now.”
34. Which word is an adverb in “The answer is quite simple”?
A. Answer B. Is C. Quite D. Simple
Correct Answer: C. Quite Explanation: “Quite” modifies the adjective “simple” and expresses degree. Degree adverbs include very, rather, fairly, and extremely.
35. In “The rich should help the poor,” “the rich” refers to—
A. A single abstract quality B. A verb phrase C. A prepositional phrase D. A group of people
Correct Answer: D. A group of people Explanation: “The rich” is an adjective used substantively, meaning rich people as a group. Such expressions generally take plural agreement: “The rich are not always happy.”
36. Which of the following is a proper noun?
A. Dhaka B. City C. Country D. River
Correct Answer: A. Dhaka Explanation: “Dhaka” is the specific name of a city and therefore a proper noun. “City,” “country,” and “river” are common nouns.
37. In “He speaks English fluently,” the word “English” is a—
A. Verb B. Noun C. Adverb D. Preposition
Correct Answer: B. Noun Explanation: “English” names a language and functions as the direct object of “speaks.” Words that name languages are nouns even though the same words may also function as adjectives, as in “English literature.”
38. Which sentence contains a reflexive pronoun?
A. They helped us. B. This is mine. C. She blamed herself. D. Who came yesterday?
Correct Answer: C. She blamed herself. Explanation: “Herself” is a reflexive pronoun referring back to the subject “she.” Reflexive pronouns include myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, and themselves.
39. In “The match was postponed because of rain,” “because of” is a—
A. Conjunction introducing a finite clause B. Coordinating conjunction C. Verb phrase D. Complex preposition
Correct Answer: D. Complex preposition Explanation: “Because of” is a multiword preposition followed by the noun “rain.” Compare “because it rained,” where “because” introduces a finite subordinate clause.
40. Identify the participle in “The crying baby needs attention.”
A. Crying B. Baby C. Needs D. Attention
Correct Answer: A. Crying Explanation: “Crying” is a present participle modifying the noun “baby.” Present participles often describe an action associated with the noun.
41. Which of the following is an uncountable noun?
A. Chair B. Furniture C. Coin D. Employee
Correct Answer: B. Furniture Explanation: “Furniture” is an uncountable noun in standard English and normally takes a singular verb. Say “a piece of furniture,” not “a furniture.”
42. In “He works hard,” the word “hard” is a—
A. Noun B. Adjective C. Adverb D. Conjunction
Correct Answer: C. Adverb Explanation: “Hard” modifies the verb “works” and means with great effort. Compare “a hard task,” where “hard” is an adjective.
43. Which sentence uses “well” as an adjective?
A. She sings well. B. He performed well. C. They work well together. D. She is well today.
Correct Answer: D. She is well today. Explanation: In this sentence, “well” means healthy and functions as an adjective after the linking verb “is.” In the other examples, it is an adverb of manner.
44. In “I know that he is honest,” the word “that” introduces a—
A. Noun clause B. Prepositional phrase C. Participial phrase D. Conditional clause
Correct Answer: A. Noun clause Explanation: “That he is honest” is a subordinate content clause functioning as the object of “know.” Here, “that” is a subordinating conjunction or complementizer, not a demonstrative pronoun.
45. Which word is a modal auxiliary?
A. Writes B. Should C. Written D. Writing
Correct Answer: B. Should Explanation: “Should” is a modal auxiliary expressing advice, obligation, expectation, or probability, depending on context. Modal auxiliaries are followed by the base form of a verb: “should go.”
46. In “The committee has reached its decision,” “its” is a—
A. Relative pronoun B. Adverb C. Possessive determiner D. Conjunction
Correct Answer: C. Possessive determiner Explanation: “Its” indicates possession and determines the noun “decision.” It should not be confused with “it's,” the contraction of “it is” or “it has.”
47. Which word is a phrasal-verb particle in “Please turn off the light”?
A. Please B. The C. Light D. Off
Correct Answer: D. Off Explanation: “Off” combines with the verb “turn” to form the phrasal verb “turn off,” meaning switch off. In this sentence, the direct object can occur between the verb and particle: “turn the light off.”
48. In “Walking along the road, he found a wallet,” “Walking along the road” is a—
A. Participial phrase B. Noun clause C. Finite verb phrase D. Prepositional clause
Correct Answer: A. Participial phrase Explanation: The phrase begins with the present participle “walking” and describes the circumstances in which he found the wallet. Its understood subject is the subject of the main clause, “he.”
49. Which word is an indefinite pronoun?
A. This B. Someone C. Mine D. Himself
Correct Answer: B. Someone Explanation: “Someone” refers to an unspecified person. “This” is demonstrative, “mine” is a possessive pronoun, and “himself” is reflexive or intensive.
50. In “She is too tired to work,” “too” is an—
A. Adjective B. Noun C. Adverb D. Preposition
Correct Answer: C. Adverb Explanation: “Too” modifies the adjective “tired” and indicates an excessive degree. The construction “too + adjective + to-infinitive” often expresses that something prevents an action.
51. Identify the word functioning as a noun in “The accused was brought before the court.”
A. Was B. Brought C. Before D. Accused
Correct Answer: D. Accused Explanation: “The accused” refers to a person charged with an offence and functions as a noun phrase. “Accused” is historically a past participle but is used substantively here.
52. Which of the following is a demonstrative pronoun?
A. Those B. Whom C. Myself D. Someone
Correct Answer: A. Those Explanation: “Those” is a demonstrative pronoun when it stands alone, as in “Those are expensive.” Before a noun, as in “those books,” it functions as a demonstrative determiner.
53. In “The more you read, the better you write,” “better” is a—
A. Noun B. Adverb C. Preposition D. Conjunction
Correct Answer: B. Adverb Explanation: “Better” modifies the verb “write” and is the comparative form of “well.” The sentence uses the correlative comparative construction “the more …, the better ….”
54. Which word is a conjunction in “Unless you hurry, you will miss the train”?
A. Hurry B. Will C. Unless D. Train
Correct Answer: C. Unless Explanation: “Unless” introduces a conditional subordinate clause and means “if not.” The sentence indicates that failing to hurry will result in missing the train.
55. In “He gave me a little advice,” “little” is a—
A. Personal pronoun B. Verb C. Adverb D. Determiner
Correct Answer: D. Determiner Explanation: “Little” quantifies the uncountable noun “advice” and means a small amount, often suggesting insufficiency. “A little advice” is positive in tone and means some advice.
56. Which sentence contains an adjective used predicatively?
A. The flowers are beautiful. B. The beautiful flowers bloomed. C. She bought a red dress. D. It was a difficult examination.
Correct Answer: A. The flowers are beautiful. Explanation: “Beautiful” follows the linking verb “are” and functions as a subject complement. An adjective placed before a noun, as in the other examples, is used attributively.
57. In “He arrived early,” “early” is a—
A. Noun B. Adverb C. Preposition D. Conjunction
Correct Answer: B. Adverb Explanation: “Early” modifies the verb “arrived” by indicating when the action occurred. Unlike many adverbs, “early” does not require the suffix -ly.
58. Which word is a relative pronoun in “The man who came yesterday is my uncle”?
A. Man B. Yesterday C. Who D. Uncle
Correct Answer: C. Who Explanation: “Who” introduces the relative clause “who came yesterday” and refers to “the man.” It functions as the subject of the relative clause.
59. In “Having finished the work, she went home,” “Having finished” is a—
A. Present-tense finite verb B. To-infinitive C. Gerund phrase D. Perfect participle construction
Correct Answer: D. Perfect participle construction Explanation: “Having finished” is the perfect participle form, indicating that the work was completed before she went home. It consists of “having” plus the past participle “finished.”
60. Which of the following is a compound preposition?
A. In front of B. Quickly C. Although D. Herself
Correct Answer: A. In front of Explanation: “In front of” is a multiword preposition expressing spatial position. It takes a complement, as in “in front of the building.”
61. Identify the noun clause in the sentence “What he said surprised everyone.”
A. Surprised everyone B. What he said C. He said D. Everyone
Correct Answer: B. What he said Explanation: “What he said” functions as the subject of the sentence. It is a fused relative construction functioning as a noun phrase, often taught as a noun clause in traditional grammar.
62. Which sentence uses “fast” as an adjective?
A. He runs fast. B. She can type fast. C. It is a fast train. D. Time passes fast.
Correct Answer: C. It is a fast train. Explanation: “Fast” modifies the noun “train,” so it is an adjective. In the other examples, it modifies verbs and functions as an adverb.
63. In “The teacher made the students rewrite the answers,” “rewrite” is a—
A. Past participle B. Gerund C. Finite verb D. Bare infinitive
Correct Answer: D. Bare infinitive Explanation: “Rewrite” is the base form of the verb without “to.” The causative verb “make” is followed by an object and a bare infinitive in active constructions: “made the students rewrite.”
64. Which of the following is a possessive pronoun?
A. Theirs B. Their C. Them D. They
Correct Answer: A. Theirs Explanation: “Theirs” expresses possession and stands independently, as in “The house is theirs.” “Their” is a possessive determiner used before a noun.
65. In “He is neither intelligent nor hardworking,” the words “neither … nor” form a—
A. Subordinating pair B. Correlative conjunction C. Relative construction D. Prepositional phrase
Correct Answer: B. Correlative conjunction Explanation: “Neither … nor” connects two alternatives and expresses that both are negative. Parallel grammatical structures should follow both parts of the pair.
66. Which word is a noun formed from the adjective “strong”?
A. Strongly B. Strengthen C. Strength D. Stronger
Correct Answer: C. Strength Explanation: “Strength” is the noun corresponding to “strong.” “Strongly” is an adverb, “strengthen” is a verb, and “stronger” is a comparative adjective.
67. In “The book lying on the table is mine,” “lying on the table” functions as a—
A. Main clause B. Noun clause C. Finite adverbial clause D. Participial phrase modifying “book”
Correct Answer: D. Participial phrase modifying “book” Explanation: “Lying on the table” is a non-finite participial phrase that identifies which book is meant. It has no finite verb of its own.
68. Which sentence contains an adverb of degree?
A. The water is almost boiling. B. She arrived yesterday. C. He waited outside. D. They walked slowly.
Correct Answer: A. The water is almost boiling. Explanation: “Almost” modifies “boiling” and indicates the extent to which the process has progressed. “Yesterday” indicates time, “outside” indicates place, and “slowly” indicates manner.
69. In “One should do one's duty,” the word “one” is a—
A. Relative pronoun B. Indefinite pronoun C. Reflexive pronoun D. Interrogative pronoun
Correct Answer: B. Indefinite pronoun Explanation: “One” refers to people in general and functions as an indefinite pronoun. Formal English often uses “one's” as its corresponding possessive determiner.
70. Which sentence contains a noun functioning as an adjective?
A. Honesty is the best policy. B. He is honest. C. She bought a school bag. D. They spoke honestly.
Correct Answer: C. She bought a school bag. Explanation: “School” is a noun used attributively to modify the noun “bag.” Such noun modifiers are common in English, as in “bank account,” “office building,” and “exam paper.”
Section B: Tense (Questions 71–140)
71. Choose the correct form: “He usually _____ to work by bus.”
A. Go B. Is going C. Has gone D. Goes
Correct Answer: D. Goes Explanation: The simple present is used for habits and routines. With a third-person singular subject, the verb normally takes -s or -es.
72. “Look! The children _____ in the garden.”
A. Play B. Are playing C. Have played D. Played
Correct Answer: B. Are playing Explanation: The present continuous describes an action in progress at the time of speaking. “Look!” signals that the action is happening now.
73. “She _____ in Dhaka since 2020.”
A. Lives B. Lived C. Has lived D. Is living
Correct Answer: C. Has lived Explanation: The present perfect can describe a state that began in the past and continues into the present. “Since 2020” identifies the starting point.
74. “By the time we arrived, the meeting _____.”
A. Had begun B. Has begun C. Begins D. Will begin
Correct Answer: A. Had begun Explanation: The past perfect describes an event completed before another past event. The meeting began before “we arrived.”
75. “I _____ for the examination all morning, and I am still studying.”
A. Study B. Studied C. Have studied D. Have been studying
Correct Answer: D. Have been studying Explanation: The present perfect continuous emphasizes an activity that began in the past and continues into the present. “All morning” and “still studying” support this interpretation.
76. “When I entered the room, they _____ television.”
A. Watched B. Were watching C. Have watched D. Had watched
Correct Answer: B. Were watching Explanation: The past continuous describes an action in progress when another past event occurred. “Entered” is the shorter interrupting event.
77. “Water _____ at 100°C at standard atmospheric pressure.”
A. Is boiling B. Has boiled C. Boils D. Boiled
Correct Answer: C. Boils Explanation: The simple present expresses general scientific facts. The stated boiling point assumes standard atmospheric pressure.
78. “I _____ him yesterday at the station.”
A. Met B. Have met C. Had met D. Meet
Correct Answer: A. Met Explanation: The simple past is used for a completed event at a definite past time. “Yesterday” normally requires a past-tense form in this context.
79. “By next June, she _____ here for ten years.”
A. Works B. Has worked C. Will work D. Will have worked
Correct Answer: D. Will have worked Explanation: The future perfect expresses completion or duration up to a specified future point. “By next June” provides that future reference point.
80. “They _____ the project before the deadline last week.”
A. Complete B. Completed C. Have completed D. Will complete
Correct Answer: B. Completed Explanation: The simple past is appropriate because the action was completed at a definite past time, “last week.” The past perfect would be used if the sentence emphasized completion before another past event.
81. “She _____ English for three hours, and she has not finished yet.”
A. Studies B. Studied C. Has been studying D. Is studied
Correct Answer: C. Has been studying Explanation: The present perfect continuous emphasizes the ongoing duration of an activity. The phrase “has not finished yet” confirms that the activity continues.
82. “Listen! Someone _____ at the door.”
A. Is knocking B. Knocks C. Knocked D. Has knocked
Correct Answer: A. Is knocking Explanation: The present continuous describes an action happening now. “Listen!” directs attention to an ongoing sound.
83. “He _____ the report before the manager arrived.”
A. Finishes B. Has finished C. Was finishing D. Had finished
Correct Answer: D. Had finished Explanation: The past perfect shows that the report was completed before the manager arrived. Both events are in the past, but one occurred earlier than the other.
84. “I _____ this book twice.”
A. Read yesterday B. Have read C. Had read yesterday D. Am reading yesterday
Correct Answer: B. Have read Explanation: The present perfect describes life experience or an event count up to the present when no finished past time is specified. “Twice” indicates the number of completed readings.
85. “At 8 p.m. yesterday, I _____ for the interview.”
A. Prepare B. Have prepared C. Was preparing D. Had prepared tomorrow
Correct Answer: C. Was preparing Explanation: The past continuous describes an action in progress at a specific past time. “At 8 p.m. yesterday” identifies that time.
86. “The train _____ before we reached the station.”
A. Had left B. Has left C. Leaves D. Will leave
Correct Answer: A. Had left Explanation: The past perfect marks the train's departure as earlier than our arrival. The sentence contrasts two completed past events.
87. “She _____ her homework every evening.”
A. Is doing B. Has done C. Did D. Does
Correct Answer: D. Does Explanation: The simple present expresses a regular habit. “Every evening” is a frequency expression commonly used with this tense.
88. “They _____ to Chattogram tomorrow morning.”
A. Travelled B. Are travelling C. Have travelled yesterday D. Had travelled
Correct Answer: B. Are travelling Explanation: The present continuous can express a definite future arrangement. A planned trip with a specified time is a natural use.
89. “I _____ him since he left the country.”
A. Didn't see B. Don't see C. Haven't seen D. Hadn't seen tomorrow
Correct Answer: C. Haven't seen Explanation: The present perfect is used with “since” when the period continues to the present. “Since he left” identifies the starting point of the period.
90. “By the time you arrive, we _____ dinner.”
A. Will have finished B. Finished C. Have finished yesterday D. Had finished
Correct Answer: A. Will have finished Explanation: The future perfect describes an action that will be completed before a future event. “By the time you arrive” establishes the deadline.
91. “While the teacher _____, the students were taking notes.”
A. Speaks B. Has spoken C. Is speaking D. Was speaking
Correct Answer: D. Was speaking Explanation: The past continuous describes the background action occurring simultaneously with another past action. “Were taking” is also past continuous.
92. “He _____ his keys, so he cannot enter the house.”
A. Lost yesterday B. Has lost C. Had lost last year D. Loses yesterday
Correct Answer: B. Has lost Explanation: The present perfect links a past event to a present result: he does not have the keys now. A definite finished-time expression such as “yesterday” would normally require the simple past.
93. “The sun _____ in the east.”
A. Is rising B. Rose yesterday C. Rises D. Has risen every day at 6
Correct Answer: C. Rises Explanation: The simple present expresses established facts and regular natural phenomena. The sentence describes a general truth, not a single event.
94. “At this time next week, we _____ for Cox's Bazar.”
A. Will be travelling B. Travelled C. Have travelled D. Had travelled
Correct Answer: A. Will be travelling Explanation: The future continuous describes an action that will be in progress at a particular future time. “At this time next week” is a typical signal.
95. “He _____ the letter before he posted it.”
A. Signs B. Has signed C. Was signing tomorrow D. Had signed
Correct Answer: D. Had signed Explanation: The past perfect emphasizes that signing was completed before posting. The sequence of events is signing first, posting second.
96. “She _____ in this bank since January.”
A. Works yesterday B. Has been working C. Worked tomorrow D. Is worked
Correct Answer: B. Has been working Explanation: The present perfect continuous emphasizes the ongoing activity from January until now. The present perfect simple, “has worked,” is also possible, especially when emphasizing the employment fact rather than the activity's continuity.
97. “I _____ breakfast when the telephone rang.”
A. Have B. Had C. Was having D. Have had tomorrow
Correct Answer: C. Was having Explanation: The past continuous describes breakfast as an activity in progress when the telephone rang. The ringing is the shorter event.
98. “The patient _____ before the doctor arrived.”
A. Had died B. Has died C. Dies D. Will die
Correct Answer: A. Had died Explanation: The past perfect shows that the patient's death preceded the doctor's arrival. It clarifies the chronological relationship between the two past events.
99. “She usually _____ coffee, but today she is drinking tea.”
A. Is drinking B. Drank C. Has drunk D. Drinks
Correct Answer: D. Drinks Explanation: The simple present expresses her usual habit, while the present continuous describes today's temporary situation. The contrast between “usually” and “today” is important.
100. “By 2030, scientists _____ new solutions to this problem.”
A. Discovered in 2020 B. Will have discovered C. Have discovered yesterday D. Had discovered tomorrow
Correct Answer: B. Will have discovered Explanation: The future perfect expresses an anticipated achievement by a specified future time. “By 2030” indicates the deadline.
101. “It _____ heavily when we left the office.”
A. Rains B. Has rained C. Was raining D. Will rain yesterday
Correct Answer: C. Was raining Explanation: The past continuous describes the weather in progress at the time of departure. Weather conditions are often expressed with continuous forms when emphasizing ongoing activity.
102. “I _____ my assignment already.”
A. Have submitted B. Submit yesterday C. Had submitted tomorrow D. Am submit

200. Choose the grammatically correct sentence.
A. Having finished his work, he went home. B. Having finished his work, the television was switched off. C. Having finished his work, the rain started. D. Having finish his work, he went home.
Correct Answer: A. Having finished his work, he went home. Explanation: The perfect participial phrase “Having finished his work” correctly refers to the subject “he.” Options B and C contain dangling modifiers because the opening phrase does not logically describe the grammatical subject of the main clause. Option D uses the wrong verb form; “having” requires the past participle “finished.”
