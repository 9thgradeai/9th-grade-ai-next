Section A: Idioms and Idiomatic Expressions (1–50)
1. What does the idiom “at sixes and sevens” mean?
A. In perfect order B. In a state of confusion C. In complete agreement D. In a state of celebration
Correct Answer: B. In a state of confusion
Explanation: “At sixes and sevens” means confused or disorganized. Example: After the manager resigned, the entire office was at sixes and sevens.
2. What does “a blessing in disguise” mean?
A. A hidden danger B. An unexpected expense C. Something that seems bad but proves beneficial D. A deliberate act of kindness
Correct Answer: C. Something that seems bad but proves beneficial
Explanation: The idiom describes an apparently unfortunate event that eventually produces a positive result. Example: Losing that job was a blessing in disguise because she found a better one.
3. What does “once in a blue moon” mean?
A. Very rarely B. Every month C. At the right moment D. Unexpectedly soon
Correct Answer: A. Very rarely
Explanation: The expression refers to something that happens infrequently. Example: He visits his hometown once in a blue moon.
4. If someone “spills the beans,” what do they do?
A. Waste food B. Make a careless mistake C. Reveal a secret D. Avoid responsibility
Correct Answer: C. Reveal a secret
Explanation: “Spill the beans” means to disclose confidential information, often unintentionally. Example: She spilled the beans about the surprise party.
5. What does “a red-letter day” mean?
A. A day of financial loss B. A memorable or important day C. A public holiday only D. A day of bad luck
Correct Answer: B. A memorable or important day
Explanation: A red-letter day is a particularly significant or happy occasion. Example: The day he received his appointment letter was a red-letter day in his life.
6. What does “to add fuel to the fire” mean?
A. To solve a difficult problem B. To make a situation more intense or worse C. To provide necessary assistance D. To end a disagreement
Correct Answer: B. To make a situation more intense or worse
Explanation: This idiom means to aggravate an already difficult or tense situation. Example: His insulting remarks added fuel to the fire.
7. What does “to beat about the bush” mean?
A. To avoid coming directly to the point B. To work extremely hard C. To criticize someone openly D. To make a quick decision
Correct Answer: A. To avoid coming directly to the point
Explanation: The expression describes speaking indirectly instead of addressing the main issue. Example: Stop beating about the bush and tell me what happened.
8. What does “to hit the nail on the head” mean?
