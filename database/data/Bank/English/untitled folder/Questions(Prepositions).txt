Section 1: Basic Prepositions of Time, Place and Direction (1–40)
1. He usually gets up ___ 6 a.m.
A. in B. at C. on D. by
Correct Answer: B. at Explanation: Use at for a specific clock time. Example: The meeting starts at 10 a.m.
2. The independence of Bangladesh was declared ___ March 1971.
A. at B. on C. in D. by
Correct Answer: C. in Explanation: Use in with months, years, and longer periods. Example: in April, in 2026, in winter.
3. The examination will be held ___ Monday.
A. at B. in C. by D. on
Correct Answer: D. on Explanation: Use on with days and dates. Example: on Friday, on 15 October.
4. You must submit the application ___ Friday.
A. by B. at C. in D. on
Correct Answer: A. by Explanation: By Friday means no later than Friday. It sets a deadline rather than simply identifying the day.
5. She has been working here ___ 2020.
A. for B. from C. since D. during
Correct Answer: C. since Explanation: Use since with the starting point of an action continuing to the present. Example: since 2020, since Monday.
6. He has lived in Dhaka ___ five years.
A. since B. for C. during D. from
Correct Answer: B. for Explanation: Use for to express a duration. Example: for five years, for two hours.
7. Nobody is allowed to talk ___ the examination.
A. while B. since C. by D. during
Correct Answer: D. during Explanation: During is followed by a noun or noun phrase to indicate when something happens. Example: during the meeting.
8. I will wait here ___ you return.
A. until B. by C. since D. during
Correct Answer: A. until Explanation: Until means up to the time when something happens. Example: Wait until the manager arrives.
9. She completed the assignment ___ two hours.
A. at B. on C. in D. by
Correct Answer: C. in Explanation: In two hours can mean within a period of two hours or after two hours, depending on context. To express the time needed to finish something, in two hours is standard.
10. The train arrived ___ time to catch the connecting bus.
A. at B. in C. on D. by
Correct Answer: B. in Explanation: In time means early enough for a particular purpose. On time means at the scheduled time.
11. The train arrived exactly ___ time.
A. in B. at C. by D. on
Correct Answer: D. on Explanation: The fixed expression on time means punctual or at the expected time. Example: The officer arrived on time.
12. He worked hard ___ the night.
A. throughout B. between C. since D. until
Correct Answer: A. throughout Explanation: Throughout means during the whole of a period. Example: It rained throughout the night.
13. I have not seen him ___ last Thursday.
A. for B. from C. since D. during
Correct Answer: C. since Explanation: Since identifies the point at which a period began. The present perfect is commonly used with it when the situation continues to the present.
14. The office remains open ___ 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.
A. between B. from C. since D. during
Correct Answer: B. from Explanation: Use from ... to ... to indicate the beginning and end of a period. Example: from Monday to Friday.
15. You should finish the work ___ noon.
A. at B. in C. on D. by
Correct Answer: D. by Explanation: By noon means at or before noon. It indicates the latest acceptable time for completion.
16. The library is closed ___ Sundays.
A. on B. at C. in D. by
Correct Answer: A. on Explanation: Use on with days of the week, including plural forms for recurring days. Example: on Sundays.
17. The weather is usually pleasant ___ spring.
A. at B. on C. in D. by
Correct Answer: C. in Explanation: Use in with seasons. Example: in summer, in autumn.
18. The shop closes ___ midnight.
A. in B. at C. on D. by
Correct Answer: B. at Explanation: Use at with specific points of time, including midnight, noon, and dawn.
19. He was born ___ 15 October 2000.
A. in B. at C. by D. on
Correct Answer: D. on Explanation: Use on with a complete date. Example: on 21 February 1952.
20. The new policy will come into effect ___ the beginning of next month.
A. at B. in C. on D. by
Correct Answer: A. at Explanation: The standard expression is at the beginning of. By contrast, in the beginning generally means initially.
21. The keys are ___ the table.
A. above B. between C. on D. into
Correct Answer: C. on Explanation: Use on when something rests on a surface. Example: The file is on the desk.
22. The students are waiting ___ the classroom.
A. into B. in C. onto D. across
Correct Answer: B. in Explanation: Use in to indicate being inside an enclosed space. Example: The students are in the room.
23. The picture is hanging ___ the wall.
A. at B. in C. into D. on
Correct Answer: D. on Explanation: Use on for something attached to or displayed on a surface. Example: There is a clock on the wall.
24. The bank is located ___ the post office and the school.
A. between B. among C. beside D. across
Correct Answer: A. between Explanation: Use between for two distinct people, places, or things. It can also be used for more than two when the items are considered individually.
25. The teacher distributed the prizes ___ the five winners.
A. between B. beside C. among D. across
Correct Answer: C. among Explanation: Among is commonly used when referring to members of a group. Example: The money was divided among the participants.
26. He sat ___ his two colleagues.
A. among B. between C. beside D. across
Correct Answer: B. between Explanation: Between is appropriate because the person is positioned relative to two colleagues. The focus is on the two individual relationships.
27. The boy jumped ___ the river.
A. at B. over C. on D. into
Correct Answer: D. into Explanation: Into indicates movement from outside to inside or toward the interior of something. Compare in, which generally indicates location.
28. The cat jumped ___ the wall and landed on the other side.
A. over B. through C. under D. beside
Correct Answer: A. over Explanation: Over indicates movement across and above an obstacle. Example: The athlete jumped over the hurdle.
29. We walked ___ the tunnel to reach the other side.
A. across B. above C. through D. beside
Correct Answer: C. through Explanation: Through indicates movement within an enclosed space or from one side of it to the other. Example: The train passed through the tunnel.
30. The road runs ___ the river.
A. through B. along C. into D. onto
Correct Answer: B. along Explanation: Along means following the length or direction of something. Example: They walked along the riverbank.
31. The plane flew ___ the clouds.
A. under B. beside C. between D. above
Correct Answer: D. above Explanation: Above indicates a higher position, without necessarily implying direct vertical alignment. Example: The sun rose above the hills.
32. The temperature fell ___ zero last night.
A. below B. beneath C. under D. down
Correct Answer: A. below Explanation: Below indicates a lower level or value. It is natural to say below zero, below average, and below sea level.
33. The child was hiding ___ the bed.
A. above B. across C. under D. through
Correct Answer: C. under Explanation: Under indicates a position beneath something. Example: The shoes are under the bed.
34. The village lies ___ sea level.
A. over B. below C. onto D. across
Correct Answer: B. below Explanation: The standard expression is below sea level. Use below for measurements or levels lower than a reference point.
35. The hospital is ___ the main road.
A. into B. through C. across D. beside
Correct Answer: D. beside Explanation: Beside means next to or at the side of something. Do not confuse it with besides, meaning in addition to.
36. The pharmacy is ___ the street from the bank.
A. across B. through C. along D. among
Correct Answer: A. across Explanation: The expression across the street from means on the opposite side of the street. Example: The school is across the street from the park.
37. The boy ran ___ the room when he heard the news.
A. at B. over C. into D. beside
Correct Answer: C. into Explanation: Into emphasizes movement toward the interior of the room. In would generally describe the boy's location rather than the movement.
38. The passengers got ___ the bus at the terminal.
A. into B. on C. onto D. in
Correct Answer: B. on Explanation: We normally say get on a bus, train, or plane and get in a car or taxi. These are conventional usage patterns.
39. She got ___ the car and fastened her seat belt.
A. onto B. on C. into D. over
Correct Answer: C. into Explanation: For a car, the usual expression is get into the car. Use get out of the car for the opposite movement.
40. The athlete ran ___ the finish line.
A. beside B. under C. between D. across
Correct Answer: D. across Explanation: Across indicates movement from one side to the other. Example: The runner raced across the finish line.
Section 2: Verb–Preposition Combinations (41–80)
41. She is good ___ mathematics.
A. at B. in C. on D. for
Correct Answer: A. at Explanation: The standard expression is good at a subject or activity. Example: He is good at solving problems.
42. He is interested ___ economics.
A. on B. for C. in D. with
Correct Answer: C. in Explanation: The adjective interested takes in. Example: She is interested in banking.
43. The manager agreed ___ our proposal.
A. with B. to C. on D. for
Correct Answer: B. to Explanation: Agree to a proposal, plan, or suggestion means accept it. Agree with a person or an opinion is also correct in a different context.
44. I agree ___ you on this issue.
A. to B. for C. on D. with
Correct Answer: D. with Explanation: Use agree with when expressing the same opinion as a person. Example: I agree with your assessment.
45. The officer insisted ___ checking the documents personally.
A. on B. at C. for D. to
Correct Answer: A. on Explanation: Insist on is followed by a noun or a gerund. Example: She insisted on paying the bill.
46. The committee consists ___ ten members.
A. from B. with C. of D. in
Correct Answer: C. of Explanation: The verb consist takes of when identifying the components of something. Example: The board consists of five directors.
47. Success depends ___ hard work and perseverance.
A. in B. on C. at D. with
Correct Answer: B. on Explanation: The fixed combination is depend on. Example: The outcome depends on the evidence.
48. He apologized ___ his rude behaviour.
A. with B. at C. on D. for
Correct Answer: D. for Explanation: Use apologize for an action or mistake. Use apologize to a person: He apologized to his colleague for the error.
49. The accused was charged ___ theft.
A. with B. for C. of D. by
Correct Answer: A. with Explanation: The standard legal expression is charged with an offence. Example: He was charged with fraud.
50. The police accused him ___ stealing the money.
A. for B. with C. of D. from
Correct Answer: C. of Explanation: The verb accuse takes of before an offence or wrongdoing. Example: They accused him of dishonesty.
51. The patient is suffering ___ a serious illness.
A. from B. with C. by D. of
Correct Answer: A. from Explanation: Suffer from is the usual combination for an illness or ongoing problem. Example: He suffers from asthma.
52. She congratulated me ___ my success.
A. at B. for C. on D. with
Correct Answer: C. on Explanation: Use congratulate someone on something. Example: We congratulated her on her promotion.
53. The officer prevented him ___ entering the building.
A. to B. from C. for D. against
Correct Answer: B. from Explanation: Prevent someone from doing something is the standard construction. The word from is followed by a gerund in this sentence.
54. The new policy will contribute ___ economic growth.
A. for B. on C. with D. to
Correct Answer: D. to Explanation: Contribute to means help bring about or add to something. Example: Education contributes to national development.
55. He applied ___ the post of Senior Officer.
A. for B. to C. on D. at
Correct Answer: A. for Explanation: Use apply for a job or position. Use apply to an institution: She applied to the university.
56. The applicant complied ___ all the requirements.
A. to B. by C. with D. for
Correct Answer: C. with Explanation: Comply with means act according to a rule, request, or requirement. Example: All employees must comply with the regulations.
57. He borrowed a book ___ the library.
A. from B. of C. by D. with
Correct Answer: A. from Explanation: Borrow from identifies the source from which something is temporarily obtained. The opposite action is lend to.
58. The bank provides loans ___ eligible applicants.
A. for B. with C. at D. to
Correct Answer: D. to Explanation: Provide something to someone is a standard construction. Provide someone with something is another correct form.
59. The success of the project resulted ___ careful planning.
A. from B. in C. with D. by
Correct Answer: A. from Explanation: Result from identifies a cause. By contrast, result in identifies an outcome: Careful planning resulted in success.
60. The investigation resulted ___ several arrests.
A. from B. of C. in D. with
Correct Answer: C. in Explanation: Result in means lead to or produce an outcome. Example: The inquiry resulted in major reforms.
61. She suffers ___ frequent headaches.
A. from B. with C. by D. at
Correct Answer: A. from Explanation: The natural expression is suffer from headaches. Remember this combination for health-related sentence-completion questions.
62. We should not interfere ___ other people's affairs.
A. on B. with C. at D. for
Correct Answer: B. with Explanation: Interfere with means meddle in or disrupt something. Example: Do not interfere with the investigation.
63. The company specializes ___ financial services.
A. at B. on C. in D. for
Correct Answer: C. in Explanation: Use specialize in a subject, field, or service. Example: The firm specializes in risk management.
64. He succeeded ___ solving the problem.
A. at B. on C. with D. in
Correct Answer: D. in Explanation: Succeed in is followed by a noun or gerund. Example: She succeeded in passing the examination.
65. The officer objected ___ the proposed changes.
A. to B. against C. for D. on
Correct Answer: A. to Explanation: The verb object takes to. When followed by an action, the next verb is usually a gerund: objected to changing the rules.
66. This product differs ___ the previous model.
A. with B. than C. from D. to
Correct Answer: C. from Explanation: In standard formal English, differ from is used to indicate dissimilarity. Example: The new policy differs from the old one.
67. The candidate was selected ___ merit.
A. in B. on C. at D. with
Correct Answer: B. on Explanation: The fixed expression is on merit or on the basis of merit. It means selection according to ability or achievement.
68. He abstained ___ voting.
A. of B. against C. to D. from
Correct Answer: D. from Explanation: Abstain from means deliberately refrain from an action. Example: The member abstained from voting.
69. We must adhere ___ the rules.
A. to B. with C. by D. for
Correct Answer: A. to Explanation: Adhere to means follow or remain faithful to a rule, principle, or agreement. Example: Employees must adhere to the code of conduct.
70. The government should invest ___ education.
A. at B. for C. in D. on
Correct Answer: C. in Explanation: Use invest in an asset, sector, or activity. Example: The country invests in human capital.
71. The manager is looking ___ a suitable candidate.
A. after B. for C. into D. over
Correct Answer: B. for Explanation: Look for means search for something or someone. Do not confuse it with look after (take care of) or look into (investigate).
72. The committee will look ___ the complaint.
A. after B. for C. over D. into
Correct Answer: D. into Explanation: Look into means investigate or examine. Example: The authorities will look into the allegations.
73. She looks ___ her younger brother when their parents are away.
A. after B. into C. for D. at
Correct Answer: A. after Explanation: Look after means take care of. Example: The nurse looks after the patients.
74. You should not look ___ people who are less fortunate.
A. over B. into C. down on D. after
Correct Answer: C. down on Explanation: Look down on means regard someone as inferior. The opposite is look up to, meaning admire or respect.
75. I look ___ my teacher as a role model.
A. down on B. up to C. into D. away from
Correct Answer: B. up to Explanation: Look up to means admire and respect someone. Example: Many students look up to their teachers.
76. The meeting was put ___ until next week.
A. off B. out C. away D. up
Correct Answer: A. off Explanation: Put off means postpone. Example: The interview was put off because of bad weather.
77. The fire broke ___ at midnight.
A. into B. up C. out D. off
Correct Answer: C. out Explanation: Break out means begin suddenly, especially of a fire, war, or disease. Example: A fire broke out in the warehouse.
78. The thief broke ___ the house through a window.
A. out B. into C. away D. down
Correct Answer: B. into Explanation: Break into means enter a place by force or unlawfully. Example: Someone broke into the office.
79. The negotiations broke ___ without an agreement.
A. out B. into C. away D. down
Correct Answer: D. down Explanation: Break down can mean fail or collapse, including negotiations or a process. Example: The talks broke down after a dispute.
80. The suspect finally gave ___ to police pressure.
A. in B. up C. away D. off
Correct Answer: A. in Explanation: Give in means surrender or stop resisting. Give up usually means quit or abandon an activity.
Section 3: Adjective–Preposition Combinations (81–110)
81. He is afraid ___ snakes.
A. from B. of C. with D. at
Correct Answer: B. of Explanation: The standard combination is afraid of. Example: She is afraid of heights.
82. She is capable ___ handling difficult situations.
A. to B. for C. of D. with
Correct Answer: C. of Explanation: Use capable of followed by a noun or gerund. Example: He is capable of managing the team.
83. The manager is responsible ___ supervising the staff.
A. for B. of C. to D. with
Correct Answer: A. for Explanation: Responsible for identifies a duty or obligation. Example: She is responsible for preparing the report.
84. This region is rich ___ natural resources.
A. of B. with C. by D. in
Correct Answer: D. in Explanation: The usual expression is rich in resources, minerals, or nutrients. Example: Bangladesh is rich in natural beauty.
85. He is senior ___ me in the service.
A. than B. to C. from D. over
Correct Answer: B. to Explanation: Senior takes to, not than, in standard comparisons. The same pattern applies to junior to, superior to, and inferior to.
86. This machine is superior ___ the old one.
A. than B. from C. to D. over
Correct Answer: C. to Explanation: The correct construction is superior to. Avoid superior than, which is nonstandard in this usage.
87. His views are contrary ___ mine.
A. to B. with C. from D. against
Correct Answer: A. to Explanation: Contrary to means opposite to or in conflict with. Example: His actions were contrary to the rules.
88. The witness was familiar ___ the facts of the case.
A. to B. by C. on D. with
Correct Answer: D. with Explanation: Use familiar with when someone knows something. Familiar to means known or recognizable to someone, as in The song is familiar to me.
89. I am grateful ___ you for your assistance.
A. with B. to C. for D. by
Correct Answer: B. to Explanation: Use grateful to someone for something. Example: I am grateful to my teacher for her guidance.
90. She is keen ___ learning foreign languages.
A. on B. at C. in D. for
Correct Answer: A. on Explanation: The expression keen on indicates enthusiasm or strong interest. It is followed by a noun or gerund: keen on reading.
91. The child is fond ___ sweets.
A. with B. at C. of D. for
Correct Answer: C. of Explanation: Fond of means having a liking for someone or something. Example: She is fond of classical music.
92. The officer was indifferent ___ the criticism.
A. with B. at C. for D. to
Correct Answer: D. to Explanation: Indifferent to means not interested in or not affected by something. Example: He remained indifferent to public opinion.
93. He is eligible ___ the position.
A. to B. for C. of D. with
Correct Answer: B. for Explanation: Use eligible for a position, benefit, or opportunity. Example: She is eligible for promotion.
94. This medicine is effective ___ treating the infection.
A. in B. on C. at D. for
Correct Answer: A. in Explanation: Effective in doing something is a standard construction. Example: The method is effective in reducing costs.
95. He is accustomed ___ getting up early.
A. with B. for C. to D. at
Correct Answer: C. to Explanation: Accustomed to means used to something. Here, to is a preposition, so it is followed by the gerund getting.
96. The new employee is not accustomed ___ this kind of pressure.
A. for B. with C. at D. to
Correct Answer: D. to Explanation: The fixed expression is accustomed to. It can be followed by a noun or a gerund: accustomed to pressure, accustomed to working late.
97. He is addicted ___ gambling.
A. to B. with C. for D. on
Correct Answer: A. to Explanation: The correct combination is addicted to. Since to is a preposition here, use a gerund after it: addicted to gambling.
98. She is anxious ___ her son's health.
A. for B. about C. with D. at
Correct Answer: B. about Explanation: Anxious about expresses worry or concern. Example: The parents are anxious about the results.
99. He is anxious ___ please his superiors.
A. about B. with C. to D. for
Correct Answer: C. to Explanation: Anxious to do something can mean eager or strongly wishing to do it. Example: She is anxious to succeed.
100. The teacher was pleased ___ the students' performance.
A. at B. for C. with D. by
Correct Answer: C. with Explanation: Pleased with is commonly used for satisfaction with a person, result, or thing. Example: The examiner was pleased with the answer.
101. He is annoyed ___ me for arriving late.
A. with B. at C. for D. on
Correct Answer: A. with Explanation: Annoyed with someone is standard when identifying the person causing irritation. Annoyed at/about something is also possible.
102. The country is dependent ___ imported fuel.
A. in B. of C. from D. on
Correct Answer: D. on Explanation: Use dependent on to identify what someone or something relies upon. Example: The company is dependent on foreign investment.
103. This rule is applicable ___ all employees.
A. for B. to C. with D. on
Correct Answer: B. to Explanation: The correct expression is applicable to. Example: The regulation is applicable to all applicants.
104. He is conscious ___ the importance of discipline.
A. about B. at C. of D. with
Correct Answer: C. of Explanation: Conscious of means aware of. Example: She is conscious of her responsibilities.
105. I am confident ___ his success.
A. of B. for C. with D. on
Correct Answer: A. of Explanation: Confident of is used when expressing belief in a likely result. Example: We are confident of victory.
106. She is disappointed ___ the examination result.
A. on B. at C. for D. in
Correct Answer: B. at Explanation: Disappointed at is commonly used for a result or event. Disappointed with a person or thing and disappointed in someone are also valid in appropriate contexts.
107. The child is obedient ___ his parents.
A. with B. for C. to D. at
Correct Answer: C. to Explanation: The adjective obedient takes to. Example: The students are obedient to their teachers.
108. He is notorious ___ his dishonesty.
A. with B. about C. of D. for
Correct Answer: D. for Explanation: Notorious for identifies the undesirable conduct or quality for which someone is widely known. Example: The area is notorious for traffic congestion.
109. The two brothers are jealous ___ each other.
A. of B. with C. at D. on
Correct Answer: A. of Explanation: The standard combination is jealous of. Example: He was jealous of his colleague's success.
110. The company is committed ___ improving customer service.
A. for B. with C. to D. on
Correct Answer: C. to Explanation: Committed to means dedicated to a cause or activity. Because to is a preposition here, it can be followed by a gerund: committed to improving.

112. The demand ___ skilled workers is increasing.

113. He has a great aptitude ___ mathematics.

114. The government should take measures to prevent the spread ___ disease.
A. for B. with C. to D. of

115. The reason ___ his absence is still unknown.
A. of B. for C. to D. with
Correct Answer: B. for Explanation: The standard expression is the reason for something. Example: What is the reason for the delay?
116. What is the cause ___ the accident?

117. His application was rejected on the grounds ___ insufficient evidence.

118. The bank has introduced a new system for the benefit ___ its customers.

119. There is a close relationship ___ education and development.

120. The success of a business depends on the quality ___ its management.

121. He has a strong preference ___ tea rather than coffee.

122. The minister expressed his concern ___ rising unemployment.
A. for B. at C. to D. about
Correct Answer: D. about Explanation: Concern about identifies the issue causing worry. Concern for often emphasizes care for a person or their welfare.
123. The report provides an explanation ___ the unexpected decline.

124. The teacher has great respect ___ honest students.

125. There is a shortage ___ qualified teachers in rural areas.

126. The company made a profit ___ its investment.
A. at B. with C. on D. for
Correct Answer: C. on Explanation: The conventional expression is make a profit on an investment. Example: The firm made a profit on the transaction.
127. The employee has access ___ confidential information.
A. with B. to C. for D. at
Correct Answer: B. to Explanation: The fixed phrase is access to. Example: Only authorized staff have access to the records.
128. The police conducted an investigation ___ the incident.
A. on B. for C. at D. into
Correct Answer: D. into Explanation: Use an investigation into an event, allegation, or problem. Example: An investigation into the fraud is underway.
129. He has no control ___ his emotions.
A. over B. on C. at D. to
Correct Answer: A. over Explanation: The expression control over means the power to direct or regulate something. Example: The manager has control over the budget.
130. The company has a monopoly ___ the local market.
A. at B. for C. on D. in
Correct Answer: D. in Explanation: A monopoly in a market is a standard expression. A monopoly on something is also correct, especially when referring to exclusive control over a particular product or activity.
131. He has a good command ___ English.
A. in B. of C. over D. on
Correct Answer: B. of Explanation: The standard expression is a command of a language. Example: She has an excellent command of English grammar.
132. The proposal is in accordance ___ the existing regulations.
A. to B. of C. with D. for
Correct Answer: C. with Explanation: In accordance with means in agreement with or following a rule. Example: The decision was made in accordance with the law.
133. The project was completed in compliance ___ safety standards.
A. with B. to C. for D. by
Correct Answer: A. with Explanation: The fixed expression is in compliance with. It means following the applicable rules or requirements.
134. He is in charge ___ the accounts department.
A. for B. to C. with D. of
Correct Answer: D. of Explanation: In charge of identifies the person responsible for a task or department. Example: She is in charge of recruitment.
135. The decision was taken in response ___ public criticism.
A. with B. to C. for D. at
Correct Answer: B. to Explanation: In response to means as an answer or reaction to something. Example: The bank revised its policy in response to complaints.
136. The new system is designed with a view ___ reducing costs.
A. for B. at C. to D. of
Correct Answer: C. to Explanation: The fixed phrase is with a view to, meaning with the aim of. Here to is a preposition, so it is followed by the gerund reducing.
137. He has an objection ___ the proposed arrangement.
A. against B. to C. for D. with
Correct Answer: B. to Explanation: Use an objection to something. Example: The committee raised an objection to the proposal.
138. There is a possibility ___ rain this evening.
A. for B. to C. with D. of
Correct Answer: D. of Explanation: The standard expression is a possibility of. Example: There is a possibility of delays.
139. The committee reached an agreement ___ the terms of the contract.
A. on B. at C. with D. for
Correct Answer: A. on Explanation: Agreement on is used for the subject or terms agreed upon. Agreement with can identify the person or party whose views are shared.
140. His lack ___ experience made the task difficult.
A. for B. to C. of D. with
Correct Answer: C. of Explanation: The noun lack takes of when followed by the thing that is missing. Example: a lack of confidence.
Section 5: Commonly Confused Prepositions and Advanced Usage (141–170)
141. He prefers tea ___ coffee.
A. than B. to C. over D. from
Correct Answer: B. to Explanation: In standard exam grammar, prefer X to Y is the usual construction. Example: She prefers reading to watching television.
142. The principal presided ___ the meeting.
A. in B. over C. at D. on
Correct Answer: B. over Explanation: The standard expression is preside over a meeting. Example: The chairman presided over the annual meeting.
143. The accused was acquitted ___ the charge of theft.
A. from B. with C. of D. for
Correct Answer: C. of Explanation: The legal expression is acquit someone of a charge. Example: The court acquitted him of all charges.
144. He was deprived ___ his rightful inheritance.
A. from B. with C. off D. of
Correct Answer: D. of Explanation: Deprive someone of something means prevent someone from having it. Example: The policy deprived them of their rights.
145. The company was accused ___ violating the regulations.
A. of B. for C. with D. to
Correct Answer: A. of Explanation: Use accused of before a noun or gerund describing the alleged offence. Example: She was accused of leaking confidential information.
146. The witness testified ___ the accused.
A. for B. against C. to D. with
Correct Answer: B. against Explanation: Testify against means give evidence that may be used to establish someone's guilt. Testify for means give evidence in someone's favour.
147. The new law prohibits people ___ smoking in this area.
A. to B. against C. from D. for
Correct Answer: C. from Explanation: The construction is prohibit someone from doing something. Example: The rules prohibit employees from sharing passwords.
148. The officer was entrusted ___ the responsibility of managing the funds.
A. to B. for C. by D. with
Correct Answer: D. with Explanation: Use entrust someone with something to assign responsibility. The alternative construction is entrust something to someone.
149. I cannot dispense ___ your assistance.
A. with B. from C. of D. for
Correct Answer: A. with Explanation: Dispense with means manage without or do away with something. Example: We cannot dispense with the required checks.
150. The officer is entitled ___ a leave allowance.
A. for B. to C. with D. of
Correct Answer: B. to Explanation: Entitled to means having a right to something. Example: Eligible employees are entitled to the benefit.
151. The chairman presided ___ the proceedings.
A. at B. in C. over D. on
Correct Answer: C. over Explanation: Use preside over when someone directs or chairs a meeting or proceedings. This is a common fixed-preposition question.
152. The candidate was prevented ___ attending the interview.
A. to B. against C. for D. from
Correct Answer: D. from Explanation: The passive form is be prevented from doing something. Example: He was prevented from entering the examination hall.
153. The employees protested ___ the unfair decision.
A. against B. for C. to D. with
Correct Answer: A. against Explanation: Protest against expresses opposition to a decision, policy, or action. Example: Workers protested against the wage cuts.
154. He has been accused of tampering ___ the evidence.
A. at B. with C. to D. for
Correct Answer: B. with Explanation: The standard expression is tamper with, meaning interfere with something improperly. Example: Never tamper with official records.
155. The company embarked ___ an ambitious expansion programme.
A. at B. with C. on D. into
Correct Answer: C. on Explanation: Embark on means begin an important activity or undertaking. Example: The bank embarked on a digital transformation programme.
156. The new rule applies ___ every applicant.
A. for B. with C. on D. to
Correct Answer: D. to Explanation: The verb apply takes to when identifying the person or situation to which a rule is relevant. Example: This condition applies to all candidates.
157. He was accused of being involved ___ the fraud.
A. in B. at C. with D. on
Correct Answer: A. in Explanation: Involved in identifies participation in an activity or event. Example: Several people were involved in the investigation.
158. The committee arrived ___ a unanimous decision.
A. to B. at C. on D. with
Correct Answer: B. at Explanation: The fixed expression is arrive at a decision or conclusion. We usually say arrive at a decision but arrive in Dhaka for reaching a city.
159. The two parties finally agreed ___ the terms of settlement.
A. with B. to C. on D. for
Correct Answer: C. on Explanation: Agree on is used when parties reach a common decision about a subject or terms. Agree to means accept a proposal or arrangement.
160. The bank's decision was based ___ the applicant's credit history.
A. in B. at C. from D. on
Correct Answer: D. on Explanation: The standard expression is based on. Example: The assessment was based on verified information.
161. The manager warned the staff ___ the consequences of negligence.
A. about B. for C. with D. to
Correct Answer: A. about Explanation: Warn someone about something means alert them to a possible danger or problem. Warn someone against doing something advises them not to do it.
162. The doctor advised him to refrain ___ smoking.
A. of B. from C. with D. against
Correct Answer: B. from Explanation: Refrain from means deliberately avoid an action. Example: Candidates must refrain from using mobile phones.
163. The manager is accountable ___ the board for the department's performance.
A. for B. with C. to D. at
Correct Answer: C. to Explanation: Accountable to identifies the person or body to whom one must answer. Accountable for identifies the responsibility or result.
164. The success of the scheme is attributable ___ effective management.
A. for B. with C. from D. to
Correct Answer: D. to Explanation: Attributable to means caused by or explained by something. Example: The improvement is attributable to better planning.
165. He is averse ___ taking unnecessary risks.
A. to B. for C. against D. with
Correct Answer: A. to Explanation: Averse to means opposed or unwilling. Here to is a preposition, so it is followed by the gerund taking.
166. The candidate was eligible ___ appointment.
A. to B. for C. of D. with
Correct Answer: B. for Explanation: Use eligible for a job, appointment, or benefit. Example: The applicant is eligible for appointment to the post.
167. He is not immune ___ criticism.
A. with B. from C. to D. against
Correct Answer: C. to Explanation: The standard expression is immune to. Example: No public institution is immune to criticism.
168. The officer was commended ___ his honesty and diligence.
A. with B. at C. for D. on
Correct Answer: C. for Explanation: Commend someone for something means praise them because of a quality or achievement. Example: She was commended for her excellent service.
169. The manager was dissatisfied ___ the quality of the report.
A. at B. with C. on D. for
Correct Answer: B. with Explanation: Dissatisfied with identifies the person or thing causing dissatisfaction. Example: The customers were dissatisfied with the service.
170. The officer has authority ___ approve the expenditure.
A. for B. on C. to D. of
Correct Answer: C. to Explanation: The construction authority to do something identifies the power to take an action. Example: The manager has authority to approve the request.
Section 6: Error Detection, Sentence Completion and Exam-Level Applications (171–200)
171. Choose the sentence with the correct preposition.
A. He is married with a doctor. B. He is married to a doctor. C. He is married by a doctor. D. He is married from a doctor.
Correct Answer: B. He is married to a doctor. Explanation: The standard expression is married to someone. Do not confuse it with marry someone, which takes a direct object without a preposition.
172. Choose the correct sentence.
A. She entered into the room quietly. B. She entered at the room quietly. C. She entered the room quietly. D. She entered to the room quietly.
Correct Answer: C. She entered the room quietly. Explanation: Enter normally takes a direct object when it means go into a place. Thus, enter the room is preferred to enter into the room in this context.
173. Choose the correct sentence.
A. We discussed about the matter yesterday. B. We discussed on the matter yesterday. C. We discussed over the matter yesterday. D. We discussed the matter yesterday.
Correct Answer: D. We discussed the matter yesterday. Explanation: Discuss is normally transitive and does not take about before its direct object. Say discuss the issue, not discuss about the issue.
174. Choose the correct sentence.
A. He resembles his father in appearance. B. He resembles to his father in appearance. C. He resembles with his father in appearance. D. He resembles after his father in appearance.
Correct Answer: A. He resembles his father in appearance. Explanation: Resemble takes a direct object without a preposition. Therefore, resembles his father is correct.
175. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The accident happened due to of negligence. B. The accident happened because negligence. C. The accident happened due to negligence. D. The accident happened owing negligence.
Correct Answer: C. The accident happened due to negligence. Explanation: Due to means caused by and is followed by a noun or noun phrase. The expressions owing to negligence and because of negligence are also correct.
176. Choose the correct sentence.
A. He has been absent since three days. B. He has been absent for three days. C. He has been absent from three days. D. He has been absent during three days.
Correct Answer: B. He has been absent for three days. Explanation: Use for with a duration and since with a starting point. Compare for three days with since Monday.
177. Choose the correct sentence.
A. I have known him since five years. B. I know him for five years. C. I have known him from five years. D. I have known him for five years.
Correct Answer: D. I have known him for five years. Explanation: The present perfect with for expresses a state continuing over a period. Know is a stative verb and is not normally used in the continuous form here.
178. Choose the correct sentence.
A. He is capable to solve the problem. B. He is capable for solving the problem. C. He is capable of solving the problem. D. He is capable in solve the problem.
Correct Answer: C. He is capable of solving the problem. Explanation: The adjective capable takes of followed by a noun or gerund. Example: She is capable of completing the task.
179. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The teacher emphasized on the importance of discipline. B. The teacher emphasized the importance of discipline. C. The teacher emphasized at the importance of discipline. D. The teacher emphasized for the importance of discipline.
Correct Answer: B. The teacher emphasized the importance of discipline. Explanation: Emphasize takes a direct object without a preposition. Say emphasize the need, emphasize the importance, or place emphasis on something.
180. Choose the correct sentence.
A. He is good in English grammar. B. He is good for English grammar. C. He is good with English grammar. D. He is good at English grammar.
Correct Answer: D. He is good at English grammar. Explanation: Use good at to describe skill in a subject or activity. Example: She is good at mathematics.
181. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The train consists of ten coaches. B. The train consists with ten coaches. C. The train consists from ten coaches. D. The train consists in ten coaches.
Correct Answer: A. The train consists of ten coaches. Explanation: Consist of identifies the parts that make up a whole. Example: The committee consists of seven members.
182. Choose the correct sentence.
A. He ordered for a cup of tea. B. He ordered to a cup of tea. C. He ordered a cup of tea. D. He ordered with a cup of tea.
Correct Answer: C. He ordered a cup of tea. Explanation: Order takes a direct object when requesting food or goods. Example: She ordered a meal.
183. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The principal forbade us to enter the hall. B. The principal forbade us from to enter the hall. C. The principal forbade us for entering the hall. D. The principal forbade us against entering the hall.
Correct Answer: A. The principal forbade us to enter the hall. Explanation: Forbid someone to do something is a standard construction. Another correct form is forbid someone from doing something.
184. Choose the correct sentence.
A. He is suffering with fever. B. He is suffering by fever. C. He is suffering of fever. D. He is suffering from fever.
Correct Answer: D. He is suffering from fever. Explanation: The usual expression is suffer from an illness. Example: The patient is suffering from a viral infection.
185. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The officer prevented him to leave. B. The officer prevented him from leaving. C. The officer prevented him for leaving. D. The officer prevented him against leaving.
Correct Answer: B. The officer prevented him from leaving. Explanation: Use prevent someone from doing something. The verb following from takes the gerund form.
186. Choose the correct sentence.
A. She insisted to pay the bill. B. She insisted for paying the bill. C. She insisted on paying the bill. D. She insisted at paying the bill.
Correct Answer: C. She insisted on paying the bill. Explanation: Insist on is followed by a noun or gerund. Example: He insisted on checking the figures himself.
187. Choose the correct sentence.
A. I look forward to meet you. B. I look forward for meeting you. C. I look forward to meeting you. D. I look forward at meeting you.
Correct Answer: C. I look forward to meeting you. Explanation: In look forward to, the word to is a preposition, so it is followed by a noun or gerund. Example: I look forward to hearing from you.
188. Choose the correct sentence.
A. He prevented me to make a mistake. B. He prevented me from making a mistake. C. He prevented me for making a mistake. D. He prevented me against making a mistake.
Correct Answer: B. He prevented me from making a mistake. Explanation: The pattern is prevent + object + from + gerund. This is a frequent structure in sentence-correction questions.
189. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The manager is angry on me. B. The manager is angry from me. C. The manager is angry to me. D. The manager is angry with me.
Correct Answer: D. The manager is angry with me. Explanation: Angry with someone is standard when identifying the person who caused the anger. Angry about something identifies the issue.
190. Choose the correct sentence.
A. He was prevented from entering the building. B. He was prevented to enter the building. C. He was prevented for entering the building. D. He was prevented against entering the building.
Correct Answer: A. He was prevented from entering the building. Explanation: In the passive voice, the construction remains be prevented from + gerund. Example: They were prevented from participating.
191. Fill in the blank: The manager congratulated her ___ winning the award.
A. for B. at C. on D. with
Correct Answer: C. on Explanation: Use congratulate someone on something. Example: They congratulated him on his promotion.
192. Fill in the blank: The committee is divided ___ the question of recruitment policy.
A. among B. over C. between D. beside
Correct Answer: B. over Explanation: Divided over an issue means having opposing opinions about it. Example: The members were divided over the proposed reform.
193. Fill in the blank: The officer was entrusted ___ the task of conducting the inquiry.
A. to B. for C. by D. with
Correct Answer: D. with Explanation: Entrust someone with a task means assign that person responsibility for it. Example: She was entrusted with managing the project.
194. Fill in the blank: He has a strong aversion ___ dishonesty.
A. to B. for C. against D. with
Correct Answer: A. to Explanation: Aversion to means a strong dislike of something. When followed by an action, the preposition takes a gerund: an aversion to lying.
195. Fill in the blank: The new regulations are in keeping ___ international standards.
A. to B. for C. with D. by
Correct Answer: C. with Explanation: In keeping with means consistent with or in agreement with. Example: The policy is in keeping with international best practices.
196. Fill in the blank: He was accused ___ negligence in the performance of his duties.
A. with B. of C. for D. by
Correct Answer: B. of Explanation: Accuse someone of something is the correct construction. Here, negligence names the alleged wrongdoing.
197. Fill in the blank: The decision is not consistent ___ the stated policy.
A. to B. for C. on D. with
Correct Answer: D. with Explanation: The standard expression is consistent with, meaning in agreement with or compatible with. Example: The evidence is consistent with the findings.
198. Fill in the blank: The company is on the verge ___ bankruptcy.
A. of B. for C. to D. with
Correct Answer: A. of Explanation: On the verge of means very close to a particular event or state. Example: The firm was on the verge of collapse.
199. Fill in the blank: The government acted in accordance ___ the court's order.
A. to B. of C. with D. for
Correct Answer: C. with Explanation: The fixed expression is in accordance with, meaning in compliance with or following something. Example: The action was taken in accordance with the law.
200. Fill in the blank: Notwithstanding the difficulties, the team succeeded ___ completing the project.
A. at B. in C. on D. for
Correct Answer: B. in Explanation: Succeed in doing something means achieve the desired result through an action. Notwithstanding means despite and does not require an additional preposition before the noun phrase that follows it.
