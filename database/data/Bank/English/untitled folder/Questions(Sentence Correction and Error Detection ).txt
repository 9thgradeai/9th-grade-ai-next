Section 1: Subject–Verb Agreement and Sentence Structure (1–25)
1. Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
Each of the candidates / have submitted / the application form / before the deadline.
A. Each of the candidates B. have submitted C. the application form D. before the deadline
Correct Answer: B. have submitted
Explanation: “Each” is singular and takes a singular verb. The correct phrase is “has submitted.”
2. Choose the grammatically correct sentence.
A. Neither the manager nor the employees was present. B. Neither the manager nor the employees were present. C. Neither the manager or the employees were present. D. Neither the manager nor the employees is present yesterday.
Correct Answer: B. Neither the manager nor the employees were present.
Explanation: With “neither…nor,” the verb generally agrees with the nearer subject. Since “employees” is plural, “were” is correct.
3. Identify the error in the sentence.
The quality of the products / manufactured by these factories / have improved / significantly.
A. The quality of the products B. manufactured by these factories C. have improved D. significantly
Correct Answer: C. have improved
Explanation: The subject is “quality,” which is singular. The plural noun “products” is part of a prepositional phrase and does not determine the verb. Use “has improved.”
4. Choose the correct sentence.
A. One of my friends live in Dhaka. B. One of my friends lives in Dhaka. C. One of my friend lives in Dhaka. D. One of my friends are living in Dhaka permanently.
Correct Answer: B. One of my friends lives in Dhaka.
Explanation: “One” is the subject and requires a singular verb. The phrase “of my friends” identifies the group from which one friend is selected.
5. Identify the error.
The number of applicants / applying for government jobs / are increasing / every year.
A. The number of applicants B. applying for government jobs C. are increasing D. every year
Correct Answer: C. are increasing
Explanation: “The number of” takes a singular verb, so the correct form is “is increasing.” In contrast, “a number of applicants” takes a plural verb.
6. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The committee have reached its decision. B. The committee has reached its decision. C. The committee are reach its decision. D. The committee were reached its decision.
Correct Answer: B. The committee has reached its decision.
Explanation: When a committee is treated as a single unit, it takes a singular verb. Collective nouns can sometimes take plural verbs when their members are emphasized individually, particularly in British English.
7. Find the erroneous part.
Neither of the two proposals / are acceptable / to the board / at present.
A. Neither of the two proposals B. are acceptable C. to the board D. at present
Correct Answer: B. are acceptable
Explanation: “Neither” is conventionally treated as singular in formal examination grammar. Therefore, “is acceptable” is the expected form.
8. Select the correct sentence.
A. Mathematics are my favourite subject. B. Mathematics is my favourite subject. C. Mathematics were my favourite subject. D. Mathematics have been my favourite subject.
Correct Answer: B. Mathematics is my favourite subject.
Explanation: The names of academic disciplines ending in “-s,” such as mathematics, economics, and physics, generally take singular verbs when referring to a field of study.
9. Identify the error.
The principal, along with the teachers, / were attending / the annual meeting / yesterday.
A. The principal, along with the teachers B. were attending C. the annual meeting D. yesterday
Correct Answer: B. were attending
Explanation: The main subject is “the principal.” The phrase “along with the teachers” does not change the subject's number. Use “was attending.”
10. Choose the correct sentence.
A. There is many reasons for the delay. B. There are many reasons for the delay. C. There has many reasons for the delay. D. There was many reasons for the delay.
Correct Answer: B. There are many reasons for the delay.
Explanation: In sentences beginning with “there,” the verb agrees with the actual subject. “Reasons” is plural, so “are” is correct.
11. Identify the error.
The list of selected candidates / have been published / on the notice board / this morning.
A. The list of selected candidates B. have been published C. on the notice board D. this morning
Correct Answer: B. have been published
Explanation: “The list” is the singular subject. The correct verb phrase is “has been published.”
12. Choose the correct sentence.
A. Every employee and every officer are required to attend. B. Every employee and every officer is required to attend. C. Every employee and every officer were required to attend tomorrow. D. Every employee and every officer have required to attend.
Correct Answer: B. Every employee and every officer is required to attend.
Explanation: When “every” precedes each member of a compound subject, the subject is generally treated as singular. Therefore, “is required” is appropriate.
13. Identify the error.
A pair of shoes / were lying / near the entrance / of the office.
A. A pair of shoes B. were lying C. near the entrance D. of the office
Correct Answer: B. were lying
Explanation: The grammatical subject is “a pair,” which is singular. Use “was lying.”
14. Select the correct sentence.
A. The news are encouraging. B. The news is encouraging. C. The news have been encouraging. D. The news were encouraging today.
Correct Answer: B. The news is encouraging.
Explanation: “News” is an uncountable singular noun despite ending in “-s.” It therefore takes a singular verb.
15. Identify the erroneous part.
More than one applicant / have complained / about the recruitment process / so far.
A. More than one applicant B. have complained C. about the recruitment process D. so far
Correct Answer: B. have complained
Explanation: “More than one” is normally followed by a singular noun and singular verb in formal English. The correct form is “has complained.”
16. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The police has arrested the suspect. B. The police have arrested the suspect. C. The police is arrested the suspect. D. The police was arresting the suspect yesterday and have finished.
Correct Answer: B. The police have arrested the suspect.
Explanation: “Police” generally takes a plural verb when referring to the police force or its members. The correct construction is “the police have arrested.”
17. Find the error.
What he needs / are a good job / and a stable income / to support his family.
A. What he needs B. are a good job C. and a stable income D. to support his family
Correct Answer: B. are a good job
Explanation: The subject is the noun clause “What he needs,” which is singular in this context. The expected verb is “is.”
18. Select the correct sentence.
A. Bread and butter are my usual breakfast. B. Bread and butter is my usual breakfast. C. Bread and butter have my usual breakfast. D. Bread and butter were my usual breakfast every day now.
Correct Answer: B. Bread and butter is my usual breakfast.
Explanation: When two nouns joined by “and” represent one combined item or a single idea, a singular verb may be used. Here, “bread and butter” refers to one customary breakfast.
19. Identify the error.
The captain, as well as his players, / were confident / of winning / the final match.
A. The captain, as well as his players B. were confident C. of winning D. the final match
Correct Answer: B. were confident
Explanation: “As well as” does not make the main subject plural. Since “captain” is singular, the correct verb is “was.”
20. Choose the correct sentence.
A. Neither the officers nor the manager have signed the document. B. Neither the officers nor the manager has signed the document. C. Neither the officers or the manager has signed the document. D. Neither the officers nor the manager are signing the document yesterday.
Correct Answer: B. Neither the officers nor the manager has signed the document.
Explanation: With “neither…nor,” the verb generally agrees with the nearer subject. Here, the nearer subject is the singular “manager,” so “has signed” is correct.
21. Identify the error.
A large number of workers / is demanding / better working conditions / from the company.
A. A large number of workers B. is demanding C. better working conditions D. from the company
Correct Answer: B. is demanding
Explanation: “A large number of” takes a plural verb when referring to many individual people or things. The correct form is “are demanding.”
22. Choose the correct sentence.
A. Ten kilometres are a long distance to walk. B. Ten kilometres is a long distance to walk. C. Ten kilometres have a long distance to walk. D. Ten kilometres were a long distance to walk tomorrow.
Correct Answer: B. Ten kilometres is a long distance to walk.
Explanation: A measurement of distance considered as a single unit generally takes a singular verb. The same principle applies to sums of money, periods of time, and weights treated as units.
23. Find the erroneous part.
The results of the investigation / indicates / that the company violated / several regulations.
A. The results of the investigation B. indicates C. that the company violated D. several regulations
Correct Answer: B. indicates
Explanation: The subject “results” is plural, so the correct verb is “indicate.”
24. Select the correct sentence.
A. Either of these two methods are suitable. B. Either of these two methods is suitable. C. Either of these two methods were suitable tomorrow. D. Either of these two methods have suitable.
Correct Answer: B. Either of these two methods is suitable.
Explanation: “Either” means one or the other and is conventionally singular in formal grammar questions. It therefore takes “is.”
25. Identify the error.
Not only the employees but also the manager / have expressed / concern about the new policy / recently.
A. Not only the employees but also the manager B. have expressed C. concern about the new policy D. recently
Correct Answer: B. have expressed
Explanation: With “not only…but also,” the verb generally agrees with the nearer subject. Since “manager” is singular, “has expressed” is correct.
Section 2: Tense, Verb Forms, and Conditional Sentences (26–50)
26. Identify the erroneous part.
By the time we reached the station, / the train already left / the platform / without us.
A. By the time we reached the station B. the train already left C. the platform D. without us
Correct Answer: B. the train already left
Explanation: The earlier of two past actions normally takes the past perfect. The correct form is “the train had already left.”
27. Choose the grammatically correct sentence.
A. If I knew about the meeting, I would have attended it. B. If I had known about the meeting, I would have attended it. C. If I would have known about the meeting, I attended it. D. If I had knew about the meeting, I would attend it.
Correct Answer: B. If I had known about the meeting, I would have attended it.
Explanation: This is a third conditional, expressing an unreal past situation. Its usual structure is “if + past perfect,” followed by “would have + past participle.”
28. Identify the error.
She has been working / in this bank / since five years / without a promotion.
A. She has been working B. in this bank C. since five years D. without a promotion
Correct Answer: C. since five years
Explanation: Use “for” with a duration and “since” with a starting point. The correct phrase is “for five years.”
29. Choose the correct sentence.
A. I am knowing the answer to this question. B. I have been knowing the answer to this question. C. I know the answer to this question. D. I was know the answer to this question.
Correct Answer: C. I know the answer to this question.
Explanation: “Know” is generally a stative verb and is not normally used in the continuous form to describe a state of knowledge. The simple present is appropriate.
30. Identify the error.
When the director will arrive, / the meeting / will begin / immediately.
A. When the director will arrive B. the meeting C. will begin D. immediately
Correct Answer: A. When the director will arrive
Explanation: In a future time clause introduced by “when,” the simple present is normally used. Say, “When the director arrives, the meeting will begin.”
31. Choose the correct sentence.
A. He has completed his degree last year. B. He completed his degree last year. C. He had completed his degree last year and is still completing it. D. He completes his degree last year.
Correct Answer: B. He completed his degree last year.
Explanation: The simple past is used with a definite, completed past time such as “last year.” The present perfect is generally incompatible with this time expression.
32. Identify the erroneous part.
I am living / in Rajshahi / since my childhood / with my family.
A. I am living B. in Rajshahi C. since my childhood D. with my family
Correct Answer: A. I am living
Explanation: For an action or situation that began in the past and continues now, the present perfect or present perfect continuous is appropriate. Use “I have been living in Rajshahi since my childhood.”
33. Choose the correct sentence.
A. No sooner had the bell rung when the students left. B. No sooner did the bell rung than the students left. C. No sooner had the bell rung than the students left. D. No sooner the bell had rung than the students left.
Correct Answer: C. No sooner had the bell rung than the students left.
Explanation: The standard construction is “no sooner…than.” When “no sooner” begins the sentence, subject–auxiliary inversion is required: “No sooner had…the bell rung than…”
34. Identify the error.
He said that he is busy / and could not attend / the meeting / that day.
A. He said that he is busy B. and could not attend C. the meeting D. that day
Correct Answer: A. He said that he is busy
Explanation: In traditional sequence-of-tenses exercises, a past reporting verb usually shifts the present tense to the past. The expected form is “He said that he was busy.” However, an unchanged tense can be acceptable when the statement remains true.
35. Select the correct sentence.
A. I wish I was taller so that I could reach the shelf. B. I wish I were taller so that I could reach the shelf. C. I wish I am taller so that I could reach the shelf. D. I wish I have been taller so that I could reach the shelf.
Correct Answer: B. I wish I were taller so that I could reach the shelf.
Explanation: “Were” is the traditional subjunctive form used for an unreal present situation. It expresses a wish contrary to the current facts.
36. Identify the error.
The committee / has been discussing the proposal / for three hours when the chairman arrived / yesterday.
A. The committee B. has been discussing the proposal C. for three hours when the chairman arrived D. yesterday
Correct Answer: B. has been discussing the proposal
Explanation: The discussion was already in progress before another past event occurred. The past perfect continuous is appropriate: “had been discussing.”
37. Choose the correct sentence.
A. If he studies hard, he would pass the examination. B. If he studied hard, he will pass the examination. C. If he studies hard, he will pass the examination. D. If he will study hard, he will pass the examination.
Correct Answer: C. If he studies hard, he will pass the examination.
Explanation: The first conditional describes a realistic future possibility. Its standard structure is “if + simple present,” followed by “will + base verb.”
38. Find the error.
I have finished / the report / before the manager arrived / yesterday.
A. I have finished B. the report C. before the manager arrived D. yesterday
Correct Answer: A. I have finished
Explanation: The sentence refers to completed past events and includes “yesterday.” Use “I had finished” to emphasize that the report was completed before the manager arrived.
39. Select the correct sentence.
A. Hardly had I reached home when it began to rain. B. Hardly I had reached home when it began to rain. C. Hardly had I reached home than it began to rain. D. Hardly did I reached home when it began to rain.
Correct Answer: A. Hardly had I reached home when it began to rain.
Explanation: The standard pattern is “hardly…when,” with inversion after initial “hardly.” The past perfect indicates the earlier action.
40. Identify the error.
The sun / rises in the east / and rose in the west / in the same season.
A. The sun B. rises in the east C. and rose in the west D. in the same season
Correct Answer: C. and rose in the west
Explanation: The statement describes a general scientific fact, so the simple present should be used consistently. The corrected phrase is “and sets in the west.”
41. Choose the correct sentence.
A. I would rather you leave now. B. I would rather you left now. C. I would rather you had leave now. D. I would rather you will leave now.
Correct Answer: B. I would rather you left now.
Explanation: “Would rather + subject” is commonly followed by a past-tense verb when expressing a preference about the present or future. The past tense expresses remoteness, not necessarily past time.
42. Identify the error.
Unless you do not submit the form, / your application / will not be considered / by the authority.
A. Unless you do not submit the form B. your application C. will not be considered D. by the authority
Correct Answer: A. Unless you do not submit the form
Explanation: “Unless” already means “if…not.” The expected form is “Unless you submit the form, your application will not be considered.”
43. Choose the correct sentence.
A. Had I known the truth, I would help you. B. Had I knew the truth, I would have helped you. C. Had I known the truth, I would have helped you. D. If I had knew the truth, I would have helped you.
Correct Answer: C. Had I known the truth, I would have helped you.
Explanation: “Had I known” is an inverted form of “If I had known.” For an unreal past condition, the result clause generally uses “would have + past participle.”
44. Identify the error.
It is high time / we take / effective measures / against corruption.
A. It is high time B. we take C. effective measures D. against corruption
Correct Answer: B. we take
Explanation: “It is high time” is conventionally followed by a subject and a past-tense verb when urging that something should already happen. The expected form is “we took.”
45. Select the correct sentence.
A. He is used to get up early. B. He is used to getting up early. C. He used to getting up early. D. He is use to getting up early.
Correct Answer: B. He is used to getting up early.
Explanation: In “be used to,” the word “to” is a preposition and takes a noun or gerund. Do not confuse it with “used to + base verb,” which describes a past habit.
46. Find the error.
By next December, / she will work / at the bank / for ten years.
A. By next December B. she will work C. at the bank D. for ten years
Correct Answer: B. she will work
Explanation: The future perfect continuous is appropriate for an activity continuing up to a future point. Use “she will have been working.”
47. Choose the correct sentence.
A. Scarcely had the match started when it began to rain. B. Scarcely had the match started than it began to rain. C. Scarcely the match had started when it began to rain. D. Scarcely did the match started when it began to rain.
Correct Answer: A. Scarcely had the match started when it began to rain.
Explanation: The usual pattern is “scarcely…when” or “scarcely…before.” Initial “scarcely” requires subject–auxiliary inversion.
48. Identify the error.
If I were you, / I will accept / the job offer / without hesitation.
A. If I were you B. I will accept C. the job offer D. without hesitation
Correct Answer: B. I will accept
Explanation: This is a second conditional expressing hypothetical advice. The result clause should use “would accept,” not “will accept.”
49. Choose the correct sentence.
A. She has gone to London last week. B. She went to London last week. C. She had went to London last week. D. She goes to London last week.
Correct Answer: B. She went to London last week.
Explanation: “Last week” indicates a finished period in the past, so the simple past is appropriate. The past participle of “go” is “gone,” but it does not fit this sentence's required tense.
50. Identify the error.
The moment / he will arrive / we shall inform / the director.
A. The moment B. he will arrive C. we shall inform D. the director
Correct Answer: B. he will arrive
Explanation: A future time clause beginning with “the moment” normally uses the simple present. The corrected clause is “he arrives.”
Section 3: Articles, Determiners, and Pronouns (51–70)
51. Identify the error.
He is / an honest officer / and a university graduate / of considerable experience.
A. He is B. an honest officer C. and a university graduate D. of considerable experience
Correct Answer: No error.
Explanation: Both articles are correct. “Honest” begins with a vowel sound, so “an” is appropriate; “university” begins with a /juː/ sound, so “a” is appropriate.
52. Choose the correct sentence.
A. He is the best of the two candidates. B. He is better of the two candidates. C. He is the better of the two candidates. D. He is best between the two candidates.
Correct Answer: C. He is the better of the two candidates.
Explanation: The comparative degree is generally used when comparing two people or things. “The better of the two” is the standard construction.
53. Identify the error.
Neither of the two sisters / has completed / their assignment / yet.
A. Neither of the two sisters B. has completed C. their assignment D. yet
Correct Answer: C. their assignment
Explanation: In traditional examination grammar, “neither” is singular, so the expected possessive is “her assignment” if the sisters are female. Singular “their” is widely accepted in modern English when gender is unspecified, but “her” is unambiguous here.
54. Choose the correct sentence.
A. He gave me an useful piece of advice. B. He gave me a useful piece of advice. C. He gave me a useful advice. D. He gave me an advice.
Correct Answer: B. He gave me a useful piece of advice.
Explanation: “Useful” begins with a /juː/ consonant sound, requiring “a.” Also, “advice” is uncountable, so use “a piece of advice” rather than “an advice.”
55. Identify the error.
The Ganges / is one of the longest river / in South Asia / and supports millions of people.
A. The Ganges B. is one of the longest river C. in South Asia D. and supports millions of people
Correct Answer: B. is one of the longest river
Explanation: The pattern “one of the + superlative + plural noun” requires a plural noun. The correction is “one of the longest rivers.”
56. Select the correct sentence.
A. Each student must bring their admit card. B. Each student must bring his or her admit card. C. Each students must bring his admit card. D. Each student must brings his or her admit card.
Correct Answer: B. Each student must bring his or her admit card.
Explanation: In traditional competitive-exam grammar, “each student” is singular and is paired with “his or her.” Modern English also commonly accepts singular “their,” as in option A.
57. Identify the error.
The officer asked me / that which document / I had submitted / to the department.
A. The officer asked me B. that which document C. I had submitted D. to the department
Correct Answer: B. that which document
Explanation: An indirect question normally does not use “that” before an interrogative determiner in this construction. Use “which document I had submitted.”
58. Choose the correct sentence.
A. This is the same book which I bought yesterday. B. This is the same book as I bought yesterday. C. This is same book that I bought yesterday. D. This is the same book than I bought yesterday.
Correct Answer: A. This is the same book which I bought yesterday.
Explanation: “The same book which I bought yesterday” is acceptable in ordinary English. “The same…as” is also used in comparisons of identity, such as “the same book as yours,” but it is not required here.
59. Identify the error.
The two brothers / resemble to each other / in appearance / and behaviour.
A. The two brothers B. resemble to each other C. in appearance D. and behaviour
Correct Answer: B. resemble to each other
Explanation: “Resemble” is a transitive verb and does not take “to” before its object. Say “resemble each other.”
60. Choose the correct sentence.
A. I have fewer money than my brother. B. I have less money than my brother. C. I have lesser money than my brother. D. I have few money than my brother.
Correct Answer: B. I have less money than my brother.
Explanation: “Money” is uncountable, so “less” is normally used. “Fewer” is used with countable plural nouns, such as “fewer coins.”
61. Identify the error.
There are less applicants / for this position / than the bank / expected.
A. There are less applicants B. for this position C. than the bank D. expected
Correct Answer: A. There are less applicants
Explanation: “Applicants” is a countable plural noun, so formal grammar prefers “fewer applicants.” The corrected sentence begins “There are fewer applicants.”
62. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The teacher explained me the rule. B. The teacher explained the rule to me. C. The teacher explained to me the rule it. D. The teacher explained about me the rule.
Correct Answer: B. The teacher explained the rule to me.
Explanation: “Explain” normally takes the thing explained as its direct object, followed by “to” before the recipient. Say “explain something to someone.”
63. Identify the error.
Between you and I, / the proposal / is unlikely / to be approved.
A. Between you and I B. the proposal C. is unlikely D. to be approved
Correct Answer: A. Between you and I
Explanation: A preposition takes an object pronoun. Therefore, the standard form is “between you and me.”
64. Choose the correct sentence.
A. Whom do you think is responsible? B. Who do you think is responsible? C. Whose do you think is responsible? D. Who do you think him responsible?
Correct Answer: B. Who do you think is responsible?
Explanation: “Who” is the subject of “is responsible,” even though the clause is embedded after “do you think.” Use “whom” when the pronoun functions as an object.
65. Identify the error.
The manager asked / my colleague and I / to prepare the report / before noon.
A. The manager asked B. my colleague and I C. to prepare the report D. before noon
Correct Answer: B. my colleague and I
Explanation: The pronouns are the object of “asked,” so objective case is required. The correction is “my colleague and me.”
66. Select the correct sentence.
A. Every one of the files have been checked. B. Every one of the files has been checked. C. Every one of the file have been checked. D. Every one of the files are checked yesterday.
Correct Answer: B. Every one of the files has been checked.
Explanation: “Every one” is singular and takes a singular verb. Do not confuse it with “everyone,” which also takes a singular verb but usually refers to people.
67. Identify the error.
This is the officer / which approved / the loan application / yesterday.
A. This is the officer B. which approved C. the loan application D. yesterday
Correct Answer: B. which approved
Explanation: “Who” is normally used for people, while “which” refers to things or animals. The corrected clause is “who approved.”
68. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The furniture in these rooms are expensive. B. The furniture in these rooms is expensive. C. The furnitures in these rooms are expensive. D. The furniture in these rooms have expensive.
Correct Answer: B. The furniture in these rooms is expensive.
Explanation: “Furniture” is an uncountable noun and takes a singular verb. Use “pieces of furniture” when counting individual items.
69. Identify the error.
He is senior than me / in the department / although we joined / at the same time.
A. He is senior than me B. in the department C. although we joined D. at the same time
Correct Answer: A. He is senior than me
Explanation: “Senior” takes “to,” not “than,” in standard English. The correct phrase is “senior to me.”
70. Choose the correct sentence.
A. Neither answer are correct. B. Neither answers is correct. C. Neither answer is correct. D. Neither of answer are correct.
Correct Answer: C. Neither answer is correct.
Explanation: “Neither” means not one nor the other and is conventionally singular in formal examination grammar. Hence, “neither answer is correct.”
Section 4: Prepositions, Conjunctions, and Common Expressions (71–90)
71. Identify the error.
He is good in mathematics / but weak in English / and general knowledge / according to his results.
A. He is good in mathematics B. but weak in English C. and general knowledge D. according to his results
Correct Answer: A. He is good in mathematics
Explanation: The conventional expression is “good at mathematics.” Use “weak in English” or “weak at English,” depending on the intended emphasis.
72. Choose the correct sentence.
A. She is married with a banker. B. She is married to a banker. C. She is married by a banker. D. She is married from a banker.
Correct Answer: B. She is married to a banker.
Explanation: The standard expression is “married to someone.” “Married by” identifies the person performing the ceremony, not the spouse.
73. Identify the error.
The candidate is capable / to solve / difficult mathematical problems / within seconds.
A. The candidate is capable B. to solve C. difficult mathematical problems D. within seconds
Correct Answer: B. to solve
Explanation: “Capable” is followed by “of” and a noun or gerund. The correct construction is “capable of solving.”
74. Choose the correct sentence.
A. He insisted to pay the bill. B. He insisted on paying the bill. C. He insisted for paying the bill. D. He insisted at paying the bill.
Correct Answer: B. He insisted on paying the bill.
Explanation: “Insist” is commonly followed by “on” plus a gerund when expressing determination to do something. Thus, “insisted on paying” is correct.
75. Identify the error.
The meeting was postponed / due to of / the absence of the chairman / and several directors.
A. The meeting was postponed B. due to of C. the absence of the chairman D. and several directors
Correct Answer: B. due to of
Explanation: “Due to” already expresses the reason and must not be followed by another “of.” The correct phrase is “due to the absence.”
76. Choose the correct sentence.
A. Despite of the rain, the match continued. B. Despite the rain, the match continued. C. In spite the rain, the match continued. D. Although of the rain, the match continued.
Correct Answer: B. Despite the rain, the match continued.
Explanation: “Despite” is followed directly by a noun or gerund. The alternative “in spite of the rain” is also correct.
77. Identify the error.
He congratulated me / for my success / in the competitive examination / last month.
A. He congratulated me B. for my success C. in the competitive examination D. last month
Correct Answer: B. for my success
Explanation: The usual construction is “congratulate someone on something.” Use “congratulated me on my success.”
78. Choose the correct sentence.
A. I prefer tea than coffee. B. I prefer tea over than coffee. C. I prefer tea to coffee. D. I prefer more tea than coffee.
Correct Answer: C. I prefer tea to coffee.
Explanation: In the standard construction “prefer X to Y,” the preposition “to” introduces the preferred alternative. “Prefer X over Y” is also acceptable in contemporary usage.
79. Identify the error.
The officer is responsible of / maintaining the records / and preparing monthly statements / for the department.
A. The officer is responsible of B. maintaining the records C. and preparing monthly statements D. for the department
Correct Answer: A. The officer is responsible of
Explanation: The standard expression is “responsible for.” The corrected phrase is “responsible for maintaining the records.”
80. Choose the correct sentence.
A. He has been absent since Monday. B. He has been absent for Monday. C. He has been absent from Monday ago. D. He is absent since Monday.
Correct Answer: A. He has been absent since Monday.
Explanation: “Since” identifies the starting point of a continuing situation. “For” introduces its duration, as in “for three days.”
81. Identify the error.
The new policy aims / at reduce / administrative costs / across all branches.

Correct Answer: B. Please return the documents by tomorrow.
Explanation: “Return” already conveys the idea of bringing or sending something back. The additional word “back” is unnecessary here.
160. Identify the error.
The new system is / capable to process / thousands of transactions / every minute.
A. The new system is B. capable to process C. thousands of transactions D. every minute
Correct Answer: B. capable to process
Explanation: “Capable” is followed by “of” and a gerund. Use “capable of processing.”
161. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The officer made a mistake unintentionally by accident. B. The officer made a mistake by accident. C. The officer made an intentional mistake by accident. D. The officer accidentally made an intentional mistake unintentionally.
Correct Answer: B. The officer made a mistake by accident.
Explanation: “By accident” already means unintentionally. Adding “unintentionally” is redundant.
162. Identify the error.
The company is facing / a serious scarcity of / qualified employees / in its regional offices.
A. The company is facing B. a serious scarcity of C. qualified employees D. in its regional offices
Correct Answer: B. a serious scarcity of
Explanation: “Scarcity” is grammatical, but “serious scarcity of” is less natural in this context than “shortage of.” For formal editing, “a serious shortage of qualified employees” is preferable.
163. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The manager gave me a valuable advice. B. The manager gave me valuable advice. C. The manager gave me many advice. D. The manager gave me an advice.
Correct Answer: B. The manager gave me valuable advice.
Explanation: “Advice” is uncountable in English. Use “some advice,” “a piece of advice,” or simply “valuable advice.”
164. Identify the error.
The report contains / many useful informations / about the financial condition / of the institution.
A. The report contains B. many useful informations C. about the financial condition D. of the institution
Correct Answer: B. many useful informations
Explanation: “Information” is uncountable and does not normally have a plural form. Use “much useful information” or “many useful details.”
165. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The new policy will benefit to all employees. B. The new policy will benefit all employees. C. The new policy will benefit for all employees. D. The new policy will beneficial all employees.
Correct Answer: B. The new policy will benefit all employees.
Explanation: “Benefit” functions as a transitive verb and takes a direct object. The adjective form is “beneficial,” as in “beneficial to all employees.”
166. Identify the error.
The manager asked / a question to me / regarding the missing document / during the interview.
A. The manager asked B. a question to me C. regarding the missing document D. during the interview
Correct Answer: B. a question to me
Explanation: The more natural construction is “asked me a question.” Alternatively, “put a question to me” is acceptable in formal English.
167. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The evidence is sufficient enough to prove the claim. B. The evidence is sufficient to prove the claim. C. The evidence is enough sufficient to prove the claim. D. The evidence is sufficiently enough to prove the claim.
Correct Answer: B. The evidence is sufficient to prove the claim.

168. Identify the error.
The chairman / emphasised on the need / for greater transparency / in the recruitment process.
A. The chairman B. emphasised on the need C. for greater transparency D. in the recruitment process
Correct Answer: B. emphasised on the need
Explanation: “Emphasise” normally takes a direct object. Use “emphasised the need,” not “emphasised on the need.”
169. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The new rules will come into effect from next month. B. The new rules will come into effect next month. C. The new rules will come in effect next month. D. The new rules will come to effect next month.
Correct Answer: B. The new rules will come into effect next month.
Explanation: “Come into effect” is a standard expression meaning to become operative. In this sentence, “from” is unnecessary before “next month.”
170. Identify the error.
The officer / returned back to his office / after attending the meeting / at headquarters.
A. The officer B. returned back to his office C. after attending the meeting D. at headquarters
Correct Answer: B. returned back to his office

171. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The committee reached to a conclusion after discussion. B. The committee reached a conclusion after discussion. C. The committee reached at a conclusion after discussion. D. The committee reached into a conclusion after discussion.
Correct Answer: B. The committee reached a conclusion after discussion.

172. Identify the error.
The organisation aims / to provide better services / and improving customer satisfaction / through digital innovation.
A. The organisation aims B. to provide better services C. and improving customer satisfaction D. through digital innovation
Correct Answer: C. and improving customer satisfaction

173. Choose the correct sentence.
A. He has a strong command over English. B. He has a strong command of English. C. He has a strong command on English. D. He has a strong command in English language.
Correct Answer: B. He has a strong command of English.

174. Identify the error.
The bank needs / to cope up with / the growing demand / for digital transactions.
A. The bank needs B. to cope up with C. the growing demand D. for digital transactions
Correct Answer: B. to cope up with
Explanation: The correct expression is “cope with,” not “cope up with.” It means to manage or deal successfully with a difficult situation.
175. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The candidate is confident to pass the examination. B. The candidate is confident of passing the examination. C. The candidate is confident for passing the examination. D. The candidate is confident on passing the examination.
Correct Answer: B. The candidate is confident of passing the examination.
Explanation: The conventional pattern is “confident of + noun/gerund” or “confident that + clause.” For example, “She is confident that she will pass.”
Section 9: Mixed Error Detection and Sentence Improvement (176–200)
176. Identify the part containing an error.
Neither the evidence nor the witness statements / was sufficient / to establish the accused's guilt / beyond reasonable doubt.
A. Neither the evidence nor the witness statements B. was sufficient C. to establish the accused's guilt D. beyond reasonable doubt
Correct Answer: B. was sufficient
Explanation: With “neither…nor,” the verb generally agrees with the nearer subject. Since “witness statements” is plural, use “were sufficient.”
177. Choose the best correction.
The manager is one of those officers who always completes their assignments on time.
A. who always completes their assignments B. who always complete their assignments C. which always completes their assignments D. who always has completed their assignments yesterday
Correct Answer: B. who always complete their assignments
Explanation: The relative pronoun “who” refers to “officers,” not “manager.” Since “officers” is plural, the verb should be “complete.”
178. Identify the error.
Had the authorities acted promptly, / the damage would be avoided / and the situation / would not have deteriorated.
A. Had the authorities acted promptly B. the damage would be avoided C. and the situation D. would not have deteriorated
Correct Answer: B. the damage would be avoided
Explanation: The sentence describes an unreal past condition. The result should use “would have + past participle,” giving “the damage would have been avoided.”
179. Choose the grammatically correct sentence.
A. Each of the proposals have certain advantages. B. Each of the proposals has certain advantages. C. Each of the proposal has certain advantages. D. Each proposals have certain advantages.
Correct Answer: B. Each of the proposals has certain advantages.
Explanation: “Each” is singular and takes “has.” The plural noun “proposals” is correct after “of the.”
180. Identify the error.
The manager asked / that why the payment / had not been processed / within the stipulated period.
A. The manager asked B. that why the payment C. had not been processed D. within the stipulated period
Correct Answer: B. that why the payment
Explanation: An indirect question introduced by “why” does not require “that” in this construction. Use “The manager asked why the payment had not been processed.”
181. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The number of people attending the seminar have increased considerably. B. The number of people attending the seminar has increased considerably. C. A number of people attending the seminar has increased considerably. D. The number of people attending the seminar are increasing considerably.
Correct Answer: B. The number of people attending the seminar has increased considerably.
Explanation: “The number of” takes a singular verb. By contrast, “a number of people” takes a plural verb.
182. Identify the error.
No sooner had the interview begun / when the electricity failed / and all the computers / shut down.
A. No sooner had the interview begun B. when the electricity failed C. and all the computers D. shut down
Correct Answer: B. when the electricity failed
Explanation: The conventional correlative expression is “no sooner…than.” The corrected clause is “than the electricity failed.”
183. Choose the best correction.
The officer, along with two assistants, have been transferred to another branch.
A. have been transferred B. has been transferred C. were transferred D. are being transferred yesterday
Correct Answer: B. has been transferred
Explanation: The main subject is “the officer.” The phrase “along with two assistants” does not change its grammatical number.
184. Identify the error.
The new regulations / are intended to ensure that every applicant / submit the required documents / before the deadline.
A. The new regulations B. are intended to ensure that every applicant C. submit the required documents D. before the deadline
Correct Answer: C. submit the required documents
Explanation: In traditional formal grammar, “every applicant” is singular, so the indicative form is “submits.” However, after “ensure that,” a mandative subjunctive (“every applicant submit”) is also grammatical in formal English. Therefore, this item has no uniquely defensible error under all accepted grammatical standards.
185. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The information provided by the applicants were insufficient. B. The information provided by the applicants was insufficient. C. The informations provided by the applicants were insufficient. D. The information provided by the applicants have been insufficient yesterday.
Correct Answer: B. The information provided by the applicants was insufficient.
Explanation: “Information” is an uncountable singular noun. It takes “was,” not “were,” in this sentence.
186. Identify the error.
Having been informed about the change, / the necessary arrangements / were made by the staff / before the guests arrived.
A. Having been informed about the change B. the necessary arrangements C. were made by the staff D. before the guests arrived
Correct Answer: A. Having been informed about the change
Explanation: The introductory participial phrase should logically describe the staff, not the arrangements. Write “Having been informed about the change, the staff made the necessary arrangements.”
187. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The report comprises of five chapters. B. The report comprises five chapters. C. The report is comprised five chapters. D. The report comprises with five chapters.
Correct Answer: B. The report comprises five chapters.
Explanation: “Comprise” means to consist of or include and normally takes a direct object. In traditional examination grammar, “comprises of” is treated as incorrect.
188. Identify the error.
The candidate was confident / that he would succeed / because he had prepared / himself good for the examination.
A. The candidate was confident B. that he would succeed C. because he had prepared D. himself good for the examination
Correct Answer: D. himself good for the examination
Explanation: The verb “prepared” requires the adverb “well” to describe how the candidate prepared. The correct expression is “had prepared himself well.”
189. Choose the correct sentence.
A. If the bank had invested earlier, it would earn higher returns last year. B. If the bank invested earlier, it would have earned higher returns last year. C. If the bank had invested earlier, it would have earned higher returns last year. D. If the bank would invest earlier, it would have earned higher returns last year.
Correct Answer: C. If the bank had invested earlier, it would have earned higher returns last year.
Explanation: The third conditional describes a hypothetical past condition and its unreal past result. Its standard structure is “if + past perfect,” followed by “would have + past participle.”
190. Identify the error.
The manager recommended / that the proposal is reviewed / by the legal department / before approval.
A. The manager recommended B. that the proposal is reviewed C. by the legal department D. before approval
Correct Answer: B. that the proposal is reviewed
Explanation: In traditional formal grammar, a recommendation may take the mandative subjunctive: “that the proposal be reviewed.” This construction expresses a requirement or recommendation rather than a factual statement.
191. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The more carefully you examine the contract, the fewer errors you will find. B. The more carefully you examine the contract, the less errors you will find. C. The most carefully you examine the contract, the fewer errors you will find. D. More carefully you examine the contract, fewer errors you will find.
Correct Answer: A. The more carefully you examine the contract, the fewer errors you will find.
Explanation: “The more…the fewer” is a correct correlative comparative construction. “Fewer” is used with countable plural nouns such as “errors.”
192. Identify the error.
Despite of facing severe financial difficulties, / the company managed / to continue its operations / throughout the year.
A. Despite of facing severe financial difficulties B. the company managed C. to continue its operations D. throughout the year
Correct Answer: A. Despite of facing severe financial difficulties
Explanation: Use “despite facing” or “in spite of facing.” “Despite of” incorrectly combines two separate constructions.
193. Choose the best correction.
The reason he could not attend the meeting was because he was ill.
A. The reason he could not attend the meeting was because of he was ill. B. The reason he could not attend the meeting was that he was ill. C. The reason why he could not attend the meeting because he was ill. D. The reason he could not attend the meeting was due he was ill.
Correct Answer: B. The reason he could not attend the meeting was that he was ill.
Explanation: “The reason was that” is the preferred formal construction. It avoids the commonly criticized redundancy in “the reason was because.”
194. Identify the error.
Neither the chairman nor the directors / has agreed / to the revised budget / proposed by the finance department.
A. Neither the chairman nor the directors B. has agreed C. to the revised budget D. proposed by the finance department
Correct Answer: B. has agreed
Explanation: In the usual proximity rule for “neither…nor,” the verb agrees with the nearer subject. Since “directors” is plural, use “have agreed.”
195. Choose the correct sentence.
A. The employee was asked why had he submitted the report late. B. The employee was asked why he had submitted the report late. C. The employee was asked why did he submit the report late. D. The employee was asked why he has submitted the report late yesterday.
Correct Answer: B. The employee was asked why he had submitted the report late.
Explanation: Indirect questions follow statement word order, so the subject precedes the auxiliary verb. The past perfect is appropriate when reporting a submission that occurred before the questioning.
196. Identify the error.
The proposal, together with the supporting documents, / have been forwarded / to the head office / for final approval.
A. The proposal, together with the supporting documents B. have been forwarded C. to the head office D. for final approval
Correct Answer: B. have been forwarded
Explanation: The main subject is “the proposal,” which is singular. The intervening phrase “together with the supporting documents” does not make it plural. Use “has been forwarded.”
197. Choose the best correction.
The officer is not only responsible for preparing the report but also to present it.
A. but also to present it B. but also presenting it C. but also responsible for presenting it D. but also he presents it
Correct Answer: C. but also responsible for presenting it
Explanation: Parallel structure requires the elements following “not only…but also” to match. “Responsible for preparing” pairs naturally with “responsible for presenting.”
198. Identify the error.
The data collected during the survey / indicates that customer satisfaction / has improved significantly / over the past year.
A. The data collected during the survey B. indicates that customer satisfaction C. has improved significantly D. over the past year
Correct Answer: B. indicates that customer satisfaction
Explanation: In traditional academic usage, “data” is the plural of “datum” and takes “indicate.” However, “data” is also widely treated as a singular mass noun in contemporary English. For a traditional competitive-exam question, “indicate” is the expected answer.
199. Choose the grammatically correct sentence.
A. Not until the audit was completed the discrepancies were discovered. B. Not until the audit had completed were the discrepancies discovered. C. Not until the audit was completed were the discrepancies discovered. D. Not until was the audit completed the discrepancies were discovered.
Correct Answer: C. Not until the audit was completed were the discrepancies discovered.
Explanation: When “not until” begins a sentence, the main clause requires subject–auxiliary inversion. The correct structure is “were the discrepancies discovered.”
200. Choose the best correction.
Although the bank introduced several reforms, but operational inefficiencies continued to persist.
A. Although the bank introduced several reforms, but operational inefficiencies continued to persist. B. Although the bank introduced several reforms, operational inefficiencies continued. C. Despite the bank introduced several reforms, operational inefficiencies continued. D. Although introducing several reforms, but operational inefficiencies continued.
Correct Answer: B. Although the bank introduced several reforms, operational inefficiencies continued.
Explanation: “Although” and “but” should not normally be used together to connect the same clauses. “Continued to persist” is also unnecessarily repetitive because “persist” already means to continue.
